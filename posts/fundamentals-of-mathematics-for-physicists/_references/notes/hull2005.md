# Conventions

- Signature: not stated explicitly anywhere; Lagrangians are written with Hodge duals, e.g. L_D = R̂ ∗ 1 (4.2), with kinetic terms −½ ∗dφ ∧ dφ, so mostly-plus Lorentzian is implied but never declared.
- Index conventions: D = total dimension, d = dimension of the internal space X; coordinates {x^μ, y^i}; internal coordinate indices i, j, k; frame (Lie-algebra) indices m, n, p, q, ...; the vielbein is σ_i^m(y) with inverse σ^i_m(y). Gauge-group index A = 1..2d for O(d,d).
- Actions reduced: (i) pure Einstein–Hilbert L_D = R̂ ∗ 1 (4.2), p. PDF 19; (ii) a p-form gauge field with L_Ĝ = −½ ∗Ĝ_{(p+1)} ∧ Ĝ_{(p+1)} (4.18), p. PDF 21; (iii) the string-frame NS-NS sector L_S = e^{−Φ̂}(R̂ ∗ 1 + ∗dΦ̂ ∧ dΦ̂ − ½ ∗Ĝ_{(3)} ∧ Ĝ_{(3)}) (5.1), p. PDF 22, with Ĝ = dB̂.
- Left-invariant forms and Maurer–Cartan equation, exactly as printed (eqs (1.3), (2.3), (4.1)):
  $$ d\sigma^m + \tfrac{1}{2} f^m_{np}\, \sigma^n \wedge \sigma^p = 0 , $$
  i.e. dσ^m = −½ f^m_{np} σ^n ∧ σ^p, with the group index up and the first index of f the "output" index. With σ^m t_m = g^{-1} dg (left-invariant) for g ∈ G. Right-invariant forms σ̃^m t_m = dg g^{-1} satisfy dσ̃^m − ½ f^m_{np} σ̃^n ∧ σ̃^p = 0 (2.8). Left-invariant vector fields Z_m = σ^i_m ∂_i (2.6) obey [Z_m, Z_n] = f^p_{mn} Z_p (2.7); they generate the right action G_R, and the left-invariant forms σ^m are invariant under G_L (p. PDF 8).
- Cartan–Killing metric: η_{mn} = ½ f^q_{mp} f^p_{nq} (sec. 2.2, and (6.13)).
- Metric ansatz on the twisted torus (Einstein frame), eq. (2.10) = (4.3):
  $$ ds_D^2 = e^{2\alpha\varphi} g_{\mu\nu} dx^\mu dx^\nu + e^{2\beta\varphi} g_{mn}\, \nu^m \nu^n , \qquad \nu^m = \sigma^m - A^m \ (2.11)=(4.4), $$
  with g_{mn}(x) positive definite of unit determinant, φ(x) the breathing scalar, A^m = A^m_μ dx^μ the d Kaluza–Klein vectors, and α = −(D−d)^{1/2}/(2(d−2)(D−2))^{1/2}, β = ((d−2)/(2(D−d)(D−2)))^{1/2} (4.8). String frame: ds_D^2 = g_{μν} dx^μ dx^ν + g_{mn} ν^m ν^n (5.12).
- KK field strength F^m = dA^m + ½ f^m_{np} A^n ∧ A^p (4.5); covariant derivative Dg_{mn} = dg_{mn} + g_{mp} f^p_{nq} A^q + g_{np} f^p_{mq} A^q (4.9).

# Equations the tutorial uses

(a) Scherk–Schwarz ansatz and the definition of the twist (sec. 1, PDF 2; sec. 2.1, PDF 7).
- Internal metric: $g_{ij}(x,y) = \phi_{mn}(x)\, \sigma_i^m(y)\, \sigma_j^n(y)$ (1.1); general tensor: $T_{ij\ldots k}(x,y) = T_{mn\ldots p}(x)\, \sigma_i^m \sigma_j^n \ldots \sigma_k^p$ (2.2), with $\sigma^m = \sigma_i^m(y) dy^i$ (2.1).
- Twist coefficients: $f^m_{np} = -\sigma^i_n \sigma^j_p (\partial_i \sigma_j^m - \partial_j \sigma_i^m)$ (1.2); the y-dependence drops out of the reduced theory iff these are constant. The paper's words: the toroidal ansatz g_{ij} = φ_{ij}(x) is "twisted" by the matrices σ_i^m(y), hence "twisted torus" (PDF 2).
- Duality-twist version (sec. 3.1, PDF 11): $\psi(x^\mu, y) = \gamma_y[\psi(x^\mu)]$ (3.1), $\gamma(y) = \exp(My)$ (3.2), monodromy $\mathcal{M}(\gamma) = \gamma(0)\gamma(1)^{-1} = e^M$; torus moduli $H(\tau(y))_{ab} = (\gamma(y)^t)_a{}^c H(\tau_0)_{cd} \gamma(y)^d{}_a$ (3.6) [index placement as printed]. Lift to a (d+1)-dim metric $ds^2_{d+1} = dy^2 + H(\tau(y))_{ab} dz^a dz^b = (\sigma^y)^2 + H(\tau_0)_{ab}\sigma^a\sigma^b$ (3.7) with $\sigma^y = dy,\ \sigma(y)^a = \gamma(y)^a{}_b dz^b$ (3.8), obeying $d\sigma^a + M^a{}_b \sigma^y \wedge \sigma^b = 0$ (3.9), i.e. Lie algebra $[t_a, t_y] = M_a{}^b t_b,\ [t_a, t_b] = 0$ (3.10), group element $g = \begin{pmatrix} \gamma^{-1}(y) & z \\ 0 & 1 \end{pmatrix}$ (3.12), $g^{-1}dg = \begin{pmatrix} -M^a{}_b \sigma^y & \sigma^a \\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix} = \sigma^m t_m$ (3.13).

(b) Maurer–Cartan equations and the Heisenberg nilmanifold example (sec. 3.3.2, PDF 15–16).
- $d\sigma^m + \tfrac12 f^m_{np}\sigma^n\wedge\sigma^p = 0$ (2.3); Jacobi $f^q_{[mn} f^t_{p]q} = 0$ (2.4).
- Parabolic SL(2,Z) twist $\mathcal{M}_p = \begin{pmatrix}1 & m\\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ (3.32), $\gamma_p(y) = \begin{pmatrix}1 & my \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix},\ \tau(y) = \tau_0 + my$ (3.33), mass matrix $M_p = \begin{pmatrix} 0 & m \\ 0 & 0\end{pmatrix}$ (3.34).
- Heisenberg algebra: $[t_2, t_y] = m t_1,\ [t_1, t_y] = 0,\ [t_1, t_2] = 0$ (3.35), i.e. the only non-zero structure constant is $f^1_{2y} = -f^1_{y2} = m$ (this index reading is mine; the paper gives the brackets only).
- Group element $g(y,z^a) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -my & z^1 \\ 0 & 1 & z^2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ (3.36).
- Identifications: $(y, z^1, z^2) \sim (y + \alpha,\ z^1 - m\alpha z^2 + \beta^1,\ z^2 + \beta^2)$, $\alpha, \beta^a \in \mathbb{Z}$ (3.37), realised as the left quotient $g \sim h\cdot g$ with $h(\alpha,\beta^a) = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & -m\alpha & \beta^1 \\ 0 & 1 & \beta^2 \\ 0 & 0 & 1\end{pmatrix} \in \Gamma$ (3.38). Requires $m \in \mathbb{Z}$. Metric: (3.23) with τ(y) from (3.33), A = R = 1.
- General geometric twist: identifications $y \sim y + \alpha,\ z^a \sim (\mathcal{M}^{-\alpha})^a{}_b z^b + \beta^a$ (3.18), from $h(\alpha,\beta^a) = \begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{M}^{-\alpha} & \beta \\ 0 & 1\end{pmatrix}$ (3.17), $\mathcal{M} \in SL(d,\mathbb{Z})$.
- Elliptic (ISO(2)) and hyperbolic (ISO(1,1)) twists: (3.39)–(3.45) and (3.46)–(3.49); ISO(2) group manifold S^1 × R^2 compactified by (3.45) with β^a ∈ Z.

(c) Compactness / cocompactness (sec. 1 PDF 3–4, sec. 2.3 PDF 10, sec. 5 PDF 22).
- X = G/Γ with Γ ⊂ G_L a freely acting discrete subgroup; Γ must be in G_L because only the left action preserves σ^m (PDF 4). "Such a Γ is said to be cocompact, and not all groups have cocompact discrete subgroups" (PDF 4). "Importantly, not every group G has a cocompact discrete subgroup Γ that gives a compact space G/Γ" (sec. 2.3, PDF 10).
- Necessary condition: unimodularity, $f^m_{nm} = 0$ (4.6), which is also the condition for the volume element √g to be G_L-invariant, hence for a consistent reduction of the action (sec. 4.1, PDF 19; restated sec. 5, PDF 22). For the algebra (3.10) this is $M_i{}^i = 0$ (traceless mass matrix).
- No general sufficient criterion is given in the paper; compactness is checked case by case (nilmanifold, ISO(2), ISO(1,1)).

(d) Reduced theory (sec. 4.1, 5.1, 5.2).
- Gauge group of pure-gravity reduction: G_R (the isometries generated by the Z_m), with ansatz invariant under rigid G_L and local G_R (PDF 9). Fields: g_{μν}, d vectors A^m, d(d+1)/2 scalars φ and g_{mn}.
- Reduced Einstein-frame gravity Lagrangian (4.7), PDF 20:
  $$ \mathcal{L}_D = R\ast 1 - \tfrac12 \ast d\varphi\wedge d\varphi - \tfrac12 g^{mp} g^{nq} \ast Dg_{mn}\wedge Dg_{pq} - \tfrac12 e^{2(\beta-\alpha)\varphi} g_{mn} \ast F^m \wedge F^n - \tfrac12 e^{2(\beta-\alpha)\varphi}\left( g_{mn} g^{pq} g^{ts} f^m_{pt} f^n_{qs} + 2 g^{mn} f^p_{qm} f^q_{pn}\right)\ast 1 $$
  so the scalar potential (as a term in L, overall sign as printed) is $-\tfrac12 e^{2(\beta-\alpha)\varphi}( g_{mn} g^{pq} g^{ts} f^m_{pt} f^n_{qs} + 2 g^{mn} f^p_{qm} f^q_{pn})$, quadratic in f and built from the moduli g_{mn} only.
- String-frame NS-NS reduction with H-flux (5.13)–(5.14), PDF 24: $\mathcal{L}_D = e^{-\phi}(R\ast1 + \ast d\phi\wedge d\phi + \tfrac12 \ast Dg^{mn}\wedge Dg_{mn} - \tfrac12 g_{mn}\ast F^m\wedge F^n - \tfrac12 \ast G_{(3)}\wedge G_{(3)} - \tfrac12 g^{mn}\ast G_{(2)m}\wedge G_{(2)n} - \tfrac12 g^{mn}g^{pq}\ast G_{(1)mp}\wedge G_{(1)nq} + V\ast 1)$ with
  $$ V = -\tfrac14 g_{mn} g^{pq} g^{st} f^m_{ps} f^n_{qt} - \tfrac12 g^{mn} f^p_{mq} f^q_{np} - \tfrac12 g^{mn} g^{pq} g^{st} G_{(0)mps} G_{(0)pqt} \quad (5.14) $$
  [transcribed as printed; the last factor is probably meant to be G_{(0)nqt}], $G_{(0)mnp} = 3 B_{(0)[m|q} f^q_{np]} + K_{mnp}$ (5.9), shifted dilaton $\phi = \hat\Phi - \ln\sqrt{g}$ (5.15).
- Flux: $K = \tfrac16 K_{mnp}\sigma^m\wedge\sigma^n\wedge\sigma^p$ (1.5)=(5.4), closure $K_{t[mn} f^t_{pq]} = 0$ (5.5); general p-form version $K_{[m_1\ldots m_p|n} f^n_{|m_{p+1} m_{p+2}]} = 0$ (4.11).
- Gauge algebra with H-flux (5.22), PDF 25 (Kaloper–Myers algebra):
  $$ [Z_m, Z_n] = f^p_{mn} Z_p - K_{mnp} X^p, \qquad [X^m, Z_n] = -f^m_{np} X^p, \qquad [X^m, X^n] = 0, $$
  Jacobi ⇔ (2.4) and (5.5). Gauge group G_{2d}, 2d-dimensional; for K = 0, G_{2d} ≃ G_d ⋉ U(1)^d. O(d,d) form: $[T_A, T_B] = t_{AB}{}^C T_C$ (5.23), $T_A = (Z_m, X^m)$ (5.24), $t_{np}{}^m = f^m_{np}$, $t_{mnp} = K_{mnp}$ (PDF 26). Field-dependent algebra of the actual transformations: (5.21), (5.38).
- Mass terms: from $(D\mathcal{M})^2$ with $D\mathcal{M}_n{}^m = g^{mp} f^q_{np} B_{(1)q} + g^{mp} K_{npq} A^q + \ldots$ (5.32): masses for B_{(1)m} and A^m. Explicit mass terms: $\mathcal{L}_D = \tfrac14 e^{-\phi}\mathcal{M}^{AB}\mathcal{M}^{CD} t_{AC}{}^{E'} t_{BD}{}^{F'} \ast \mathcal{A}_{E'}\wedge\mathcal{A}_{F'} + \ldots$ (6.39); pure-twist case $-\tfrac12 e^{-\phi} g^{mn} g^{pq} f^{t'}_{mp} f^{s'}_{nq} \ast B_{(1)t'}\wedge B_{(1)s'}$ (6.52); pure-flux case $-\tfrac12 e^{-\phi} g^{mn} g^{pq} K_{mpt'} K_{nqs'} \ast A^{t'}\wedge A^{s'}$ (6.47). Masses of the duality-twist reduction are "given by the matrix M" (sec. 3.1, PDF 11).
- Goldstone/Stueckelberg: $\chi_p = \tfrac12 f_p{}^{mn} B_{(0)mn}$ (6.3)/(6.26), eaten by B_{(1)p}.

(e) Duality chain. The paper does NOT use the Q, R notation (that is Shelton–Taylor–Wecht, hep-th/0508133, later in 2005). What it states (sec. 7, PDF 39): "The transformation corresponding to T-duality in one torus direction removes the flux and turns on a twist, giving some structure constants f^m_{np} and a twisted torus [26, 46]. However, a further T-duality would then take the background to a non-geometric background [32]". So the paper supplies H_{abc} → f_{ab}^c (geometric) and the statement that the next step is non-geometric (ref. [32] = Hull, hep-th/0406102). Also: the O(d,d) action rotates (f, K) into each other as the tensor t_{AB}^C (PDF 27, 39); "the flux and twist then transform into each other and fit together into an irreducible representation".

(f) Relation to toroidal and group-manifold reductions.
- Toroidal: "In the abelian limit in which the structure constants t_{AB}^C are set to zero, this reduces to the standard reduction on a d-torus, giving a field theory with manifest global O(d,d) symmetry. The non-abelian reduction ... can be thought of as a gauging of a 2d-dimensional subgroup G_{2d} of the O(d,d) symmetry" (sec. 5.3, PDF 28). f = 0 ⇒ G_d = U(1)^d, algebra $[Z_m, Z_n] = -K_{mnp}X^p$ (6.44), sec. 6.5.1.
- Group manifold: conventional KK on a compact group manifold with bi-invariant metric ds^2 = η_{mn}σ^mσ^n (2.5) gauges G_L × G_R but has no consistent truncation (except for the string-type metric+2-form+dilaton systems, [5,6]); the SS ansatz keeps only G_L-singlets and gauges G_R, and this truncation is consistent (sec. 2.2, PDF 8–9). The SS reduction on a compact G is therefore a truncation of the group-manifold reduction; on non-compact G it is a reduction on G/Γ.

# Attributable statements

1. Scherk–Schwarz's "twisted torus" is locally a group manifold G, but globally the internal space is X = G/Γ with Γ a cocompact discrete subgroup of G_L; "in general G/Γ will not be a group manifold" (sec. 2.1, PDF 7–8; sec. 1, PDF 3–4).
2. Consistency of the truncation: the SS ansatz is invariant under rigid G_L and local G_R, and the G_L-invariance "guarantees consistency" [3]; the full G_L × G_R group-manifold ansatz is not consistent in general (sec. 2.2, PDF 8–9; sec. 1, PDF 4).
3. Non-compact G: the gauge group G_R is non-compact and "necessarily non-linearly realised and so is broken to a linearly realised compact subgroup"; the vacuum value of g_{mn} breaks G_R to the subgroup preserving it (sec. 1, PDF 5; sec. 2.3, PDF 10).
4. A geometric duality twist by M ∈ SL(d,Z) is a reduction on a T^d bundle over S^1 that is the twisted torus G/Γ of the non-semi-simple algebra (3.10) (sec. 3.1, PDF 11–13).
5. The parabolic SL(2,Z) twist with m ∈ Z is the Heisenberg nilmanifold with identifications (3.37); elliptic twists give ISO(2) with gauge group broken to SO(2); hyperbolic twists give ISO(1,1) (sec. 3.3, PDF 15–18).
6. Unimodularity f^m_{nm} = 0 is necessary for reducing the action (and for a cocompact Γ); non-unimodular groups allow a consistent reduction of the field equations only (sec. 4.1, PDF 19; sec. 5, PDF 22).
7. With H-flux K_{mnp} the gauge algebra is (5.22), 2d-dimensional, with (Z_m, X^m) forming an O(d,d) vector and (f, K) the components of the O(d,d) tensor t_{AB}^C; the theory is a gauging of G_{2d} ⊂ O(d,d) (sec. 5.2–5.3, PDF 25–28).
8. For compact G with flux from the Killing metric, field redefinitions bring the gauge group to G_d × U(1)^d, spontaneously broken to G_d [6]; for non-compact/non-semi-simple G such redefinitions are not possible in general (sec. 1, PDF 6; sec. 6.3, PDF 30–33).
9. One T-duality maps H-flux to a geometric twist f; a second leads to a non-geometric background; O(d,d) acts on the couplings t_{AB}^C, so it is a duality not a symmetry, and does not commute with the gauge symmetry (sec. 7, PDF 39–40).

# Caveats

- Global vs local: all the physics of the truncated theory depends only on the local structure (Lie algebra); the choice of Γ is invisible to the lower-dimensional supergravity but fixes the KK spectrum and whether a mass gap exists. Different cocompact Γ, Γ' give different compactifications with the same effective theory (sec. 2.3, PDF 10).
- Compactness is not automatic: only groups with a cocompact lattice yield compactifications; the paper gives unimodularity (4.6) as a necessary condition and gives no sufficient criterion (for nilpotent groups the Malcev rational-structure-constant criterion is not mentioned). For the duality-twist algebra (3.10) one needs M ∈ SL(d,Z) up to conjugation, which quantises the mass parameters (e.g. m ∈ Z for the parabolic class, θ = mπ/2, 2π/3, π/3 for elliptic classes; sec. 3.3.1).
- Jacobi identities: f must satisfy (2.4) and the flux must satisfy (5.5)/(4.11) (closure of K); together they are the Jacobi identities of the 2d-dimensional gauge algebra (5.22).
- Sign/placement conventions: f^m_{np} with the upper index first; dσ^m = −½ f^m_{np} σ^n σ^p for left-invariant forms (right-invariant forms have the opposite sign, (2.8)). Some index placements in sec. 3 look typographically inconsistent (e.g. (3.6) has γ(y)^d{}_a on the right factor); (5.14) prints G_{(0)pqt} in the last term where nqt is expected. The signature is never stated.
- Non-unimodular groups: the reduced field equations are consistent but do not come from a y-independent action (ref. [39]); the paper restricts its action formulas (4.7), (5.13) to unimodular G.
- Non-compact gauge groups must be spontaneously broken; there is never a vacuum preserving all of G_R unless G admits a positive-definite invariant metric (compact, or special non-semi-simple cases, ref. [34]) (sec. 2.3).
- The Q/R non-geometric fluxes and the full "duality chain" are not in this paper; only the H → f step and the statement that a further T-duality is non-geometric (ref. [32]) appear (sec. 7).
- The O(d,d) covariant Lagrangian (5.29) and its field-dependent gauge algebra (5.38) were only skimmed; the "gauge algebra" (5.22)/(5.23) is strictly the Lie algebra underlying a field-dependent algebra with a Chern–Simons-type extra term (sec. 5.2, PDF 25; sec. 5.3, PDF 27–28).
