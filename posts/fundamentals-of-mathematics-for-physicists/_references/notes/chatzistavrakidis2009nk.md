# Conventions

- Signature: never stated explicitly. Everything is in differential-form language with Hodge duals, Einstein frame (PDF p. 5, p. 19). The 4D line element is written $e^{2\alpha\varphi}\eta_{mn}e^m e^n$ in (3.4), so a flat $\eta_{mn}$ is used but its sign convention is not fixed in the text.
- 10D fields: $\mathcal N=1$, $D=10$ supergravity multiplet $(g_{MN},\psi_M,B_{MN},\lambda,\varphi)$ plus vector multiplet $(A_M,\chi)$; gauge group $E_8\times E_8$ (SO(32) dismissed in footnote 1). Hats denote 10D quantities. $\hat\kappa$ has dimensions $[\text{length}]^4$, $\alpha'$ has $[\text{length}]^2$ (PDF p. 5).
- 10D bosonic Lagrangian, eq. (2.1), PDF p. 5 (Einstein frame, ref. [34]):
  $$\hat e^{-1}\mathcal L_b=-\frac{1}{2\hat\kappa^2}\Big(\hat R\,\hat*\mathbf 1+\tfrac12 e^{-\hat\phi}\hat H_{(3)}\wedge\hat*\hat H_{(3)}+\tfrac12 d\hat\phi\wedge\hat* d\hat\phi+\frac{\alpha'}{2}e^{-\hat\phi/2}\,Tr(\hat F_{(2)}\wedge\hat*\hat F_{(2)})\Big).$$
  Equivalently (3.1): $\hat e^{-1}\mathcal L=-\frac{1}{2\hat\kappa^2}\hat R\hat*\mathbf 1-\frac{1}{4\hat\kappa^2}d\hat\phi\wedge\hat*d\hat\phi$; (3.32): $\mathcal L_H=-\frac{1}{4\hat\kappa^2}\hat e\,e^{-\hat\phi}\hat H\wedge\hat*\hat H$; gauge term (PDF p. 14): $\mathcal L_{gauge}=-\frac{\alpha'}{4\hat\kappa^2}\hat e\,e^{-\frac12\hat\phi}Tr\hat F\wedge\hat*\hat F$. Note the overall sign convention: the whole bosonic Lagrangian is written with a global minus sign (so this is a "mostly-minus"-type or $-R$ convention; the paper does not comment).
- Fermionic and interaction terms also in (2.1) (up to four-fermion terms); the Lorentz Chern–Simons/Gauss–Bonnet terms are dropped as "not needed in the minimal supergravity Lagrangian" (PDF p. 6, p. 15).
- H-flux, eq. (3.33): $\hat H=\hat d\hat B-\frac{\alpha'}{2}(\hat\omega_{YM}-\hat\omega_L)$, with $\hat\omega_{YM}=Tr(\hat F\wedge\hat A-\frac13\hat A\wedge\hat A\wedge\hat A)$ (3.35), $\hat\omega_L=Tr(\hat{\tilde\theta}\wedge d\hat{\tilde\theta}+\frac23\hat{\tilde\theta}^3)$ (3.36), $\hat{\tilde\theta}=\hat\theta-\frac12 H$; Bianchi (3.37): $d\hat H=\frac{\alpha'}{2}\big(Tr(\hat{\tilde R}\wedge\hat{\tilde R})-Tr(\hat F\wedge\hat F)\big)$. Only $\omega_{YM}$ is reduced.
- Gauge field: $\hat F=\hat d\hat A+\frac12 f^{\tilde I}{}_{\tilde J\tilde K}\hat A^{\tilde J}\wedge\hat A^{\tilde K}$ (3.21); internal $F_{ab}=f^C_{ab}\phi_C-[\phi_a,\phi_b]$ (3.42); $D\phi^{\tilde I}_A=d\phi^{\tilde I}_A+f^{\tilde I}{}_{\tilde J\tilde K}A^{\tilde J}\phi^{\tilde K}_A$ (3.25).
- 4D coupling: $\kappa^2=\hat\kappa^2/vol_6$ (PDF p. 11). Metric ansatz (3.4): $d\hat s^2=e^{2\alpha\varphi(x)}\eta_{mn}e^me^n+e^{2\beta\varphi(x)}\gamma_{ab}(x)e^ae^b$ with $\alpha=-\frac{\sqrt3}{4}$, $\beta=-\frac{\alpha}{3}$, $\gamma_{ab}$ unimodular; KK gauge fields set to zero because $N(R)/R$ is trivial when $\mathrm{rank}\,S=\mathrm{rank}\,R$ (PDF p. 11, footnote 8).
- Coset conventions (Appendix A, PDF p. 31): $s\sim e^{y^aQ_a}e^{z^iQ_i}$, $L(y)=e^{y^aQ_a}$, Maurer–Cartan form $e=L^{-1}dL=e^aQ_a+e^iQ_i$ (A.1), $e^i=e^i_a(y)e^a$; $de^A=-\frac12 f^A_{BC}e^B\wedge e^C$ (A.3). Reductive algebra (A.4): $[Q_i,Q_j]=f^k_{ij}Q_k,\ [Q_i,Q_a]=f^b_{ia}Q_b,\ [Q_a,Q_b]=f^c_{ab}Q_c+f^i_{ab}Q_i$ (so $f^j_{bi}=0$). Coset Maurer–Cartan (A.7): $de^a=-\frac12 C^a_{bc}(y)e^b\wedge e^c$, $C^a_{bc}=f^a_{bc}-2e^i_{[b}f^a_{c]i}$. Invariant metric (A.8): $g_{\alpha\beta}=\delta_{ab}e^a_\alpha e^b_\beta$; identities (A.9)–(A.11): $e^a\wedge*_de^b=\delta^{ab}vol_d$, $(e^a\wedge e^b)\wedge*_d(e^c\wedge e^d)=\delta^{ab}_{cd}vol_d$, $(e^{abc})\wedge*_d(e^{def})=\delta^{abc}_{def}vol_d$.
- Radii: enter via the diagonal invariant metric $ds^2=R_1^2(e^1e^1+\dots)$ (B.1), (B.11), (B.22); in Sec. 3 they are scalar fields $R_i(x)$ realised through the exponentials of $\varphi,\chi,\psi$ (3.10) and redefinitions (4.3), (4.16), (4.29).
- Torsion: no explicit torsionful connection on the coset is introduced; "torsion" appears only as the SU(3)-structure intrinsic torsion classes $\mathcal W_1,\dots,\mathcal W_5$ (2.4) and via the non-closed expansion forms ($d\omega_i\neq0$).

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) Nearly-Kähler definition and the homogeneous examples

- SU(3)-structure forms from the invariant spinor $\eta$, eq. (2.2), PDF p. 6: $J_{mn}=\mp i\eta^\dagger_\pm\gamma_{mn}\eta_\pm$, $\Omega_{mnp}=\eta^\dagger_-\gamma_{mnp}\eta_+$, $\Omega^*_{mnp}=-\eta^\dagger_+\gamma_{mnp}\eta_-$, with $\eta^\dagger_\pm\eta_\pm=1$.
- Compatibility (2.3): $J\wedge J\wedge J=\frac34 i\,\Omega\wedge\Omega^*$, $J\wedge\Omega=0$.
- Intrinsic torsion classes (2.4):
  $$dJ=\tfrac32 i(\mathcal W_1\Omega^*-\mathcal W_1^*\Omega)+\mathcal W_4\wedge J+\mathcal W_3,\qquad d\Omega=\mathcal W_1 J\wedge J+\mathcal W_2\wedge J+\mathcal W_5^*\wedge\Omega.$$
  Complex iff $\mathcal W_1=\mathcal W_2=0$; Kähler additionally $\mathcal W_3=\mathcal W_4=0$; CY all zero (PDF p. 7).
- Nearly-Kähler: "all the torsion classes but $\mathcal W_1$ are vanishing", so "not Kähler and not even complex" (Sec. 2.2.2, PDF p. 7). No equation number is attached to this definition.
- Homogeneous NK six-manifolds (classification ref. [20], Butruille): $G_2/SU(3)$, $Sp_4/SU(2)\times U(1)$ (non-maximal embedding, footnote 3), $SU(3)/U(1)\times U(1)$ and the group manifold $SU(2)\times SU(2)$. The first three are "the only non-symmetric coset spaces $S/R$ in six dimensions which preserve the rank, namely $rank S=rank R$" (PDF p. 7). The group manifold is excluded because it gives no chiral fermions (Conclusions, PDF p. 30).
- Invariant forms: no S-invariant 1-forms on any of the three; invariant 2-forms $\omega_i$: one, two, three respectively; two invariant 3-forms $\rho_1,\rho_2$ in all cases (PDF p. 7).
- Structure forms in terms of invariant forms, (2.5)–(2.6), PDF p. 8:
  $J=R_1^2\omega_1$ ($G_2/SU(3)$); $J=R_1^2\omega_1+R_2^2\omega_2$ ($Sp_4$); $J=R_1^2\omega_1+R_2^2\omega_2+R_3^2\omega_3$ ($SU(3)$);
  $\Omega=R_1^3(\rho_2+i\rho_1)$; $\Omega=R_1^2R_2(\rho_2+i\rho_1)$; $\Omega=R_1R_2R_3(\rho_2+i\rho_1)$.

## (b) Conditions on the gauge field and the CSDR solution

- The paper imposes NO supersymmetry/BPS (e.g. Hermitian-Yang-Mills or $\Omega\wedge F=0$) conditions on the gauge field; it only imposes the CSDR constraints. Scherk–Schwarz invariance (2.8) $\mathcal L_{X^I}\hat A=0$; CSDR generalised invariance (2.10) $\mathcal L_{X^I}\hat A=DW_I=dW_I+[\hat A,W_I]$ with consistency (2.11) $[\mathcal L_{X^I},\mathcal L_{X^J}]=\mathcal L_{[X^I,X^J]}$.
- Ansatz (2.16)–(2.17) / (3.20): $\hat A^{\tilde I}=A^{\tilde I}(x)+\phi^{\tilde I}_A(x)e^A(y)$, $A$ an S-index split into $(i,a)$. Constraints in compact form (2.18)=(3.29): $D\phi^{\tilde I}_i=F^{\tilde I}_{ai}=F^{\tilde I}_{ij}=0$, with $F^{\tilde I}_{AB}=f^C_{AB}\phi^{\tilde I}_C-f^{\tilde I}{}_{\tilde J\tilde K}\phi^{\tilde J}_A\phi^{\tilde K}_B$ (3.24).
- CSDR rules (PDF p. 9): $H=C_G(R_G)$; scalars from $adjS=adjR+v$ (2.12), $adjG=(adjR,1)+(1,adjH)+\sum(r_i,h_i)$ (2.13); fermions from $F=\sum(t_i,h_i)$ (2.14), $\sigma_d=\sum\sigma_j$ (2.15).
- Explicit solutions: $G_2/SU(3)$ (4.9): $\phi^a=Q^a,\ \phi_\rho=R_1\beta^iQ_{i\rho},\ \phi^\rho=R_1\beta_iQ^{i\rho}$; $Sp_4$ (4.23): $\phi^\rho=G^\rho,\ \phi=\sqrt3G,\ \phi_a=R_1\frac1{\sqrt2}\beta^iG_{1i},\ \phi_+=R_2\gamma^iG_i$; $SU(3)/U(1)^2$ (4.35): $\phi_1=R_1\alpha^iQ_{1i},\ \phi_2=R_2\beta^iQ_{2i},\ \phi_3=R_3\gamma^iQ_{3i}$. The $R$-generators are identified with the corresponding $S$-generators ($\phi^a=Q^a$ etc.); the coset components are the unconstrained Higgs fields in the $27$ of $E_6$.

## (c) The reduced 4D theory for a general nearly-Kähler coset

- Gravity + dilaton (3.5): $\mathcal L=-\frac1{2\kappa^2}\big(R*\mathbf 1-P_{ab}\wedge*P_{ab}+\frac12 d\varphi\wedge*d\varphi\big)-V_{grav}$, with (3.6)
  $$V_{grav}=\frac1{8\kappa^2}e^{2(\alpha-\beta)\varphi}\big(\gamma_{ab}\gamma^{cd}\gamma^{ef}f^a_{ce}f^b_{df}+2\gamma^{ab}f^c_{da}f^d_{cb}+4\gamma^{ab}f_{iac}f^{ic}_b\big)*\mathbf 1,$$
  $P_{ab}=\frac12[(\Phi^{-1})^c_ad\Phi^b_c+(\Phi^{-1})^c_bd\Phi^a_c]$ (3.7), $\gamma_{cd}=\delta_{ab}\Phi^a_c\Phi^b_d$ (3.8). Dilaton: $\hat\phi(x,y)=\phi(x)$, term $-\frac1{4\kappa^2}d\phi\wedge*d\phi$. Parametrisations (3.10): $\gamma_{ab}=\delta_{ab}$; $\mathrm{diag}(e^{2\gamma\chi},e^{2\gamma\chi},e^{-4\gamma\chi},e^{-4\gamma\chi},e^{2\gamma\chi},e^{2\gamma\chi})$; $\mathrm{diag}(e^{2(\gamma\chi+\delta\psi)},e^{2(\gamma\chi+\delta\psi)},e^{2(\gamma\chi-\delta\psi)},e^{2(\gamma\chi-\delta\psi)},e^{-4\gamma\chi},e^{-4\gamma\chi})$, with $\gamma^2=\frac1{24}$, $\delta^2=\frac18$ fixed by (3.13) $P_{ab}\wedge*P_{ab}=12\gamma^2d\chi\wedge*d\chi$ and (3.16) $=4(3\gamma^2d\chi\wedge*d\chi+\delta^2d\psi\wedge*d\psi)$.
- Gravity potentials (3.17)–(3.19): $V_{grav}=-\frac5{\kappa^2}e^{\frac{8\alpha}3\varphi}$; $-\frac1{4\kappa^2}e^{\frac{8\alpha}3\varphi}(4e^{4\gamma\chi}+12e^{-2\gamma\chi}-e^{-8\gamma\chi})$; $-\frac1{4\kappa^2}e^{\frac{8\alpha}3\varphi}(6e^{4\gamma\chi}+6e^{-2(\gamma\chi-\delta\psi)}+6e^{-2(\gamma\chi+\delta\psi)}-e^{4(\gamma\chi+\delta\psi)}-e^{4(\gamma\chi-\delta\psi)}-e^{-8\gamma\chi})$.
- Gauge sector (3.30): $\mathcal L_{gauge}=-\frac{\alpha'}{4\kappa^2}e^{-\frac12\phi}\big[e^{-2\alpha\varphi}F^{\tilde I}\wedge*_4F^{\tilde I}\wedge vol_6+e^{-2\beta\varphi}\gamma^{ab}D\phi^{\tilde I}_a\wedge*_4D\phi^{\tilde I}_b\wedge vol_6\big]-V_{gauge}$, (3.31) $V_{gauge}=\frac{\alpha'}{8\kappa^2}e^{-\frac12\phi+2(\alpha-2\beta)\varphi}\gamma^{ac}\gamma^{bd}F_{ab}F_{cd}$.
- Three-form sector: $\hat B=B(x)+b^i(x)\omega_i(y)$ (3.34) (no $B_a\wedge e^a$ term since no invariant 1-forms); $\hat H^{(B)}=dB+db^i\wedge\omega_i+b^id\omega_i$ (3.38) — the $b^id\omega_i$ term is absent for CY; full (3.41): $\hat H=dB+db^i\wedge\omega_i+\frac{\alpha'}2Tr(\phi_aD\phi_b)\wedge e^{ab}+b^id\omega_i-\frac{\alpha'}3Tr(\phi_a\phi_b\phi_c)e^{abc}+\frac{\alpha'}4f^d_{ab}Tr(\phi_c\phi_d)e^{abc}$. Geometric constants (3.45): $\omega_i\wedge*\omega_j=m\delta_{ij}vol_6$, $\omega_i\wedge*e^{ab}=\epsilon_i^{ab}vol_6$, $d\omega_i\wedge*d\omega_j=(n_1\delta_{ij}+n_2\epsilon_{ij})vol_6$, $d\omega_i\wedge*e^{abc}=\epsilon_i^{abc}vol_6$. Dualisation (3.46) $e^{4\alpha\varphi}d\theta=*dB$ gives the axion $\theta$. Final (3.47):
  $$\mathcal L_H=-\frac1{4\kappa^2}e^{-\phi}\Big[e^{4\alpha\varphi}d\theta\wedge*d\theta-e^{4\alpha\varphi}\theta F^I\wedge F^I+me^{-4\beta\varphi}db^i\wedge*db^i+\alpha'e^{-4\beta\varphi}\epsilon_i^{ab}db^i\wedge Tr(\phi_a*D\phi_b)+\frac{\alpha'^2}4e^{-4\beta\varphi}Tr(\phi_a\overleftrightarrow D\phi_b)\wedge Tr(\phi_a*\overleftrightarrow D\phi_b)\Big]\wedge vol_6-V_H\wedge vol_6,$$
  (3.48) $V_H=\frac1{4\kappa^2}\frac{e^{-\phi+4\alpha\varphi}}{R^6}\Big[b^ib^j(n_1\delta_{ij}+n_2\epsilon_{ij})-\frac{2\alpha'}3\epsilon_i^{abc}b^iTr(\phi_a\phi_b\phi_c)+\frac{\alpha'}2\epsilon_i^{abc}b^iTr(f^d_{ab}\phi_c\phi_d)+\frac{2\alpha'^2}3Tr(\phi_a\phi_b\phi_c)^2+\frac{\alpha'^2}{16}Tr(f^d_{ab}\phi_c\phi_d)Tr(f^d_{[ab}\phi_{c]}\phi_d)-\alpha'^2Tr(\phi_a\phi_b\phi_c)Tr(f^d_{ab}\phi_c\phi_d)\Big]vol_4$.
- Total 4D potential $=V_{grav}+V_{gauge}+V_H$. The gauge group and 4D content follow the CSDR rules (always $E_6$ times possible U(1)s, see (d)).

## (e) Relation to Calabi–Yau compactification

- CY: nowhere-vanishing spinor covariantly constant w.r.t. the torsionless Levi-Civita connection (SU(3) holonomy); SU(3)-structure: covariantly constant w.r.t. a connection with torsion; CY $\subset$ SU(3)-structure (Sec. 1, 2.2.1, PDF pp. 3, 6).
- Motivation: in CY compactifications the moduli are flat directions; flux compactifications/SU(3)-structure manifolds address moduli stabilisation (PDF p. 3). NK spaces are the homogeneous SU(3)-structure manifolds with only $\mathcal W_1\neq0$; the authors reduce "at first order in $\alpha'$" with the heterotic Bianchi identity, and the $\hat H$ flux is generated internally by $\alpha'\,\omega_{YM}$ and by the non-closed $b^id\omega_i$ (3.41). Ref. [17] (Manousselis–Prezas–Zoupanos) is cited for supersymmetric $AdS_4\times S/R$ solutions with fluxes and condensates.
- Surviving supersymmetry: the 4D theory is cast as $\mathcal N=1$ supergravity (Sec. 5) with $K$ and $W$ reproducing the reduced potential, but the potential contains terms "which could be interpreted as soft scalar masses and trilinear soft terms in four dimensions, in case the minimization of the full potential would lead to Minkowski vacuum" (Conclusions, PDF p. 30; also PDF p. 20). This is the "softly broken $\mathcal N=1$" of ref. [30]. No Minkowski/AdS vacuum is found in the paper.
- Differences from CY in the reduction: expansion forms are S-invariant and not harmonic ($d\omega_i\neq0$) — extra term in (3.38), extra $b^ib^j(n_1\delta_{ij}+n_2\epsilon_{ij})$ mass term in (3.48); no complex-structure moduli (footnote 14) since the structure is not complex; Kähler moduli = radii only (1, 2, 3); no invariant 1-forms so no $B_a$ vectors; nearly-Kähler limit is a point in moduli space ($T_1=T_2(=T_3)$).

## (f) Comparison with the standard CSDR scheme

- Same: ansatz (3.20)=(2.16), constraints (2.18)/(3.29), rules for $H=C_G(R_G)$, scalar content from (2.12)–(2.13), fermion content from (2.14)–(2.15), gauge potential (3.31) and its $E_6$-covariant forms (4.10), (4.24), (4.36) taken from Manousselis–Zoupanos [30]; the structure constants and generator splittings (4.6), (4.8), (4.20), (4.22), (4.32), (4.34).
- Changes: (i) gravity and dilaton are reduced with the Weyl-rescaled ansatz (3.4) ($\alpha=-\sqrt3/4$, $\beta=-\alpha/3$) producing $V_{grav}$ (3.6) and extra moduli $\varphi,\chi,\psi$ (unimodular $\gamma_{ab}$); (ii) KK gauge fields are dropped by consistency since $N(R)/R$ is trivial for rank-preserving cosets (footnote 8, PDF p. 11); (iii) a new sector — the two-form $\hat B$ expanded in invariant 2-forms — gives axion $\theta$, scalars $b^i$, and $V_H$ (3.48), which couples the Higgs fields to $b^i$ and adds quartic/sextic terms; (iv) $\alpha'$-dependent dilaton factors $e^{-\phi/2}$, $e^{-\phi}$ multiply gauge and $H$ sectors, requiring the field redefinitions (4.1), (4.14), (4.27) so that the 4D dilaton $\tilde\phi$ appears uniformly ($e^{-\tilde\phi/2}$ in $V_{gauge}$, $e^{-\tilde\phi}$ in $V_{grav},V_H$); (v) the radii are promoted to $x$-dependent fields (4.3), (4.16), (4.29); (vi) the result is interpreted in $\mathcal N=1$ SUGRA language via GVW (5.5) rather than as a pure gauge theory; (vii) the Einstein-frame result must be multiplied by $e^{\tilde\phi/2}$ to reach string-frame variables (PDF p. 19, ref. [19]).

# Caveats

- No BPS/supersymmetry condition on the gauge field is imposed anywhere; the item (b) requested by the tutorial must be sourced elsewhere (e.g. refs. [13], [17] of this paper). Here the gauge background is fixed purely by the CSDR constraints, and supersymmetry is addressed only a posteriori by fitting the 4D potential to an $\mathcal N=1$ $K,W$.
- The reduction is at first order in $\alpha'$ and keeps only the YM Chern–Simons term in $H$; the Lorentz Chern–Simons form, the Gauss–Bonnet term and four-fermion terms are dropped. The Bianchi identity (3.37) is quoted but not solved; the paper is an effective-action reduction, not a vacuum solution.
- The signature and the sign of the curvature convention are never stated; all Lagrangians carry an overall minus sign. Be careful when comparing (2.1)/(3.1) with other heterotic references (factor $\frac12$ in front of $d\phi\wedge*d\phi$, $e^{-\phi}$ for $H^2$, $e^{-\phi/2}$ for $F^2$ in the Einstein frame).
- Kinetic normalisation choices are fixed by hand: $\alpha=-\sqrt3/4$, $\beta=-\alpha/3$, $\gamma^2=1/24$, $\delta^2=1/8$ (PDF pp. 11–13); the "$R$" in $V_H$ (3.48) is reinserted by hand to keep track of dimensions (footnote 13).
- The constants $m,n_1,n_2$ in (3.45) depend on the index $i$ for $Sp_4/SU(2)\times U(1)$ (B.21), even though (3.45) writes them as single numbers; the $Sp_4$ structure constants (B.12) contain the non-uniform entries $2f_{789}$ and $2f_{10,3,4}$.
- Footnote 3: the $Sp_4/SU(2)\times U(1)$ used is the non-maximal embedding; the maximal one is symmetric and has no SU(3)-structure. The equation (4.36) prefactor "$\sqrt2 80$" is printed as such (presumably $80\sqrt2$); transcribed as printed.
- The expansion of $\hat B$ in invariant 2-forms is "an appropriate ansatz" rather than a derived truncation; the paper claims consistency only through the CSDR/rank argument for the metric and gauge sectors.
- The 4D U(1) factors in $H$ are stated to be anomalous/massive (Green–Schwarz-type, ref. [48]), so the physical gauge group is $E_6$ in all three cases.
- $G_2/SU(3)$ in the paper has a single radius and is NK for any $R_1$; the "nearly-Kähler limit" is only non-trivial for the other two cosets, where it corresponds to $T_1=T_2(=T_3)$ in the SUGRA variables.
- Equation (4.24) contains an unspecified "const" and (4.36) "const."; the paper does not give these constants explicitly.
