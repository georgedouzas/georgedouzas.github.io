# Conventions

- Spacetime: four-dimensional Minkowski spacetime with coordinates x^mu, mu = 1,...,4 (sec. 2, p. 4). No metric signature is written anywhere in the paper; no explicit Lagrangian or Dirac operator appears. Only the scalar potential is written out.
- Starting theory: N=4 SYM with gauge group SU(3N) ("for notational convenience", footnote 1, p. 3). In N=1 language: one SU(3N) vector multiplet plus three adjoint chiral multiplets Phi^i, i = 1,2,3. Components: gauge bosons A_mu; six real adjoint scalars phi^a, a = 1..6, in the 6 of SU(4)_R; four adjoint Weyl fermions psi^p, p = 1..4, in the 4 of SU(4)_R. The paper works with three complex scalars phi^i (footnote 2), the complexification of the six real ones, and the three Weyl fermions psi^i that are their N=1 partners (the fourth Weyl fermion is the gaugino, singlet under SU(3)). R-symmetry embedding (eq. (1), p. 4): SU(4)_R ⊃ SU(3) x U(1)_R, 4 = 1_3 + 3_{-1}, 6 = 3_2 + 3bar_{-2}.
- Superpotential: W_{N=4} = epsilon_{ijk} Tr(Phi^i Phi^j Phi^k) (eq. (12), p. 6; repeated as (36), p. 10).
- Orbifold: Z_3 generated by g, labelled by integers a = (a_1,a_2,a_3) with a_1+a_2+a_3 = 0 mod 3 (this is equivalent to Z_3 ⊂ SU(3) and hence to N=1 survival). Paper uses a = (1,1,-2), so b_i = a_i. omega = e^{2 pi i/3}. Action on scalars gamma(g)_{ij} = delta_{ij} omega^{a_i} (eq. (2)); on fermions delta_{ij} omega^{b_i} (eq. (3)) with b_1 = -(a_2+a_3-a_1)/2 etc. (eq. (4)). Action on gauge indices through gamma_3 = diag(1_N, omega 1_N, omega^2 1_N) (eq. (5), p. 5).
- Fuzzy sphere: generators lambda^i_{(N)} of SU(2) in the N-dimensional irreducible representation. The ordinary fuzzy sphere relation used is [phi~^i, phi~^j] = i epsilon_{ijk} phi~^k together with phi~^i phi~^i = R^2 (eqs. (19)-(20), pp. 6-7). The Casimir value R^2 is never written in terms of N (no "(N^2-1)/4" formula appears); the paper only states [R^2, phi^i] = 0. The "twisted" fuzzy sphere S~^2_N is defined by [phi^i, phi^j] = i epsilon_{ijk} (phi^k)^dagger, phi^i (phi^i)^dagger = R^2 (eqs. (14)-(15), p. 6).
- Potential normalisation: V^{(proj)}_{N=1}(phi) = (1/4) Tr([phi^i,phi^j]^dagger [phi^i,phi^j]) (eq. (25)); D-term V_D = (1/2) D^2 = (1/2) D^I D_I with D^I = phi_i^dagger T^I phi^i (p. 8).

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) Orbifold projection (sec. 2, pp. 4-6)

Z_3 embedded in SU(3) ⊂ SU(4)_R so N=1 survives; three embeddings discussed (maximal in SU(4)_R: N=0; in SU(3): N=1; in SU(2): N=2) (p. 4).

Gauge-index action, eq. (5), p. 5:
$$\gamma_3=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{1}_N&0&0\\0&\omega\mathbb{1}_N&0\\0&0&\omega^2\mathbb{1}_N\end{pmatrix}.$$
"anomaly freedom of the projected theory typically requires that the dimension of the three blocks is the same" (p. 5; exception: sec. 4.1 with SU(2)).

Surviving fields are those invariant under the combined action on geometry (R-symmetry) and gauge indices [23]:
- Gauge bosons, eq. (6): $A_\mu=\gamma_3 A_\mu\gamma_3^{-1}$, so $SU(3N)\to H=SU(N)\times SU(N)\times SU(N)$.
- Scalars, eq. (7): $\phi^i=\omega^{a_i}\gamma_3\phi^i\gamma_3^{-1}$; with gauge indices I,J, eq. (8): $\phi^i_{IJ}=\omega^{I-J+a_i}\phi^i_{IJ}$, hence J = I + a_i and the survivors are $\phi_{I,I+a_i}$, transforming under H as eq. (9), p. 5:
$$3\cdot\Big((N,\overline N,1)+(\overline N,1,N)+(1,N,\overline N)\Big).$$
- Fermions, eqs. (10)-(11): $\psi^i=\omega^{b_i}\gamma_3\psi^i\gamma_3^{-1}$, $\psi^i_{IJ}=\omega^{I-J+b_i}\psi^i_{IJ}$; survivors $\psi^i_{I,I+b_i}$ in the same reps (9) ("another manifestation of the N=1 remnant supersymmetry"). Reps (9) guarantee gauge-anomaly freedom (footnote 3: unequal blocks would be anomalous).
Two features noted (pp. 5-6): the fermions are in chiral (complex bifundamental) reps whose conjugates do not appear; there are three generations because of the three chiral multiplets.

Projected superpotential, eq. (13), p. 6:
$$W^{(proj)}_{\mathcal N=1}=\sum_I\epsilon_{ijk}\Phi^i_{I,I+a_i}\Phi^j_{I+a_i,I+a_i+a_j}\Phi^k_{I+a_i+a_j,I},$$
using a_1+a_2+a_3 = 0 mod 3.

## (b) Fuzzy sphere: definition, dimensional-reduction interpretation, spectrum (sec. 3, pp. 6-10)

Twisted fuzzy sphere, eqs. (14)-(15), p. 6:
$$[\phi^i,\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}(\phi^k)^\dagger,\qquad \phi^i(\phi^i)^\dagger=R^2,$$
with [R^2, phi^i] = 0. (14) is compatible with the Z_3 action (7), "in contrast to the usual fuzzy sphere". Untwisting, eqs. (16)-(18): $\phi^i=\Omega\tilde\phi^i$ with $\Omega^3=1,\ [\Omega,\phi^i]=0,\ \Omega^\dagger=\Omega^{-1}$ and $(\tilde\phi^i)^\dagger=\tilde\phi^i$, i.e. $(\phi^i)^\dagger=\Omega\phi^i$. Then (14) reduces to the ordinary fuzzy sphere, eqs. (19)-(20):
$$[\tilde\phi^i,\tilde\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\tilde\phi^k,\qquad \tilde\phi^i\tilde\phi^i=R^2.$$
"It is remarkable that this construction is possible only for Z_3 and for no other Z_n" (p. 7).

Two realisations: eq. (21) $\phi^i=\omega\lambda^i_{(3N)}$ (Omega = omega; 3N-dim irrep); eq. (22)-(23)
$$\phi^i=\Omega\,(\mathbb{1}_3\otimes\lambda^i_{(N)}),\qquad \Omega=\Omega_3\otimes\mathbb{1}_N,\quad \Omega_3=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\0&0&1\\1&0&0\end{pmatrix},\quad \Omega^3=\mathbb{1}.$$
Eq. (24), p. 7, shows that the off-diagonal orbifold-sector matrix (blocks $(\lambda^i_{(N)})_{(N,\bar N,1)}$, $(\lambda^i_{(N)})_{(1,N,\bar N)}$, $(\lambda^i_{(N)})_{(\bar N,1,N)}$) equals $\Omega\,\mathrm{diag}(\lambda^i_{(N)},\lambda^i_{(N)},\lambda^i_{(N)})$: three fuzzy spheres of fuzziness N, twisted together. Since $\Omega_3=U^{-1}\mathrm{diag}(1,\omega,\omega^2)U$, (22) is a combination of (21) and an untwisted sphere. Solution (22) breaks SU(N)^3 completely.

Dynamical generation (sec. 3.2). F-term potential of the projected theory, eq. (25), p. 8:
$$V^{(proj)}_{\mathcal N=1}(\phi)=\tfrac14 Tr([\phi^i,\phi^j]^\dagger[\phi^i,\phi^j]),$$
minimised at vanishing vevs, so no fuzzy-sphere vacuum without modification. The ordinary condition $[\phi^i,\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\phi^k$ (eq. (26)) is incompatible with the orbifold; (14) is compatible. Soft SUSY-breaking terms, eq. (27):
$$V_{SSB}=\tfrac12\sum_i m_i^2\,\phi^{i\dagger}\phi^i+\tfrac12\sum_{i,j,k}h_{ijk}\phi^i\phi^j\phi^k+h.c.,$$
with h_{ijk} = 0 unless i+j+k = 0 mod 3. Full potential, eq. (28): $V=V^{(proj)}_{\mathcal N=1}+V_{SSB}+V_D$, $V_D=\tfrac12 D^2$, $D^I=\phi_i^\dagger T^I\phi^i$. With h_{ijk} = epsilon_{ijk}, m_i^2 = 1, eqs. (29)-(30), pp. 8-9:
$$V=\tfrac14(F^{ij})^\dagger F^{ij}+V_D,\qquad F^{ij}=[\phi^i,\phi^j]-i\epsilon^{ijk}(\phi^k)^\dagger.$$
The first term is positive definite and vanishes iff (14) holds, so the global minimum is a twisted fuzzy sphere "at least for a suitable range of parameters"; V_D "will typically only modify its radius, as in the case of the ordinary fuzzy sphere [26,27]". F^{ij} "will be interpreted in the following as the field strength on the spontaneously generated fuzzy extra dimensions" (p. 9). Possible metastable vacua with different radii are not discussed.

SU(2) embedding of the fuzzy-sphere structure in SU(4)_R, eqs. (31)-(32), p. 9: SU(4)_R ⊃ SU(2)^I x SU(2)^II x U(1) and SU(4)_R ⊃ SU(2)^alpha x SU(2)^beta; the diagonal SU(2)^A of these is the maximal embedding in SU(3), with 3 = 3 under SU(3) ⊃ SU(2)^A.

Partial-breaking vacuum, eq. (33), p. 9:
$$\phi^i=\Omega\Big(\mathbb{1}_3\otimes(\lambda^i_{(N-n)}\oplus 0_n)\Big),$$
breaking $SU(N)^3\to SU(n)^3$; "This vacuum should be interpreted as $\mathbb R^4\times\tilde S^2_N$ with a twisted fuzzy sphere in the phi_i coordinates."

Fluctuations/spectrum, eqs. (34)-(35), pp. 9-10: $\tilde\phi^i=\lambda^i_{(N)}+A^i$, and
$$F^{ij}=[\phi^i,\phi^j]-i\epsilon^{ijk}(\phi^k)^\dagger=\Omega^2\big([\tilde\phi^i,\tilde\phi^j]-i\epsilon^{ijk}\tilde\phi^k\big)$$
as long as (17)-(18) hold. "The vacuum can thus be interpreted at intermediate energy scales as $\mathbb R^4\times S^2_N$ with three (untwisted) fuzzy spheres in the $\tilde\phi^i_L$ coordinates. Moreover, due to the orbifolding condition there are no off-diagonal components relating these different spheres. It now follows as in [26,28] that the gauge fields and fermions can be decomposed into Kaluza-Klein towers of massive modes on $S^2_N$ resp. $\tilde S^2_N$ due to the Higgs effect, as well as a massless sector" (p. 10). No explicit mass formula for the KK tower is given in this paper; it is deferred to refs. [26,28] (Aschieri-Grammatikopoulos-Steinacker-Zoupanos 2006; Steinacker-Zoupanos 2007).

## (c) Relation to ordinary CSDR / continuum S^2 / N -> infinity

The paper contains no explicit comparison equation between the fuzzy and the continuous reduction, no N -> infinity limit statement, and no commutative S^2 potential. The only references to CSDR are in the Introduction (pp. 1-2): CSDR [8-10] and Scherk-Schwarz [11] are cited as the pioneering gauge-Higgs-Yukawa unification schemes where "the four-dimensional gauge and Higgs fields are simply the surviving components of the gauge fields of a pure gauge theory defined in higher dimensions"; Yukawas arise from higher-dimensional fermions; chirality in 4D is possible [12]; CSDR can also produce the soft SUSY-breaking sector [19]. The fuzzy-CSDR programme [24] (Aschieri-Madore-Manousselis-Zoupanos) is summarised as: renormalisable in 4 and higher dimensions, non-abelian structures from abelian higher-dimensional theory, but "the accommodation of chiral fermions in four dimensions turns out to be a difficult task" (p. 2). The reversed ("deconstruction") approach [26] is summarised as: start in 4D with scalars and a potential whose vacua are "dynamically generated fuzzy extra dimensions" with "a finite Kaluza-Klein tower of massive modes"; with fermions the best obtained so far were mirror fermions in bifundamentals [28,29] (p. 2). The present paper's claim is that orbifolding fixes the chirality obstruction.

## (d) Chiral fermions: mechanism and spectrum (secs. 2, 4, 4.4)

Mechanism: the Z_3 projection with gamma_3 of equal blocks leaves fermions only in the complex bifundamentals (9) with no conjugates (p. 5-6), i.e. the theory is chiral already before the fuzzy vacuum. The fuzzy vacuum (33) then breaks SU(N)^3 -> SU(n)^3 (or to the GUT group); fields that can pair with the vevs become a finite massive KK tower, the rest remain massless and chiral. Section 4.4 (p. 14), SU(3)^3 case: vevs in eq. (60),
$$\langle(n,\bar n,1;1,1,1)\rangle,\ \langle(1,n,\bar n;1,1,1)\rangle,\ \langle(\bar n,1,n;1,1,1)\rangle,$$
and trilinear invariants (Yukawas) eqs. (61)-(62):
$$(1,n,\bar n;1,1,1)\langle(n,\bar n,1;1,1,1)\rangle(\bar n,1,n;1,1,1)\ +\text{cyclic},\qquad (\bar n,1,1;1,1,3)\langle(n,\bar n,1;1,1,1)\rangle(1,n,1;1,1,\bar3)\ \text{etc.}$$
give masses to all fermions except $(1,1,1;3,\bar3,1),(1,1,1;\bar3,1,3),(1,1,1;1,3,\bar3)$, for which "there does not exist any trilinear invariant that they could form with one of the scalar fields which acquire a vev", so they stay massless and chiral, protected by chiral symmetry. "Therefore a finite Kaluza-Klein tower of massive fermionic modes appears, consistent with the interpretation of the vacuum (56) as a higher-dimensional theory with spontaneously generated fuzzy extra dimensions." Remark (p. 14): in (62) the "internal" and "observable" structure mix, so KK fermion masses may affect SU(3)^3 phenomenology.

## (e) Explicit models (secs. 4-5)

All start from N=4 SU(3N) SYM, Z_3 in SU(3)_R, giving SU(N)^3 with three families in (37) $(N,\bar N,1)+(\bar N,1,N)+(1,N,\bar N)$; minimal anomaly-free targets: SU(4)xSU(2)xSU(2), SU(4)^3, SU(3)^3 (p. 10).

Pati-Salam (4.1): N = n_1+4 = n_2+2, eq. (38); SU(N) ⊃ SU(n_1)xSU(4)xU(1), SU(N) ⊃ SU(n_2)xSU(2)xU(1), eq. (39); full decomposition (40)-(41). Anomaly-free only because the 2 of SU(2) is self-conjugate (p. 11). Low-energy content, eq. (42): $3\cdot((4,2,1)+(\bar4,1,2)+(1,2,2))$. Vacuum, eq. (43):
$$\phi^i=\Omega\Big(0_2\oplus\mathbb{1}_3\otimes(\lambda^i_{(N-2)}\oplus0_2)\Big),\qquad \Omega=\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{1}_2&0\\0&\Omega_3\otimes\mathbb{1}_N\end{pmatrix}.$$
First generation in (44): f ~ (4,2,1) holds (d_L^a,u_L^a; e_L,nu_L), f^c ~ (4bar,1,2) holds (d^c,e^c; u^c,nu^c); h ~ (1,2,2) Higgses/Higgsinos.

SU(4)^3 (4.2): N = n+4, eq. (45); content (46); vacuum eq. (47) $\phi^i=\Omega(\mathbb{1}_3\otimes(\lambda^i_{(N-4)}\oplus0_4))$; low energy eq. (48) $3\cdot((4,\bar4,1)+(\bar4,1,4)+(1,4,\bar4))$; fermion matrices (49) with new heavy quarks/leptons. One-loop beta coefficient of each SU(4), eq. (50):
$$b=\Big(-\tfrac{11}3+\tfrac23\Big)\cdot3+n_f\Big(\tfrac23+\tfrac13\Big)\cdot\tfrac12\cdot2\cdot3,$$
giving b = 0 for n_f = 3, eq. (51): "a general feature of models with a SU(N)^k gauge group, independently of the values of N and k [44]".

SU(3)^3 (4.3): N = n+3 (52); SU(N) ⊃ SU(n)xSU(3)xU(1) (53)-(54); content (55); vacuum eq. (56) $\phi^i=\Omega[\mathbb{1}_3\otimes(\lambda^i_{(N-3)}\oplus0_3)]$; low energy eq. (57) $3\cdot((3,\bar3,1)+(\bar3,1,3)+(1,3,\bar3))$; quarks (58) q=(d u h) ~ (3,3bar,1), q^c ~ (3bar,1,3); leptons (59) lambda = [[N,E^c,nu],[E,N^c,e],[nu^c,e^c,S]] ~ (1,3,3bar).

Sec. 5 ("fuzzy breaking"): start instead from SU(8), SU(12), SU(9) with gamma_3 = diag(1_4, omega 1_2, omega^2 1_2) (63), diag(1_4, omega 1_4, omega^2 1_4) (76), diag(1_3, omega 1_3, omega^2 1_3) (80). Superpotentials: $W=Y Tr(hf^cf)$ (65),(78); SU(3)^3 eq. (82):
$$W^{(proj)}_{\mathcal N=1}(\lambda,q,q^c)=Y\,Tr(\lambda q^c q)+Y'\epsilon_{ijk}\epsilon_{abc}(\lambda_{ia}\lambda_{jb}\lambda_{kc}+q^c_{ia}q^c_{jb}q^c_{kc}+q_{ia}q_{jb}q_{kc}).$$
Pati-Salam breaking: hypercharge $Y=B-L+2T^3_R$ (67), vev (68) in (4bar,1,2) along nu^c; $Q=T^3_L+Y/2$ (69); EW vev (70) diagonal; extra U(1)s get EW-scale masses, "this model does not seem to be phenomenologically viable" (p. 16). Rank-one vevs (71)-(73) satisfy $H^1H^2\sim\overline{H^3}$ + cyclic, "almost" a twisted fuzzy sphere; generalised relation eqs. (74)-(75): $[H^i,H^j]=ih_{ijk}\overline{H^k}$, $h_{ijk}\equiv\frac{k_ik_j}{k_k}\epsilon_{ijk}$ (no sum). SU(4)^3: two charge assignments (79), Q_2 gives sin^2 theta_W = 3/14, Q_1 leaves an unwanted U(1): not viable (p. 17). SU(3)^3: one-family matrix (83)-(84); only lepton block can get vevs, in S, nu, nu^c (-> MSSM) and N, N^c (-> EW); relabelled lambda' = Omega_3 lambda (85), vacuum (86)-(88) with k_1,k_2,k_3, satisfying eq. (89) $[\lambda'^i,\lambda'^j]=ih_{ijk}(\lambda'^k)^\dagger$ with (90) $h_{ijk}=\frac{k_ik_j}{k_k}\epsilon_{ijk}$ — a multi-scale twisted fuzzy sphere; second relabelling (91) assigns the same vacuum to nu, nu^c, N^c. Result: SU(3)^3 -> MSSM -> SU(3)_c x U(1)_em "solely due to the Higgsing of the twisted fuzzy spheres in the extra dimensions, without the need of any additional superfields and without the need of any intermediate breaking" (p. 20).

# Attributable statements

1. Z_3 orbifold projection of N=4 SU(3N) SYM with gamma_3 = diag(1_N, omega 1_N, omega^2 1_N) yields N=1 SU(N)^3 with three chiral families in $(N,\bar N,1)+(\bar N,1,N)+(1,N,\bar N)$, chiral and anomaly free (sec. 2, p. 5, eqs. (5)-(11)).
2. The ordinary fuzzy-sphere relation $[\phi^i,\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\phi^k$ is incompatible with the orbifold projection; the twisted relation $[\phi^i,\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}(\phi^k)^\dagger$ is compatible and is related to the ordinary one by $\phi^i=\Omega\tilde\phi^i$, $\Omega^3=1$ (sec. 3.1, pp. 6-7, eqs. (14)-(19); sec. 3.2, p. 8, eq. (26)).
3. The twisted construction "is possible only for Z_3 and for no other Z_n" (sec. 3.1, p. 7).
4. The F-term potential of the projected theory is minimised at zero vev, so fuzzy vacua require soft SUSY-breaking terms $m_i^2$ and $h_{ijk}$ (with i+j+k = 0 mod 3); with $h_{ijk}=\epsilon_{ijk}$, $m_i^2=1$ the potential becomes $\frac14 F^\dagger F+V_D$ with $F^{ij}=[\phi^i,\phi^j]-i\epsilon^{ijk}(\phi^k)^\dagger$, whose global minimum is a twisted fuzzy sphere; V_D only shifts the radius (sec. 3.2, pp. 8-9, eqs. (25)-(30)).
5. The vacuum $\phi^i=\Omega(\mathbb 1_3\otimes(\lambda^i_{(N-n)}\oplus0_n))$ breaks SU(N)^3 to SU(n)^3 and is interpreted as $\mathbb R^4\times\tilde S^2_N$; fluctuations decompose into a finite KK tower on three untwisted fuzzy spheres plus a massless sector (sec. 3.2, pp. 9-10, eqs. (33)-(35)).
6. Earlier fuzzy-CSDR [24] and the deconstruction-like reversed approach [26,28,29] could not produce chiral fermions (only mirror fermions); the orbifold structure is what makes chiral low-energy models possible (Introduction p. 2; Conclusions p. 20).
7. Chirality of the massless sector is a consequence of the absence of trilinear invariants pairing the chiral fermions with the vev-carrying scalars; the remaining fermions acquire masses through trilinear Yukawa invariants and form a finite KK tower (sec. 4.4, p. 14, eqs. (60)-(62)).
8. For SU(N)^k with three families the one-loop beta coefficient vanishes, b = 0, a necessary condition for finiteness (sec. 4.2, p. 12, eqs. (50)-(51)); SU(3)^3 "can be promoted even to an all-loop finite theory" (Conclusions, p. 21).
9. In the SU(3)^3 model the breaking to the MSSM and then to SU(3)_c x U(1)_em is achieved by vevs of S, nu, nu^c and N, N^c in the lepton block, which form a multi-scale twisted fuzzy sphere $[\lambda'^i,\lambda'^j]=ih_{ijk}(\lambda'^k)^\dagger$, $h_{ijk}=\frac{k_ik_j}{k_k}\epsilon_{ijk}$ (sec. 5.3, pp. 19-20, eqs. (86)-(90)).
10. The Pati-Salam and SU(4)^3 fuzzy-breaking variants are judged not phenomenologically viable (extra U(1)s at the EW scale; sin^2 theta_W = 3/14 or an unbroken extra U(1)) (sec. 5.1, p. 16; sec. 5.2, p. 17).

# Caveats

- No metric signature, no explicit 4D Lagrangian, no Dirac operator and no KK mass formula are written in this paper; the KK-tower statement on p. 10 is by reference to [26,28]. The tutorial must take the spectrum from those papers, not from here.
- The fuzzy-sphere radius R^2 is never expressed in terms of N (no Casimir formula); "fuzziness N" means the N-dimensional irrep of SU(2). The normalisation $[\tilde\phi^i,\tilde\phi^j]=i\epsilon_{ijk}\tilde\phi^k$ has no explicit scale factor; the actual radius is said to be fixed by V_D "and a suitable range of parameters" without a computation.
- No N -> infinity or commutative-S^2 comparison appears; any statement in the tutorial about the continuum limit cannot be attributed to this paper.
- The h_{ijk} = epsilon_{ijk}, m_i^2 = 1 choice is a special case; sec. 5 insists that realistic models need different scales (GUT vs EW), i.e. the generalised h_{ijk} of (75)/(90). Footnote 6 notes gaugino masses and other soft terms are omitted; footnote 8 notes that EW breaking of the MSSM needs extra soft terms.
- The claim that the twisted-sphere vacuum is the global minimum is qualified ("at least for a suitable range of parameters"); metastable vacua and phase transitions are explicitly not studied (p. 9).
- The gamma_3 with equal N-blocks is required for anomaly freedom except in the Pati-Salam case, where the self-conjugacy of the SU(2) doublet rescues it (p. 5 and p. 11). The U(1)^3 factors in secs. 4-5 are assumed to decouple via Green-Schwarz (footnote 7).
- Printed page numbers are one less than PDF page numbers; the note uses printed numbers except where the Structure section lists both.
- The Z_3 labels: the paper chooses a = (1,1,-2) so that b_i = a_i; eq. (8) uses $\phi^i_{IJ}=\omega^{I-J+a_i}\phi^i_{IJ}$ where I, J label the three N-blocks (mod 3), not individual matrix entries.
- "Pati-Salam vacuum" (43) uses 0_2 ⊕ 1_3⊗(λ_(N−2)⊕0_2) with Ω containing 1_2: the first 2x2 block sits outside the Ω_3 ⊗ 1_N structure; the tutorial should not simplify this to the (33) form.
