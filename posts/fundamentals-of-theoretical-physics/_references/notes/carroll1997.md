# Conventions

- Signature: mostly plus, $\eta_{\mu\nu} = \mathrm{diag}(-1,+1,+1,+1)$, eq. (1.8), PDF 11 (printed 4), with the warning "Some references, especially field theory books, define the metric with the opposite sign, so be careful." Greek indices 0-3, Latin spatial.
- Units: $c = 1$, eq. (1.6), PDF 10 (printed 3); $G$ and $\hbar$ kept explicit (Planck length $l_P = (G\hbar/c^3)^{1/2}$, eq. (4.34), PDF 116).
- Christoffel symbols (torsion-free, metric compatible), eq. (3.21), PDF 67 (printed 60):
  $\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac{1}{2} g^{\sigma\rho}\left(\partial_\mu g_{\nu\rho} + \partial_\nu g_{\rho\mu} - \partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu}\right)$.
  Useful trace, eq. (3.27), PDF 68: $\Gamma^{\mu}_{\mu\lambda} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{|g|}}\partial_\lambda \sqrt{|g|}$; and eq. (4.89), PDF 131: $g^{\rho\sigma}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\rho\sigma} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\partial_\lambda(\sqrt{-g}\, g^{\lambda\mu})$.
- Riemann tensor defined through the commutator, eq. (3.66), PDF 82: $[\nabla_\mu,\nabla_\nu]V^\rho = R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}V^\sigma - T_{\mu\nu}{}^{\lambda}\nabla_\lambda V^\rho$, with the explicit formula eq. (3.67), PDF 83 (printed 76):
  $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = \partial_\mu \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\sigma} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\mu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\sigma} - \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\lambda}\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\mu\sigma}$.
  Carroll notes (PDF 82) that there is "no agreement at all" in the literature on index ordering. Loop interpretation eq. (3.63): $\delta V^\rho = (\delta a)(\delta b) A^\nu B^\mu R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} V^\sigma$.
- Ricci tensor: contraction of first and third indices, eq. (3.90), PDF 88: $R_{\mu\nu} = R^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}$; symmetric, eq. (3.91). Ricci scalar eq. (3.92): $R = R^{\mu}{}_{\mu} = g^{\mu\nu}R_{\mu\nu}$. With these choices the 2-sphere has $R = 2/a^2 > 0$, eq. (3.105), PDF 92.
- Hilbert action: $S_H = \int d^n x \sqrt{-g}\,R$ with NO prefactor, eq. (4.54)-(4.55), PDF 121. The total action is $S = \frac{1}{8\pi G}S_H + S_M$, eq. (4.68), PDF 123 (also eq. (5.34), PDF 145). Note: the constant is $1/(8\pi G)$, not $1/(16\pi G)$; this is compensated by Carroll's definition of $T_{\mu\nu}$ without a factor 2 (see Caveats). Sign: positive $+R$ with $(-,+,+,+)$.
- Variations are taken with respect to the inverse metric $g^{\mu\nu}$ (PDF 121).
- Einstein's equations: $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\, T_{\mu\nu}$, eq. (4.52), PDF 119.

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) Riemann symmetries, Bianchi identities (Chapter 3, PDF 85-88, printed 78-81)

- Coordinate-free forms, PDF 83-84: torsion eq. (3.70) $T(X,Y) = \nabla_X Y - \nabla_Y X - [X,Y]$; curvature eq. (3.71) $R(X,Y)Z = \nabla_X\nabla_Y Z - \nabla_Y\nabla_X Z - \nabla_{[X,Y]}Z$, with $X,Y$ corresponding to the antisymmetric pair $\mu\nu$ of (3.67).
- General commutator on a tensor, eq. (3.68), PDF 83: $[\nabla_\rho,\nabla_\sigma]X^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_k}{}_{\nu_1\cdots\nu_l} = -T_{\rho\sigma}{}^{\lambda}\nabla_\lambda X^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_k}{}_{\nu_1\cdots\nu_l} + R^{\mu_1}{}_{\lambda\rho\sigma}X^{\lambda\mu_2\cdots\mu_k}{}_{\nu_1\cdots\nu_l} + \cdots - R^{\lambda}{}_{\nu_1\rho\sigma}X^{\mu_1\cdots\mu_k}{}_{\lambda\nu_2\cdots\nu_l} - \cdots$.
- Lowered tensor, eq. (3.76): $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = g_{\rho\lambda}R^{\lambda}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$. In Riemann normal coordinates, eq. (3.77):
  $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\left(\partial_\mu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\nu} - \partial_\mu\partial_\rho g_{\nu\sigma} - \partial_\nu\partial_\sigma g_{\rho\mu} + \partial_\nu\partial_\rho g_{\mu\sigma}\right)$.
- Symmetries: eq. (3.64) $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu} = -R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\nu\mu}$; eq. (3.78) $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = -R_{\sigma\rho\mu\nu}$; eq. (3.79) $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$; eq. (3.80) $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + R_{\rho\mu\nu\sigma} + R_{\rho\nu\sigma\mu} = 0$, equivalently eq. (3.81) $R_{\rho[\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$; consequence eq. (3.83) $R_{[\rho\sigma\mu\nu]} = 0$. Carroll states (3.64), (3.78), (3.81) together imply (3.79).
- Number of independent components, eq. (3.85): $\tfrac18(n^4 - 2n^3 + 3n^2 - 2n) - \tfrac{1}{24}n(n-1)(n-2)(n-3) = \tfrac{1}{12}n^2(n^2-1)$; i.e. 0, 1, 6, 20 in $n = 1,2,3,4$ (PDF 87, 89).
- Bianchi identity, eq. (3.87)-(3.88), PDF 87-88: $\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu} = 0$, i.e. $\nabla_{[\lambda}R_{\rho\sigma]\mu\nu} = 0$. Related to the Jacobi identity eq. (3.89): $[[\nabla_\lambda,\nabla_\rho],\nabla_\sigma] + [[\nabla_\rho,\nabla_\sigma],\nabla_\lambda] + [[\nabla_\sigma,\nabla_\lambda],\nabla_\rho] = 0$. (For a general connection there would be extra torsion terms.)
- Twice-contracted Bianchi identity, eq. (3.93)-(3.94): $0 = g^{\nu\sigma}g^{\mu\lambda}(\nabla_\lambda R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} + \nabla_\rho R_{\sigma\lambda\mu\nu} + \nabla_\sigma R_{\lambda\rho\mu\nu}) = \nabla^\mu R_{\rho\mu} - \nabla_\rho R + \nabla^\nu R_{\rho\nu}$, hence $\nabla^\mu R_{\rho\mu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\rho R$.
- Einstein tensor eq. (3.95): $G_{\mu\nu} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu}$; contracted Bianchi eq. (3.96): $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
- Weyl tensor in $n$ dimensions, eq. (3.97), PDF 89: $C_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} - \frac{2}{(n-2)}\left(g_{\rho[\mu}R_{\nu]\sigma} - g_{\sigma[\mu}R_{\nu]\rho}\right) + \frac{2}{(n-1)(n-2)}R\, g_{\rho[\mu}g_{\nu]\sigma}$; defined only for $n \ge 3$, vanishes identically for $n = 3$, conformally invariant.

## (b) Hilbert action and its variation (Chapter 4, PDF 121-123, printed 114-116)

- Eq. (4.54) $S_H = \int d^n x\, \mathcal{L}_H$, eq. (4.55) $\mathcal{L}_H = \sqrt{-g}\,R$. Justification: $R$ is the only independent scalar built from the metric with no more than two derivatives.
- Eq. (4.56): $\delta S = \int d^n x\left[\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\delta R_{\mu\nu} + \sqrt{-g}\,R_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu} + R\,\delta\sqrt{-g}\right] = (\delta S)_1 + (\delta S)_2 + (\delta S)_3$.
- Eq. (4.57) (Ricci as contraction): $R^{\rho}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu} = \partial_\lambda\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\sigma}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu\mu} - (\lambda \leftrightarrow \nu)$.
- Eq. (4.58) $\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu} \to \Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu} + \delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}$; $\delta\Gamma$ is a difference of connections, hence a tensor; eq. (4.59) $\nabla_\lambda(\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}) = \partial_\lambda(\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}) + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\sigma}\delta\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu\mu} - \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\lambda\nu}\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\sigma\mu} - \Gamma^{\sigma}_{\lambda\mu}\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\sigma}$.
- Palatini identity (Carroll does not use that name; "Palatini" does not occur in the notes), eq. (4.60): $\delta R^{\rho}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu} = \nabla_\lambda(\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu}) - \nabla_\nu(\delta\Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\mu})$.
- Eq. (4.61): $(\delta S)_1 = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}\,g^{\mu\nu}\left[\nabla_\lambda(\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\nu\mu}) - \nabla_\nu(\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\lambda\mu})\right] = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}\,\nabla_\sigma\left[g^{\mu\sigma}(\delta\Gamma^{\lambda}_{\lambda\mu}) - g^{\mu\nu}(\delta\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\mu\nu})\right]$. Boundary remark (PDF 122): a covariant divergence integrated with the natural volume element is, by Stokes's theorem, "a boundary contribution at infinity which we can set to zero by making the variation vanish at infinity. ... Therefore this term contributes nothing to the total variation." No Gibbons-Hawking-York term is mentioned.
- Determinant variation: eq. (4.62) $\mathrm{Tr}(\ln M) = \ln(\det M)$; eq. (4.63) $\mathrm{Tr}(M^{-1}\delta M) = \frac{1}{\det M}\delta(\det M)$; applied to $M = g^{\mu\nu}$, eq. (4.64) $\delta(g^{-1}) = \frac{1}{g}g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$; eq. (4.65), PDF 123:
  $\delta\sqrt{-g} = \delta[(-g^{-1})^{-1/2}] = -\tfrac12(-g^{-1})^{-3/2}\delta(-g^{-1}) = -\tfrac12\sqrt{-g}\,g_{\mu\nu}\delta g^{\mu\nu}$.
- Eq. (4.66) $\delta S = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}\left[R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu}\right]\delta g^{\mu\nu}$; vacuum equations eq. (4.67): $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu} = 0$.
- With matter, eq. (4.68) $S = \frac{1}{8\pi G}S_H + S_M$; eq. (4.69) $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = \frac{1}{8\pi G}\left(R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu}\right) + \frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}} = 0$; definition eq. (4.70): $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$, called "the best way to define a symmetric energy-momentum tensor"; contrasted with the canonical Noether tensor eq. (4.71) $S^{\mu\nu} = \frac{\delta\mathcal{L}}{\delta(\partial_\mu\psi^i)}\partial^\nu\psi^i - \eta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}$ (PDF 124).
- Conservation of (4.70) from diffeomorphism invariance: eqs. (5.35)-(5.38), PDF 146: $\delta g_{\mu\nu} = \mathcal{L}_V g_{\mu\nu} = 2\nabla_{(\mu}V_{\nu)}$ gives $0 = -\int d^n x\sqrt{-g}\,V_\nu\nabla_\mu\left(\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g_{\mu\nu}}\right)$, hence $\nabla_\mu T^{\mu\nu} = 0$ (5.38).
- Cosmological constant, PDF 125 (printed 118): eq. (4.73) $S = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}(R - 2\Lambda)$; eq. (4.74) $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu} + \Lambda g_{\mu\nu} = 0$. Moving the term to the right gives $T_{\mu\nu} = -\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ (text, PDF 125; automatically conserved by metric compatibility). With $G$ restored, eq. (8.29), PDF 229: $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu} - \Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$, with $T^{(\mathrm{vac})}_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{\Lambda}{8\pi G}g_{\mu\nu}$ (8.30), a perfect fluid with $\rho = -p = \Lambda/8\pi G$ (8.31). Naturalness estimate eq. (4.75) $\Lambda \sim m_P^4$ vs. observed bound $10^{120}$ smaller (PDF 126).
- Higher-derivative and scalar-tensor alternatives: eq. (4.76) $S = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}(R + \alpha_1 R^2 + \alpha_2 R_{\mu\nu}R^{\mu\nu} + \alpha_3 g^{\mu\nu}\nabla_\mu R\nabla_\nu R + \cdots)$, PDF 126; eq. (4.77) $S = \int d^n x\sqrt{-g}\left[f(\lambda)R + \tfrac12 g^{\mu\nu}(\partial_\mu\lambda)(\partial_\nu\lambda) - V(\lambda)\right]$, PDF 127 (Brans-Dicke; coefficient of $R$ "proportional to the inverse of Newton's constant"). Palatini-type remark (PDF 127-128): varying the action w.r.t. an independent torsion-free connection forces it to be the Christoffel connection (left as an exercise).
- Informal route (PDF 116-119): Poisson eq. (4.35) $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$; target eq. (4.36) $\nabla^2 h_{00} = -8\pi G T_{00}$; first guess eq. (4.37) $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ fails because (4.38) $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ plus (4.40) $\nabla^\mu R_{\mu\nu} = \tfrac12\nabla_\nu R$ would force $\nabla_\mu T = 0$ (4.41); fix: eq. (4.43) $G_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$; trace in four dimensions eq. (4.44) $R = -\kappa T$; trace-reversed form eq. (4.45) $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa\left(T_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 T g_{\mu\nu}\right)$; vacuum eq. (4.53) $R_{\mu\nu} = 0$.

## (c) Maximally symmetric spaces

- Curvature form, eq. (3.106), PDF 92 (printed 85), stated "in any number of dimensions ... for some constant $a$":
  $R_{\rho\sigma\mu\nu} = a^{-2}\left(g_{\rho\mu}g_{\sigma\nu} - g_{\rho\nu}g_{\sigma\mu}\right)$.
  The form $R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = \frac{R}{n(n-1)}(g_{\mu\rho}g_{\nu\sigma} - g_{\mu\sigma}g_{\nu\rho})$ is NOT written in this notation; contracting (3.106) with Carroll's conventions gives $R_{\sigma\nu} = (n-1)a^{-2}g_{\sigma\nu}$ and $R = n(n-1)a^{-2}$, but these $n$-dimensional contractions are not displayed in the notes. For the 2-sphere of radius $a$: $R = 2/a^2$, eq. (3.105).
- Einstein-space statement: only in the 3-dimensional cosmological case, eqs. (8.2)-(8.3), PDF 225 (printed 218): ${}^{(3)}R_{ijkl} = k(\gamma_{ik}\gamma_{jl} - \gamma_{il}\gamma_{jk})$, ${}^{(3)}R_{jl} = 2k\gamma_{jl}$. The phrase "Einstein space" does not occur in the notes.
- Definition and Killing-vector count, PDF 147-148 (printed 140-141): "a maximally symmetric space is one which possesses the largest possible number of Killing vectors, which on an $n$-dimensional manifold is $n(n+1)/2$"; eq. (5.44): $n + \frac{n(n-1)}{2} = \frac{n(n+1)}{2}$ (translations plus rotations of $\mathbf{R}^n$; "we will not prove this statement"). Killing's equation eq. (5.41) $\mathcal{L}_V g_{\mu\nu} = 0$, eq. (5.42) $\nabla_{(\mu}V_{\nu)} = 0$; Lie derivative of the metric eq. (5.33) $\mathcal{L}_V g_{\mu\nu} = 2\nabla_{(\mu}V_{\nu)}$ (PDF 145).
- de Sitter and anti-de Sitter, PDF 234-235 (printed 227-228), in Robertson-Walker coordinates eq. (8.7), PDF 226: $ds^2 = -dt^2 + a^2(t)\left[\frac{dr^2}{1-kr^2} + r^2(d\theta^2 + \sin^2\theta\, d\phi^2)\right]$, for universes "empty save for the cosmological constant":
  eq. (8.56) $a = \sqrt{-3/\Lambda}\,\sin\!\left(\sqrt{-\Lambda/3}\,t\right)$ ($\Lambda < 0$, $k = -1$); eq. (8.57) $a = \sqrt{3/\Lambda}\,\sinh\!\left(\sqrt{\Lambda/3}\,t\right)$ ($k = -1$); eq. (8.58) $a \propto \exp\!\left(\pm\sqrt{\Lambda/3}\,t\right)$ ($k = 0$); eq. (8.59) $a = \sqrt{3/\Lambda}\,\cosh\!\left(\sqrt{\Lambda/3}\,t\right)$ ($k = +1$).
  Text (PDF 235): the three $\Lambda > 0$ solutions "all represent the same spacetime, just in different coordinates. This spacetime, known as de Sitter space, is actually maximally symmetric as a spacetime. (See Hawking and Ellis for details.) The $\Lambda < 0$ solution (8.56) is also maximally symmetric, and is known as anti-de Sitter space." The explicit relation between $\Lambda$ and the curvature radius $a$ of (3.106) is not written; it follows from (8.58) via $H^2 = \Lambda/3$ (Friedmann eq. (8.36) with $\rho = \Lambda/8\pi G$, $k = 0$).
- Killing vectors give conserved quantities along geodesics, eq. (5.43), PDF 147: $U^\nu\nabla_\nu(V_\mu U^\mu) = U^\nu U^\mu\nabla_\nu V_\mu + V_\mu U^\nu\nabla_\nu U^\mu = 0$; Carroll also states (PDF 148, without proof) that constant-coefficient combinations and commutators of Killing vectors are Killing vectors.
- Related: the Einstein static universe $\mathbf{R}\times S^3$, eq. (7.102), PDF 202 (printed 195): $d\bar{s}^2 = -d\eta^2 + d\chi^2 + \sin^2\chi\, d\Omega^2$, "a static (but unstable) solution to Einstein's equations with a perfect fluid and a cosmological constant"; the $S^3$ factor is called maximally symmetric.

## (d) Newtonian limit and the identification of $G$

- Weak-field setup, PDF 112 (printed 105): eq. (4.13) $g_{\mu\nu} = \eta_{\mu\nu} + h_{\mu\nu}$, $|h_{\mu\nu}| \ll 1$; eq. (4.14) $g^{\mu\nu} = \eta^{\mu\nu} - h^{\mu\nu}$; static slow-motion Christoffel eq. (4.15) $\Gamma^{\mu}_{00} = -\tfrac12\eta^{\mu\lambda}\partial_\lambda h_{00}$; geodesic equation reduces to eq. (4.19) $\frac{d^2x^i}{dt^2} = \tfrac12\partial_i h_{00}$.
- Eq. (4.21), PDF 113 (printed 106): $g_{00} = -(1 + 2\Phi)$ (from $h_{00} = -2\Phi$, eq. (4.20)), with eq. (4.22) $\Phi = -GM/r$.
- Newtonian-limit field equation, PDF 118: $R_{00} = R^{i}{}_{0i0} = \partial_i\Gamma^{i}_{00} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2 h_{00}$, eqs. (4.49)-(4.50), using $T = g^{00}T_{00} = -T_{00}$ (4.47) to lowest order.
- Normalization of the field equation, PDF 118-119 (printed 111-112): eqs. (4.46)-(4.51) give $R_{00} = \tfrac12\kappa T_{00}$ (4.48) and $R_{00} = -\tfrac12\nabla^2 h_{00}$ (4.50), so $\nabla^2 h_{00} = -\kappa T_{00}$ (4.51); comparing with (4.36) fixes $\kappa = 8\pi G$ (text below (4.51)). Result eq. (4.52): $R_{\mu\nu} - \tfrac12 R g_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G\,T_{\mu\nu}$.

# Attributable statements

1. "There is no agreement at all on what this convention should be, so be careful" (index ordering of the Riemann tensor): Sec. 3, PDF 82 (printed 75).
2. The Riemann tensor is built entirely from the connection, with no metric, and (3.67) holds for any connection, metric compatible or not, torsion free or not: Sec. 3, PDF 83 (printed 76).
3. The Riemann tensor has $n^2(n^2-1)/12$ independent components (0, 1, 6, 20 for $n = 1..4$): Sec. 3, eq. (3.85), PDF 87 and 89.
4. The Bianchi identity $\nabla_{[\lambda}R_{\rho\sigma]\mu\nu} = 0$ "basically expresses" the Jacobi identity for covariant derivatives: Sec. 3, eqs. (3.88)-(3.89), PDF 88.
5. The twice-contracted Bianchi identity is equivalent to $\nabla^\mu G_{\mu\nu} = 0$: Sec. 3, eqs. (3.94)-(3.96), PDF 88 (printed 81).
6. $R_{\mu\nu} = \kappa T_{\mu\nu}$ is inconsistent with $\nabla^\mu T_{\mu\nu} = 0$ because it forces $T$ to be constant; the Einstein tensor is the symmetric, automatically conserved (0,2) tensor that fixes this: Sec. 4, PDF 117 (printed 110).
7. Of the ten components of Einstein's equations only six are independent because of the four Bianchi identities, matching the four coordinate degrees of freedom: Sec. 4, PDF 119 (printed 112).
8. "The equations were first derived by Hilbert, not Einstein ... The action, however, is rightly called the Hilbert action": Sec. 4, PDF 121 (printed 114).
9. The Ricci scalar is the only independent scalar built from the metric with no more than second derivatives, hence the simplest Lagrangian: Sec. 4, PDF 121 (printed 114).
10. $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S_M}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$ is "the best way to define a symmetric energy-momentum tensor", and its conservation follows from diffeomorphism invariance, not merely from the equivalence principle: Sec. 4, PDF 123-124; Sec. 5, PDF 146 (printed 139).
11. A maximally symmetric $n$-manifold has $n(n+1)/2$ Killing vectors (unproven counting argument); de Sitter and anti-de Sitter are maximally symmetric spacetimes: Sec. 5, PDF 147-148; Sec. 8, PDF 235.

# Caveats

- Normalization of the gravitational action: Carroll uses $S = \frac{1}{8\pi G}\int\sqrt{-g}R + S_M$ with $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\delta S_M/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (eqs. (4.68), (4.70)), and himself says "the proper normalization is somewhat convention-dependent". The more common pair is $\frac{1}{16\pi G}\int\sqrt{-g}R$ with $T_{\mu\nu} = -\frac{2}{\sqrt{-g}}\delta S_M/\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ (e.g. Carroll's later textbook Spacetime and Geometry, 2004, and most Kaluza-Klein papers). Both give $G_{\mu\nu} = 8\pi G T_{\mu\nu}$, but with Carroll-1997's definition a canonically normalized matter action yields a $T_{\mu\nu}$ that is half the conventional one; a tutorial quoting (4.70) must pair it with (4.68) consistently, or switch to the $1/16\pi G$ convention explicitly.
- The cosmological-constant action (4.73) $\int\sqrt{-g}(R - 2\Lambda)$ is written without the $1/8\pi G$ prefactor; with it, $\Lambda$ enters as $-\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ on the right of (8.29) and as vacuum energy $\rho_{\mathrm{vac}} = \Lambda/8\pi G$.
- Signature: $(-,+,+,+)$ throughout. Under $(+,-,-,-)$ (Weinberg, Landau-Lifshitz, many particle-physics and KK papers) $g_{\mu\nu}$, $R$ (with the same Riemann convention), $T_{\mu\nu}$ and the sign of $\Lambda g_{\mu\nu}$ terms change; Carroll only warns generically that field theory books use the opposite sign (PDF 11).
- Riemann sign/ordering: Carroll's $R^{\rho}{}_{\sigma\mu\nu}$ with $R_{\mu\nu} = R^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}$ agrees with MTW/Wald and gives positive $R$ on spheres; Weinberg's Riemann tensor has the opposite sign. The de Sitter expression in (3.106) assumes these conventions.
- Boundary terms: the variation drops $(\delta S)_1$ by Stokes's theorem assuming variations vanish at infinity (PDF 122); because $\delta\Gamma$ involves $\partial\delta g$, this needs the normal derivative of $\delta g$ to vanish too. The Gibbons-Hawking-York boundary term is not discussed; "Palatini" and "Gibbons" do not appear in the notes.
- Variations are with respect to $g^{\mu\nu}$; translating to $\delta g_{\mu\nu}$ flips the sign of (4.70) ($\delta g^{\mu\nu} = -g^{\mu\alpha}g^{\nu\beta}\delta g_{\alpha\beta}$), which Carroll uses implicitly in (5.37).
- Maximally symmetric spaces: (3.106) is stated without proof and the statement "$n(n+1)/2$ Killing vectors" is explicitly unproven; the relation $a^{-2} \leftrightarrow \Lambda$ for de Sitter is never written in closed form, nor is the $n$-dimensional Einstein-space contraction. The 4-dimensional de Sitter solutions are presented only as FRW scale factors (8.56)-(8.59), not as the hyperboloid embedding or static patch.
- Dimension: most formulas (Hilbert action, variation, Weyl tensor, (3.106)) are written for general $n$, but the trace $R = -\kappa T$ (4.44) and the Newtonian-limit normalization $\kappa = 8\pi G$ are explicitly four-dimensional; in $n$ dimensions the trace reads $R = -\frac{2\kappa}{n-2}T$, which is not in the notes.
- Index placement: chapter 4 restates the Riemann tensor as (4.57) $R^{\rho}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu} = \partial_\lambda\Gamma^{\rho}_{\nu\mu} + \Gamma^{\rho}_{\lambda\sigma}\Gamma^{\sigma}_{\nu\mu} - (\lambda\leftrightarrow\nu)$, with index names permuted relative to (3.67); it is the same tensor and the same Ricci contraction $R_{\mu\nu} = R^{\lambda}{}_{\mu\lambda\nu}$.
- The variation $\delta S$ in (4.56) is written for the Lagrangian $\sqrt{-g}R$ alone; in (4.66)-(4.67) the "equation of motion" $\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\frac{\delta S}{\delta g^{\mu\nu}}$ is a functional derivative, not an ordinary one, and no symmetrization over $(\mu\nu)$ is written because $\delta g^{\mu\nu}$ is symmetric.
- Pedagogical status: these are lecture notes, "shamelessly stolen" (preface, PDF 4) from Schutz, Wald, Weinberg and MTW; results such as (3.106), the Killing-vector count and the uniqueness of $R$ as the second-order scalar are asserted, not proved. Cite them as a textbook-level source, not as original work.
