# Conventions

- Signature: not stated explicitly. The IIB Einstein-frame action (2.1) is written with $\sqrt{-g}$ and $R - \partial S\partial\bar S/2(\mathrm{Im}\,S)^2 - \ldots$, i.e. implicitly mostly-plus Lorentzian; the 3d examples are written as Euclidean internal metrics $ds^2$. Everything is "to lowest order in $g_s$ and $\alpha'$" (printed p.2).
- IIB notation (printed p.3-4): $S = C_0 + i e^{-\phi} = C_0 + i/g_s$, $G_3 = F_3 - S H_3$, $F_3 = dC_2$, $H_3 = dB_2$, $\tilde F_5 = dC_4 - \tfrac12 C_2\wedge H_3 + \tfrac12 F_3\wedge B_2$, $d\tilde F_5 = H_3\wedge F_3$. Orientifold by $\Omega\,\mathbb{Z}_2 (-1)^{F_L}$ with $\mathbb Z_2: x^i\to -x^i$, O3-planes; $T^6 = T^2\times T^2\times T^2$, all tori identical; each $T^2$ has one Greek and one Latin coordinate $(\alpha,i),(\beta,j),(\gamma,k)$; $z^1 = x^\alpha + \tau x^i$. Moduli: axiodilaton $S$, complex structure $\tau$, Kähler modulus $U$. $\bar F_{abc}$ = component of $F_3$ integrated over the $abc$-cycle (even integers by quantization); only one representative flux per index pattern is written (e.g. $\bar F_{ij\gamma} = \bar F_{jk\alpha} = \bar F_{ki\beta}$).
- T^3 with H-flux example (Sec. 4.1, printed p.12-13): coordinates $(x,y,z)$ on $T^3$, each of period 1; $N$ units of $H$-flux with $\int_{T^3} H_3 = N$ (4.11), prefactor $1/(2\pi)^2\alpha'$ set to 1; gauge $B_{xy} = Nz$, $N\in\mathbb Z$. Kähler modulus of the $(x,y)$ $T^2$: $\rho = (\int B) + iV$; complex structure $\tau$. In Sec. 6, $\rho = B + iV$ and for a rectangular torus $\tau = iL_1/L_2$, $\rho = iL_1L_2$ (footnote 4).
- Flux definitions as printed: $H_{abc}$ is NS-NS 3-form flux ($dH = 0$ in IIB on the plain torus); geometric/metric flux $f^a_{bc}$ defined by $de^a = f^a_{bc}\, e^b\wedge e^c$ (3.7) for globally defined one-forms $e^a$ with constant $f^a_{bc}$ (components of the spin connection / Lie-algebra structure constants); nongeometric flux $Q^{ab}_c$ (locally geometric, globally patched by $SL(2,\mathbb Z)\subset O(2,2;\mathbb Z)$; "behaves like a one-form", ref. to Sec. 3 of [28]); $R^{abc}$ (not even locally geometric). Defined operationally via the chain (4.17).
- Buscher rules as printed (4.7)-(4.10), T-dualizing along $x$, with the proviso that an isometry in $x$ is required:
  $G'_{xx} = \dfrac{1}{G_{xx}},\quad G'_{x\mu} = -\dfrac{B_{x\mu}}{G_{xx}},\quad B'_{x\mu} = -\dfrac{G_{x\mu}}{G_{xx}}$ (4.7);
  $G'_{\mu\nu} = G_{\mu\nu} - \dfrac{G_{x\mu}G_{x\nu} - B_{x\mu}B_{x\nu}}{G_{xx}}$ (4.8);
  $B'_{\mu\nu} = B_{\mu\nu} - \dfrac{G_{x\mu}B_{x\nu} - B_{x\mu}G_{x\nu}}{G_{xx}}$ (4.9);
  $e^{\phi'} = \dfrac{e^{\phi}}{\sqrt{G_{xx}}}$ (4.10) (dilaton shift is one-loop, not classical).
  Derivation sketch: circle action $S = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2z\,\partial\theta\bar\partial\theta\,G_{\theta\theta}$ (4.4); Lagrange multiplier form $S = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2z\,[G_{\theta\theta}L\bar L + \tilde\theta(\partial\bar L - \bar\partial L)]$ (4.5); integrating out $L$ gives $S = \frac{1}{2\pi}\int d^2z\,\partial\tilde\theta\bar\partial\tilde\theta\,\frac{1}{G_{\theta\theta}}$ (4.6).
- R-R T-duality rule (4.3): $\bar F_{x\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_p} \xleftrightarrow{T_x} \bar F_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_p}$ (only the integrated topological parts are kept).

# Equations the tutorial uses

(a) T^3 with H-flux → twisted torus → T-fold (Sec. 4.1, printed p.13-14; PDF 14-15).
- Start: flat $T^3$, $B_{xy} = Nz$, $\int_{T^3}H_3 = N$ (4.11). Viewed as $T^2_{(x,y)}$ fibered over $S^1_z$ with $\rho\to\rho+N$ as $z\to z+1$. (The paper notes this flat $T^3$ with $H$ does not solve the string equations of motion; it is illustrative only.)
- $T_x$: $ds^2 = (dx - Nz\,dy)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2,\qquad B = 0$ (4.12). This is the twisted-torus metric (3.1) with $f^x_{yz} = N$; global well-definedness requires $(x,y,z)\sim(x+Ny,y,z+1)$; fiber complex structure $\tau\to\tau+N$ as $z\to z+1$. Rule: $H_{xyz}\xrightarrow{T_x} f^x_{yz}$ (4.13).
- $T_y$: $ds^2 = \dfrac{1}{1+N^2z^2}(dx^2+dy^2) + dz^2,\qquad B_{xy} = \dfrac{Nz}{1+N^2z^2}$ (4.14), with $\dfrac{1}{\rho} = Nz - i$ (4.15), so $z\to z+1$ acts as $1/\rho\to 1/\rho + N$: an $SL(2,\mathbb Z)\subset O(2,2;\mathbb Z)$ mixing metric and $B$. Chain so far: $H_{xyz}\xrightarrow{T_x} f^x_{yz}\xrightarrow{T_y} Q^{xy}_z$ (4.16). Footnote 2: the Killing vector $\partial_y$ is no longer globally well-defined after the first duality; no rigorous argument exists for the second duality (see [47], or work on the covering space).
- Twisted-torus model (Sec. 3.1, printed p.8): $ds^2 = (dx - f^x_{yz}\,z\,dy)^2 + dy^2 + dz^2$ (3.1); identifications $(x,y,z)\sim(x+1,y,z)\sim(x,y+1,z)\sim(x+f^x_{yz}y,y,z+1)$ (3.2); $f^x_{yz}\in\mathbb Z$ required for the fiber monodromy $\tau\to\tau+f^x_{yz}$ to be in $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$. Invariant one-forms $\eta^x = dx - f^x_{yz}\,z\,dy$ (3.3), $\eta^y = dy$ (3.4), $\eta^z = dz$ (3.5); $d\eta^x = f^x_{yz}\,dy\wedge dz = f^x_{yz}\,\eta^y\wedge\eta^z$ (3.6).

(b) Full chain and duality rules (printed p.14): $H_{xyz}\xrightarrow{T_x} f^x_{yz}\xrightarrow{T_y} Q^{xy}_z\xrightarrow{T_z} R^{xyz}$ (4.17). The third duality has no isometry; its existence is argued from the duality-invariant superpotential (Sec. 5) and from the doubled formalism [35]. $R$-flux argument: a D3 wrapped on $T^3$ with $H$ would map to a D0 in $R$-flux, but the D3 cannot exist since $dF_2 = H_3\ne 0$; hence no notion of points, i.e. not even locally geometric (nonassociative geometry per [49]). The paper gives no general index-by-index Buscher rule for $f\to Q\to R$ beyond (4.13), (4.16), (4.17) and the R-R rule (4.3).

(c) Superpotentials (all with Kähler potential $K = -3\ln(-i(\tau-\bar\tau)) - 3\ln(-i(U-\bar U)) - \ln(-i(S-\bar S))$ (2.3) and $V = e^K(\sum K^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW} - 3|W|^2)$ (2.5)).
- IIB, GVW: $W = \int G_3\wedge\Omega = \int(F_3 - SH_3)\wedge\Omega$ (2.4), $\Omega = dz^1\wedge dz^2\wedge dz^3$ expanded in (2.6); $W = P_1(\tau) + SP_2(\tau)$ (2.7); explicitly
  $W = a_0 - 3a_1\tau + 3a_2\tau^2 - a_3\tau^3 + S(-b_0 + 3b_1\tau - 3b_2\tau^2 + b_3\tau^3)$ (2.8), Table 2: $a_0\leftrightarrow\bar F_{ijk}$, $a_1\leftrightarrow\bar F_{ij\gamma}$, $a_2\leftrightarrow\bar F_{i\beta\gamma}$, $a_3\leftrightarrow\bar F_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$, $b_0\leftrightarrow\bar H_{ijk}$, $b_1\leftrightarrow\bar H_{\alpha jk}$, $b_2\leftrightarrow\bar H_{i\beta\gamma}$, $b_3\leftrightarrow\bar H_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$. No $U$ dependence (no-scale), $V\ge 0$ (2.9); tadpole $\frac{1}{2\kappa_{10}^2T_3}\int H_3\wedge F_3 = 16$ (2.10) i.e. $a_0b_3 - 3a_1b_2 + 3a_2b_1 - a_3b_0 = 16$ (2.11).
- IIA on twisted $T^6$ with O6 (after $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$): $W = P_1(\tau) + SP_2(\tau) + UP_3(\tau)$ (3.9), $P_1$ cubic, $P_{2,3}$ linear:
  $W = a_0 - 3a_1\tau + 3a_2\tau^2 - a_3\tau^3 + S(-b_0 + 3b_1\tau) + 3U(c_0 + (\hat c_1 + \check c_1 + \tilde c_1)\tau)$ (3.10), Table 4: $a_0\leftrightarrow\bar F_{\alpha i\beta j\gamma k}$, $a_1\leftrightarrow\bar F_{\alpha i\beta j}$, $a_2\leftrightarrow\bar F_{\alpha i}$, $a_3\leftrightarrow F^{(0)}$, $b_0\leftrightarrow\bar H_{ijk}$, $b_1\leftrightarrow f^\alpha_{jk}$, $c_0\leftrightarrow\bar H_{\alpha\beta k}$, $\check c_1,\hat c_1,\tilde c_1\leftrightarrow f^j_{k\alpha}, f^i_{\beta k}, f^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}$. Only $f$'s with an odd number of Greek indices survive the orientifold: $f^\alpha_{jk}, f^i_{\beta k}, f^i_{j\gamma}, f^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}$. All moduli appear.
- Duality-invariant (Sec. 5, printed p.15-16; Table 5): $W = P_1(\tau) + SP_2(\tau) + UP_3(\tau)$ (5.1), all cubic:
  $W = a_0 - 3a_1\tau + 3a_2\tau^2 - a_3\tau^3 + S(-b_0 + 3b_1\tau - 3b_2\tau^2 + b_3\tau^3) + 3U(c_0 + (\hat c_1+\check c_1+\tilde c_1)\tau - (\hat c_2+\check c_2+\tilde c_2)\tau^2 - c_3\tau^3)$ (5.2).
  Table 5 (Term | IIA flux | IIB flux | integer): $1|\bar F_{\alpha i\beta j\gamma k}|\bar F_{ijk}|a_0$; $\tau|\bar F_{\alpha i\beta j}|\bar F_{ij\gamma}|a_1$; $\tau^2|\bar F_{\alpha i}|\bar F_{i\beta\gamma}|a_2$; $\tau^3|F^{(0)}|\bar F_{\alpha\beta\gamma}|a_3$; $S|\bar H_{ijk}|\bar H_{ijk}|b_0$; $U|\bar H_{\alpha\beta k}|Q^{\alpha\beta}_k|c_0$; $S\tau|f^\alpha_{jk}|\bar H_{\alpha jk}|b_1$; $U\tau|f^j_{k\alpha},f^i_{\beta k},f^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}|Q^{\alpha j}_k,Q^{i\beta}_k,Q^{\beta\gamma}_\alpha|\check c_1,\hat c_1,\tilde c_1$; $S\tau^2|Q^{\alpha\beta}_k|\bar H_{i\beta\gamma}|b_2$; $U\tau^2|Q^{\gamma i}_\beta,Q^{\gamma j}_\gamma,Q^{ij}_k|Q^{i\beta}_\gamma,Q^{\gamma i}_\beta,Q^{ij}_k|\check c_2,\hat c_2,\tilde c_2$; $S\tau^3|R^{\alpha\beta\gamma}|\bar H_{\alpha\beta\gamma}|b_3$; $U\tau^3|R^{ij\gamma}|Q^{ij}_\gamma|c_3$. (Index placement in the $U\tau^2$ row transcribed as printed; the second IIA entry reads $Q^{\gamma j}_\gamma$ in the PDF, possibly a typo for $Q^{\gamma j}_\beta$-type, flagged in Caveats.) Footnote 3: $\hat c_1 = \check c_1$, $\hat c_2 = \check c_2$, so 14 independent fluxes under 7 distinct constraints.
  Derivation: (1) $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ to IIB/O3; (2) use Latin/Greek rotational symmetry $\alpha\leftrightarrow i$, which exchanges $1\leftrightarrow\tau^3$, $\tau\leftrightarrow\tau^2$ in $P_{1,2,3}$; (3) $T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}$ back to IIA.
  SUSY equations $D_iW = 0$ (5.3): $P_1 + \bar S P_2 + UP_3 = 0$; $P_1 + SP_2 + \tfrac13(2U+\bar U)P_3 = 0$; $(\tau-\bar\tau)\partial_\tau W - W = 0$. Consequence: geometric IIA ($P_2$ linear) with $W = 0$ forces $\tau\in\mathbb R$ (zero volume), so IIA SUSY Minkowski vacua must be nongeometric (agrees with no-go [48]). Cosmological constant $\Lambda = -3e^K|W|^2$, $g_s = 1/\mathrm{Im}\,S$ (p.19). No scalar-potential-per-flux formula is given; only $W$ and (2.5).

(d) Constraints (Sec. 5.1, printed p.16-18; PDF 17-19).
- Twisted torus: $d^2e^a = 2f^a_{b[c}f^b_{de]}e^d e^c = 0$ (3.8) ⇒ Jacobi $f^a_{b[c}f^b_{de]} = 0$; $dH = 0$ ⇒ $\bar H_{a[bc}f^a_{de]} = 0$ (p.10); IIA R-R Bianchi $(d+H)\wedge F = 0$ gives $\bar F_{a[b}f^a_{cd]} + F_0\bar H_{bcd} = 0$ (3.11), $\bar F_{x[abc}f^x_{de]} + \bar F_{ab}\bar H_{cde]} = 0$ (3.12). Compactness: $f^a_{ab} = 0$ (no sum) necessary, not sufficient (p.9); nilpotent + rational ⇒ nilmanifold.
- R-R constraints, from $d\tilde F_5 = \bar F_{[abc}\bar H_{def]} = 0$ dualized: (5.5) $\bar F_{[abc}\bar H_{def]} = 0$; (5.6) $\bar F_{x[abc}f^x_{de]} - \bar F_{[ab}\bar H_{cde]} = 0$ (= (5.4)); (5.7) $\bar F_{xy[abc}Q^{xy}_{d]} - 3\bar F_{x[ab}f^x_{cd]} - 2\bar F_{[a}\bar H_{bcd]} = 0$; (5.8) $\bar F_{xyz[abc]}R^{xyz} - 9\bar F_{xy[ab}Q^{xy}_{c]} - 18\bar F_{x[a}f^x_{bc]} + 6F^{(0)}\bar H_{[abc]} = 0$; (5.9) $\bar F_{xyz[ab]}R^{xyz} + 6\bar F_{xy[a}Q^{xy}_{b]} - 6\bar F_{[a}f^x_{ab]} = 0$ (index on $f$ as printed); (5.10) $\bar F_{xyza}R^{xyz} - 3\bar F_{xy}Q^{xy}_a = 0$; (5.11) $\bar F_{xyz}R^{xyz} = 0$. These are $dF_i = 0$, $i = 5$ down to $1$; (5.11) "a bit mysterious, but required by duality"; localized sources would give nonzero RHS.
- NS-NS constraints, from $dH = f^x_{[ab}\bar H_{cd]x} = 0$ dualized: (5.12) $\bar H_{x[ab}f^x_{cd]} = 0$; (5.13) $f^a_{x[b}f^x_{cd]} + \bar H_{x[bc}Q^{ax}_{d]} = 0$; (5.14) $Q^{[ab}_x f^x_{[cd]} - 4f^{[a}_{x[c}Q^{b]x}_{d]} + \bar H_{x[cd]}R^{[ab]x} = 0$; (5.15) $Q^{[ab}_x Q^{c]x}_d + f^{[a}_{xd}R^{bc]x} = 0$; (5.16) $Q^{[ab}_x R^{cd]x} = 0$; plus (5.17) $f^x_{xa} = 0 = Q^{ax}_x$ (compactness / individual T-duality to $H$).
- Gauge-algebra form (p.18): $T^6$ gives $U(1)^{12}$ with $Z_m$ (diffeos) and $X^m$ (B-shifts); with $H,f$ [15,17]: $[Z_a,Z_b] = \bar H_{abc}X^c + f^c_{ab}Z_c$ (5.18), $[Z_a,X^b] = -f^b_{ac}X^c$ (5.19), $[X^a,X^b] = 0$ (5.20); T-duality completion: $[Z_a,Z_b] = \bar H_{abc}X^c + f^c_{ab}Z_c$ (5.21), $[Z_a,X^b] = -f^b_{ac}X^c + Q^{bc}_aZ_c$ (5.22), $[X^a,X^b] = Q^{ab}_cX^c + R^{abc}Z_c$ (5.23).
- Compact form [29]: $\mathcal D \equiv H\wedge + f\cdot + Q\cdot + R\llcorner$ (5.24); R-R: $\mathcal D\bar{\mathcal F} = 0$ (5.25); NS-NS: $\mathcal D^2 = (H\wedge + f\cdot + Q\cdot + R\llcorner)^2 = 0$ (5.26), which also implies $f^x_{xa} = 0 = Q^{xa}_x$.

(e) T-fold / nongeometric (p.14, p.22-23). Background (4.14) is "locally geometric, since the metric and B-field are defined at every point, but it is not globally a manifold. Upon going around a cycle, the metric and B-field mix by an $SL(2,\mathbb Z)\subset O(2,2;\mathbb Z)$ transformation." Hull's definition (p.22-23): $T^n$ fibered over a base with a noncontractible loop, transition functions general elements of $O(n,n;\mathbb Z)$ — "T-folds" or "monodrofolds"; generically nongeometric because the group contains T-dualities (as well as B-shifts and basis changes). Doubled torus $T^{2n}$ with $O(n,n;\mathbb Z)$ acting linearly, projected to $T^n$ by a self-duality constraint; worldsheet Lagrangian $\mathcal L = -\tfrac12\mathcal H_{IJ}(Y)\eta^{\alpha\beta}\partial_\alpha\mathbb X^I\partial_\beta\mathbb X^J - \eta^{\alpha\beta}\mathcal J_{IA}(Y)\partial_\alpha\mathbb X^I\partial_\beta Y^A + \mathcal L_N(Y)$ (6.3), $\mathcal H_{IJ}$ a coset metric for $O(n,n)/O(n)\times O(n)$, $\mathcal H = \begin{pmatrix} G - BG^{-1}B & BG^{-1}\\ -G^{-1}B & G^{-1}\end{pmatrix}$ (6.4), coordinates $\mathbb X^I\to(X^i,\tilde X_i)$. $R$-flux: no local geometric description at all. Asymmetric-orbifold example: $\tau\to-1/\tau$ as $z\to z+1$ (6.1) is geometric and modular invariant; its T-dual $\rho\to-1/\rho$ as $z\to z+1$ (6.2) is nongeometric; $\tau\to-1/\tau$ and $\rho\to-1/\rho$ together is not modular invariant.

(f) Scherk-Schwarz and twisted tori. The paper cites Scherk-Schwarz [15] (Nucl. Phys. B 153, 61 (1979)) and Kaloper-Myers [17] as the origin of twisted tori and of the gauge algebra (5.18)-(5.20): "turning on $H$-flux and geometric flux makes the Lie algebra non-abelian, with the fluxes acting as structure constants" (p.18). The metric flux $f^a_{bc}$ is the Scherk-Schwarz twist: the twisted torus is a $T^2$ fibered over $S^1$ with monodromy $\tau\to\tau+f^x_{yz}$ in $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ (p.8), and $f^a_{bc}$ are the structure constants of the Lie group viewed as a manifold / components of the spin connection via Cartan's structure equations (3.6)-(3.8). The $Q$- and $R$-fluxes are the T-duality completions of this twist, i.e. monodromies in $O(n,n;\mathbb Z)$ rather than $GL(n,\mathbb Z)$. In Sec. 6 (p.22) the shift $z\to z+1$ accompanying $\tau\to-1/\tau$ is said to "induce a Sherk-Schwarz-type potential for the moduli" (sic). The phrase "Scherk-Schwarz reduction" itself is not used beyond these citations; no explicit SS-reduction formula is given.

# Attributable statements

1. "performing one T-duality on a background with H-flux yields a twisted torus compactification, which is characterized by a set of 'geometric fluxes' $f^a_{bc}$ ... Performing another T-duality on a twisted torus yields a locally geometric but globally nongeometric space, which we will characterize by a nongeometric flux $Q^{ab}_c$." (Sec. 1, printed p.2.)
2. The twisted torus is "a $T^2$ in the $(x,y)$ directions fibered over an $S^1$ in the $z$ direction"; the fiber undergoes $\tau\to\tau+f^x_{yz}$, so $f^x_{yz}\in\mathbb Z$ is required for an $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ monodromy. (Sec. 3.1, printed p.8.)
3. "$f^a_{ab} = 0$ (no sum) is a necessary (but not sufficient) condition for compactness"; nilpotent $f$ satisfy it automatically; with rationality one gets nilmanifolds/twisted tori. (Sec. 3.1, printed p.9.)
4. The only $f^a_{bc}$ surviving the IIA O6 orientifold projection have an odd number of Greek indices; with them "all the moduli appear in this IIA superpotential". (Sec. 3.2, printed p.9-10.)
5. The second T-duality on the twisted torus is done without a globally well-defined Killing vector; "there is currently no rigorous argument in the literature". (Footnote 2, printed p.13.)
6. "This is our first example of a nongeometric background, since the transformation $1/\rho\to1/\rho+N$ mixes the metric and the B-field." (Sec. 4.1, printed p.14.)
7. $R$-flux "indicates a lack of even a locally geometric description", argued via D3 on $T^3$ with $H$ ↦ D0 in $R$-flux. (Sec. 4, printed p.14.)
8. "The nongeometric fluxes also show up as structure constants in the four-dimensional gauge group! From this perspective, one expects that a general $\mathcal N = 1$ gauged supergravity will lift to a nongeometric compactification." (Sec. 5.1, printed p.18.)
9. "IIA Minkowski theories (in this model) must be nongeometric", in agreement with the no-go of [48]. (Sec. 5, printed p.16.)
10. Hull's T-folds: fibrations with $O(n,n;\mathbb Z)$ transition functions "will generically be nongeometric"; the doubled formalism "has been used to perform the mysterious third T-duality which produces the $R^{abc}$ fluxes [35]". (Sec. 6, printed p.22-23.)

# Caveats

- Lowest order in $g_s$ and $\alpha'$ throughout; arguments "at times schematic" (p.2-3). Table 3 ($S,\tau,U$ in IIA vs IIB) is explicitly "schematic" (footnote 1).
- The $T^3$ with $H$-flux is not a string solution (flat with $H\ne 0$); used as illustration only (p.12). $B_{xy} = Nz$ is a gauge choice introducing explicit $z$-dependence.
- Second T-duality (along $y$) lacks a globally defined Killing vector; third (along $z$) has no isometry at all and is only justified via duality-invariance of $W$ and the doubled formalism.
- The duality-invariant $W$ (5.1)-(5.2) is only argued for the subset of terms obtained from the IIA twisted torus by dualities and rotations; turning on all terms simultaneously is an assumption (p.16), supported by $SU(3)\times SU(3)$-structure results [8].
- Table 5 transcription: the $U\tau^2$ IIA entry prints as $Q^{\gamma j}_\gamma$ (one repeated index), which looks like a typesetting slip; the $U\tau$ IIA row prints $f^j_{k\alpha}, f^i_{\beta k}, f^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}$ while Sec. 3.2 lists the surviving $f$ as $f^\alpha_{jk}, f^i_{\beta k}, f^i_{j\gamma}, f^\alpha_{\beta\gamma}$; the second term in (5.9) carries an $f^x_{ab]}$ with a free-looking index as printed. Cross-check against [28,29] before reuse.
- Constraints (5.5)-(5.16) are stated without derivation ("we will simply write these down"); some are identically zero in the model and not all are independent (footnote 3); RHS nonzero with localized sources.
- Solution statistics (Sec. 5.2) are purely numerical [29]; the author lists seven reasons to doubt the infinitude of nongeometric vacua (large $g_s$, string-scale sizes, no backreaction, no 10d lift, modular invariance, the $U\to U+1/n$ degeneracy, truncation of light modes).
- Worldsheet techniques of Sec. 6 "may not describe the backgrounds discussed previously" (p.21); the Conclusions misreference these as "Section 7".
- No signature is declared; no explicit Scherk-Schwarz reduction ansatz or flux-by-flux scalar potential is given, only $W$ and the standard $\mathcal N=1$ formula (2.5).
