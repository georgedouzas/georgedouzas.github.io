# Conventions

- Signature: M_4 has "the usual signature (-,+,+,+)" and the internal X^n has Euclidean signature (+,+,+,...) (Sec. 2, p. 3).
- Index conventions: D=11 indices M = 0,1,...,10; spacetime mu = 0,1,2,3; internal m = 1,...,7 (pp. 4-5).
  Tangent-space indices A,B (D=11), a,b (internal); Gamma^A are the D=11 Dirac matrices.
- Riemann/Ricci sign: not stated as a convention. Implicitly fixed by the Freund-Rubin result
  $R_{\mu\nu} = -12 m^2 g_{\mu\nu}$ (eq. 10, p. 4) for AdS_4 and $R_{mn} = 6 m^2 g_{mn}$ (eq. 11, p. 5) for S^7,
  i.e. positive Ricci for spheres, negative for AdS. The Lichnerowicz operator (eq. 21) and the coset Riemann
  tensor (eq. 28) are written in this convention.
- Spinor covariant derivative: $D_M = \partial_M - \tfrac14 \omega_M{}^{AB}\Gamma_{AB}$ (eq. 6, p. 4).
- Supercovariant derivative: $\tilde D_M = D_M - \tfrac{i}{144}(\Gamma^{NPQR}{}_M + 8\Gamma^{PQR}\delta^N{}_M) F_{NPQR}$ (eq. 5, p. 4),
  which in the Freund-Rubin background splits as $\tilde D_\mu = D_\mu + m\gamma_\mu\gamma_5$ (eq. 12) and
  $\tilde D_m = D_m - \tfrac12 m \Gamma_m$ (eq. 13), p. 5.
- Field strength: F = dA (p. 4). Bosonic field equations
  $R_{MN} - \tfrac12 g_{MN} R = \tfrac13\big(F_{MPQR}F_N{}^{PQR} - \tfrac18 g_{MN} F^{PQRS}F_{PQRS}\big)$ (eq. 7),
  $d{*F} + F\wedge F = 0$ (eq. 8), p. 4.
- Couplings: $e^2 \sim G m^2$ (eq. 1), $\Lambda \sim -m^2$ (eq. 2), hence $G\Lambda \sim -e^2$ (eq. 3), p. 4;
  m^{-1} is the radius of the compact manifold, G is Newton's constant.
- KK metric ansatz: NO explicit metric ansatz is written anywhere in the paper. Neither the abelian S^1 ansatz nor
  the M_4 x K ansatz with Killing vectors K_a^m(y) appears as an equation. The only ansatz written out is the
  Freund-Rubin one, $F_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma} = 3m\,\epsilon_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}$ (eq. 9, p. 4). The circle reduction and
  the DeWitt/Pauli non-abelian reductions are discussed only in prose in Sec. 7 (pp. 14-15). Footnote 6 (p. 14)
  names the fields of the S^1 ansatz: the 4D metric, the KK vector from the mixed (mu z) components of
  $\hat g_{MN}$, and the dilatonic scalar from the (zz) component. No warp factor or scalar exponent is given.

# Equations the tutorial uses

(a) S^1 ansatz and reduced action: NOT FOUND as equations. What the paper does give (Sec. 7, p. 14, prose and footnote 6):
- Circle reduction = Fourier-expand all fields in the circle coordinate z and keep only the zero modes; this is
  "guaranteed to be a consistent truncation" because non-zero modes are charged under the U(1) acting on the
  circle while zero modes are uncharged, so "it is impossible for powers of purely uncharged fields to generate
  a term that could act as a source for a charged field" -- one is "truncating to the singlet sector of the U(1)".
- Footnote 6: the full set of U(1) singlets of 5D pure gravity on a circle "comprises the four-dimensional metric,
  the Kaluza-Klein vector originating from the mixed (mu z) components of the higher-dimensional metric
  $\hat g_{MN}$, and the dilatonic scalar originating from the (zz) component of $\hat g_{MN}$." Truncating the
  dilaton to get Einstein-Maxwell is inconsistent "because the Maxwell field would actually act as a source,
  via an $F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$ term, for the dilaton that was supposedly set to zero."

(b) General ansatz with Killing vectors and the induced non-abelian gauge transformation: NOT FOUND as equations.
Prose only (Sec. 2, pp. 3-4; Sec. 7, p. 15). The only Killing vector written explicitly is that of the
$Sp_1^C$ isometry factor on the squashed S^7 (Sec. 8.2, p. 23):
  $s^i = s^{ia}\partial_a$ with $[s^i, s^j] = \epsilon^{ijk} s^k$, and
  $\check D_a s^i_b = -3\,\epsilon^i{}_{jk}\, s^j_a s^k_b$.   (44)

(c) Gauge group = isometry group; structure constants.
- Statement (Sec. 2, pp. 3-4): spontaneous compactification on $M_4 \times X^n$ "would yield Yang-Mills fields in
  D=4 with gauge group G, where G is the isometry group of X^n, with coupling constant e where" $e^2 \sim G m^2$ (eq. 1).
- Sec. 3, p. 6: "It was well known that the number of massless gauge bosons was determined by the isometry group
  of the compactifying manifold, but it turned out to be the holonomy group that determined the number of
  massless gravitini."
- Structure constants of a reductive coset G/H, generators $T_A$ of G split into $T_i \in H$ and the rest $T_a$ (Sec. 8.1, p. 19):
  $[T_i, T_j] = f_{ij}{}^k T_k,\quad [T_i, T_a] = f_{ia}{}^b T_b,\quad [T_a, T_b] = f_{ab}{}^i T_i + f_{ab}{}^c T_c.$   (29)
- Riemann tensor of the coset in terms of them (from their ref. [74], Bais-Nicolai-van Nieuwenhuizen), p. 19:
  $R_{abc}{}^d = f_{ab}{}^i f_{ic}{}^d + \tfrac12 f_{ab}{}^e f_{ec}{}^d + \tfrac12 f_{[a|c|}{}^e f_{b]e}{}^d.$   (28)

(d) Mass spectrum / harmonic expansion on a coset space (Sec. 8.1, pp. 19-21).
- Operators needed for the AdS_4 spectrum of a Freund-Rubin compactification on K^7:
  $\Delta_p = \delta d + d\delta\ (p=0,1,2,3),\ \Delta_L,\ Q = {*d},\ i\slashed D_{1/2},\ i\slashed D_{3/2}$   (26), with $\Delta_3 = Q^2$.
- All may be written collectively ([73,44]) as $\Delta = -\Box - R_{abcd}\Sigma^{ab}\Sigma^{cd}$   (27), $\Sigma^{ab}$ the SO(7) generators.
- H-covariant derivative is algebraic ([13,1]): $\check D_a \equiv D_a + \tfrac12 f_{abc}\Sigma^{bc} = -T_a$   (30),
  $n = \dim G - \dim H$, so $\check\Box \equiv \check D_a \check D^a = T_a T^a = -(C_G - C_H)$   (31).
- Relation to the true Laplacian: $\check\Box = \Box + f_{abc}\Sigma^{ab}\check D^c - \tfrac14 f_{abc} f^{ade}\Sigma^{bc}\Sigma_{de}$   (32).
- Universal Laplacian depending only on $C_G$ (the H Casimir cancels) [44,41]:
  $\Delta = C_G + f_{abc}\Sigma^{ab}\check D^c - \tfrac14\big(3 f_{abc} f^a{}_{de} - 2 f_{abd} f^a{}_{ce}\Sigma^{bc}\Sigma_{de}\big)$   (33).
- Ricci identity: $[\check D_a, \check D_b] = (T^i)_{ab}(T_i)_{cd}\Sigma^{cd} + f_{ab}{}^c\check D_c$   (34).
- Scalar tower at risk of instability (Sec. 6, p. 11): $M^2 = \Delta_L - 4m^2$   (20), with
  $\Delta_L Y_{mn} = -\Box Y_{mn} - 2R_{mpnq}Y^{pq} + 2R_{(m}{}^p Y_{n)p},\ \nabla^m Y_{mn} = 0,\ g^{mn}Y_{mn} = 0$   (21),
  BF-stability criterion $\Delta_L \ge 3m^2$   (22); Riemann eigenvalues $R_{mpnq}X^{pq} = \kappa X_{mn}$   (24),
  sufficient condition $\kappa_{\max} \le \tfrac{21}{4} m^2$   (25), p. 12.
- Squashed S^7 specifics (Sec. 8.2): $f_{abc} = -\tfrac{1}{\sqrt5} a_{abc}$ (36), $a_{abc}$ octonionic; $\check D_a a_{bcd} = 0$ (37);
  $a_{abc} = i\bar\eta\Gamma_{abc}\eta$ (38); $\Delta = C_G + \tfrac65 C_{SO(7)} - \tfrac32 C_{G_2} - \tfrac{1}{\sqrt5} a_{abc}\Sigma^{bc}\check D^a$ (39);
  2-form equation $\Delta_2 Y_{ab} = (C_G + 3P_7 - \tfrac{2}{\sqrt5}\check D_{[2]})Y_{ab} = \kappa_2^2 Y_{ab}$ (40) with
  eigenvalues (41)-(43), e.g. $\Delta_2^{(3)} = C_G = \tfrac{m^2}{9}\,20 C_G$ using $m^2 = 9/20$ (p. 22).
- Modes vs fields (footnote 5, p. 9): AdS_4 fields are in SO(2,3) irreps $D(E_0, s)$, connected to modes via
  $E_0 = E_0(M^2)$, $M^2 = M^2(\Delta_p)$, $\Delta_p = \Delta_p(C_G)$.
- S^2 = SU(2)/U(1) or general S^n: NOT discussed; no harmonic expansion on S^2 or S^n is given. The only mention
  of S^2 is Pauli's 1953 proposal (p. 15), see (e). Round S^7 spectrum is only cited ([75,76,77], footnote 10, p. 21).

(e) Consistent truncation (Sec. 7, pp. 14-18, prose; no equation numbers):
- Definition (p. 14): "any solution of the truncated lower-dimensional theory will also be a solution of the full
  untruncated theory"; equivalently "there cannot be any source terms in the full non-zero mode equations that
  are formed purely from the zero-mode fields that are eventually to be retained."
- Circle and torus: always consistent (singlet sector of U(1)^n), provided ALL singlets are kept (footnote 6).
- Group manifolds G (p. 15): bi-invariant metric with $G_L \times G_R$ both transitive; DeWitt (1963, ref. [55])
  ansatz keeps the gauge bosons of just one factor, say $G_R$; consistent because retained fields are all the
  $G_L$ singlets and "powers of $G_L$ singlets cannot act as sources for $G_L$ non-singlets"; finitely many fields
  survive because $G_L$ acts transitively ("DeWitt reductions").
- General coset G/H keeping the isometry gauge bosons (p. 15): "a totally different story." Pauli's 1953 letter to
  Pais: 6D Einstein gravity on S^2 would give SU(2) Yang-Mills, but the non-zero modes of the 4D metric (massive
  spin-2) "would have source terms built from the SU(2) Yang-Mills fields one wishes to retain. Thus it would be
  inconsistent to set the massive spin-2 fields to zero."
- S^7 (pp. 15-16): Gibbons objected ("eat his hat"); Duff-Nilsson-Pope-Warner [58] found the SO(8) Yang-Mills^2
  source for massive spin-2 has coefficient exactly zero by a cancellation between the Einstein-equation metric
  contribution and the 4-form reduction-ansatz contribution; de Wit-Nicolai [59] gave the full metric ansatz and
  consistency; loose ends on the 4-form ansatz fixed in [11] (Nicolai-Pilch 2012) and [60].
- S^4 (p. 16): Nastase-Vaman-van Nieuwenhuizen [61,62], simpler scalar sector; S^5 of IIB: SL(2,R)-singlet
  subsector [63], full truncation via ExFT [68]; S^7 revisited via ExFT [69]. Footnote 7: "It is always the
  scalar sector that creates the biggest headaches in the construction of consistent truncations."
- Hopf reduction S^7 -> CP^3 (p. 17): keeping U(1) singlets, 8 gravitini -> $6_0 + 1_2 + 1_{-2}$ under
  SU(4) x U(1), so N=6; "Of course, the truncation is guaranteed to be a consistent one."
- ExFT explanation (p. 18): a Pauli-type sphere reduction "can be recast as a statement of truncation to fields
  that are singlets under the enlarged symmetry group of the extended formulation, thereby making the
  consistency of the truncation manifest."

(f) Explicit ansatz for S^2 or round-sphere metrics with Killing vectors: NOT FOUND. No metric on S^2, S^n or S^7
is written. Round S^7 facts stated in words: 8 Killing spinors, isometry SO(8), $R_{mn} = 6m^2 g_{mn}$ (eq. 11).
Squashed S^7 = $(Sp_2 \times Sp_1^C)/(Sp_1^A \times Sp_1^{B+C})$ (p. 20, footnote 9), isometry $Sp_2 \times Sp_1$,
$G_2$ holonomy, group chain $SO(7) \to G_2 \to H = Sp_2 \times Sp_1^C$ (35). Killing spinor conditions:
$\tilde D_M\epsilon = 0$ (14), splitting into $\tilde D_\mu\epsilon(x) = 0$ (15), $\tilde D_m\eta(y) = 0$ (16), with
integrability $[\tilde D_m, \tilde D_n]\eta = -\tfrac14 C_{mn}{}^{ab}\Gamma_{ab}\eta = 0$ (17), $C$ the Weyl tensor (pp. 5-6).
Weak $G_2$ holonomy: $dA = 4m\,{*A}$ (18), with $A_{mnp} = \bar\eta\Gamma_{mnp}\eta$ (19), p. 6.

# Attributable statements

1. Gauge symmetry from geometry: spontaneous compactification on $M_4\times X^n$ yields D=4 Yang-Mills with gauge
   group G = isometry group of X^n, coupling $e^2 \sim Gm^2$, typically $\Lambda\sim -m^2$, so $G\Lambda\sim -e^2$ (Sec. 2, pp. 3-4, eqs. 1-3).
2. The isometry group fixes the number of massless gauge bosons, but the holonomy group (of the supercovariant
   derivative) fixes the number of massless gravitini; number of 4D supersymmetries = number of Killing spinors on X^7 (Sec. 3, pp. 5-6).
3. Circle (and torus) reduction keeping all zero modes is automatically consistent: it is a truncation to the U(1)
   singlet sector, and uncharged fields cannot source charged ones (Sec. 7, p. 14).
4. Dropping the dilaton of the S^1 reduction is inconsistent since $F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$ sources it (Sec. 7, footnote 6, p. 14).
5. Group-manifold (DeWitt) reductions keeping the gauge bosons of one of $G_L$, $G_R$ are consistent by the same
   singlet argument; general coset G/H reductions keeping the isometry gauge bosons are not guaranteed, Pauli's
   S^2 example (1953) being the first known failure (Sec. 7, p. 15).
6. Consistency of the S^7 truncation of D=11 supergravity to SO(8)-gauged N=8 supergravity: conjectured 1982 [9],
   leading-order obstruction shown to cancel in [58] (1984), full demonstration by de Wit-Nicolai [59] (1987),
   "not completely proved until 2012" [11] (Sec. 1, p. 3; Sec. 7, pp. 15-16).
7. Definition of consistent truncation: every solution of the truncated lower-dimensional theory lifts to a
   solution of the higher-dimensional theory (Sec. 7, p. 14).
8. Skew-whiffing theorem: reversing the orientation of X^7 (equivalently $m\to -m$) leaves the integrability
   condition (17) unchanged, so apart from round S^7 at most one orientation is supersymmetric; symmetric spaces
   admit no supersymmetry; skew-whiffed vacua are nonetheless classically stable (Sec. 3, pp. 6-7).
9. Classical stability of Freund-Rubin vacua reduces to $\Delta_L \ge 3m^2$ for transverse-traceless modes,
   guaranteed when Killing spinors exist; $\kappa_{\max}\le \tfrac{21}{4}m^2$ is sufficient but not necessary (Sec. 6, pp. 11-12).
10. On any reductive coset G/H the KK mass operators can be handled algebraically: $\check D_a = -T_a$, and the
    universal operator $\Delta$ depends only on $C_G$, with $C_H$ cancelling (Sec. 8.1, pp. 20-21, eqs. 30-33).
11. Witten's no-go: no chiral fermions from compactifications of D=11 supergravity; together with the huge negative
    cosmological constant this made coset compactifications untenable as realistic models until AdS/CFT (Sec. 7, p. 13).

# Caveats

- Review vs new: Secs. 1-7 are historical review of the 1986 Physics Reports [1] and later literature. Sec. 8
  reviews post-2018 work [39,40,41,44]; the explicit 2-form mode functions (45)-(48) from [41] are called
  "a novel result in this context" (p. 19), and a "more fundamental argument" for the double-derivative mode
  $\mathcal Y^{(6)i}_{ab}$ is deferred to [79] (to appear). Nothing here is a first-principles derivation of KK reduction.
- No metric ansatz is written: the tutorial cannot cite this paper for the form of the S^1 or Killing-vector
  ansatz, only for conceptual statements (consistency, isometry = gauge group). Cite Phys. Rept. 130 (1986) [1] for formulas.
- Consistency: the massless sector of a generic coset reduction is NOT a consistent truncation (Pauli's S^2);
  S^7, S^4, S^5 are special, with consistency relying on supergravity form fields cancelling the metric source
  (p. 16). Even the S^1 reduction is consistent only if all singlets (metric, vector, dilaton) are kept (footnote 6).
- Chirality: Witten showed no chiral fermionic sector arises from D=11 supergravity compactifications (p. 13);
  singular $G_2$ compactifications of full M-theory can evade this [24,25] (p. 6).
- Cosmological constant: Freund-Rubin vacua have $\Lambda\sim -m^2$, $G\Lambda\sim -e^2$, "huge negative" (pp. 4, 13).
  AdS stability uses the Breitenlohner-Freedman bound $M^2 \ge -m^2$ with $\Lambda = -12m^2$ (p. 10). The symbol m
  is the inverse-radius scale; Sec. 8.2 uses $m^2 = 9/20$ for the squashed sphere (p. 22).
- Holonomy subtlety: the relevant holonomy is that of $\tilde D_m$ (eq. 13), not Levi-Civita; $G_2$ appears as
  weak holonomy of the squashed S^7 (p. 6). Generalized holonomy $H_{gen}\subset SO(8)$ resolves the N=3 puzzle (p. 7).
- Modes vs fields: footnote 5 (p. 9) insists on distinguishing harmonics on S^7 from AdS_4 fields.
- Stability of non-supersymmetric AdS: the right-squashed S^7 is one of very few non-supersymmetric AdS vacua not
  yet proven unstable, in tension with the Ooguri-Vafa conjecture (p. 13).
- Open issues flagged in Sec. 8: degeneracies in the $\Delta_2$ spectrum, a Wess-Zumino tower with two possible
  boundary conditions, singletons needed to relate round and squashed spectra, and $OSp(4,1)$ not a subgroup
  of $OSp(4,8)$ (pp. 22-24).
