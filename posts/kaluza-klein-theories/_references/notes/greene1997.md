# Conventions

- Signature: never stated. The paper works with the Euclidean worldsheet sigma model and with the compact internal space; "M_4 is Minkowski four-dimensional space" (p. 9) is the only statement, with no explicit metric signature.
- Spinor conventions: none. The word "spinor" does not occur in the paper; there is no gravitino variation, no covariantly constant spinor, and no Killing-spinor equation (confirmed by full-text search).
- Complex coordinates: on a real 2d-dimensional manifold, z_j = x_j + i x_{d+j} (p. 15); d = n/2 is the complex dimension, with n = 6, d = 3 for most of the lectures (p. 12).
- Complex tangent-space basis (2.9), p. 19: {∂/∂z^1, ..., ∂/∂z^d, ∂/∂z̄^1, ..., ∂/∂z̄^d}; decomposition T_pX^C = T_pX^{(1,0)} ⊕ T_pX^{(0,1)} (2.11), p. 20.
- Hermitian metric (2.33), p. 23: g = g_{i j̄} dz^i ⊗ dz̄^{j̄} + g_{ī j} dz̄^{ī} ⊗ dz^j, i.e. g_{ij} = g_{ī j̄} = 0.
- Kähler form (2.34)-(2.35), p. 24: J = i g_{i j̄} dz^i ⊗ dz̄^{j̄} − i g_{j̄ i} dz̄^{j̄} ⊗ dz^i = i g_{i j̄} dz^i ∧ dz̄^{j̄}; Kähler iff dJ = 0; locally J = i ∂∂̄K with Kähler potential K, g_{i j̄} = ∂^2 K / ∂z^i ∂z̄^{j̄} (2.38), p. 24. (Note that (2.35) carries no factor 1/2; for C^m with g = Σ dz^j ⊗ dz̄^j the paper writes J = (i/2) Σ dz^j ∧ dz̄^j on p. 28, which is the normalisation one gets from the symmetrised (2.34).)
- Hodge star (2.47), p. 26, and d^† = (−1)^{np+n+1} ⋆ d ⋆ (2.48); inner product ⟨ω, ω'⟩ = ∫_X ω ∧ ⋆ω' (2.49).
- Holomorphic (d,0)-form: "h^{0,d} = h^{d,0} = 1. The latter is a holomorphic, nowhere vanishing differential form of type (d,0) on the Calabi-Yau, usually referred to as Ω" (p. 32). No normalisation of Ω is fixed anywhere; it enters only through the Kähler potential −ln(i ∫_M Ω ∧ Ω̄) (p. 35) and the invariant (2.81).
- Curvature in Kähler coordinates (2.42)-(2.43), p. 25: R_{i j̄ k l̄} = g_{i s̄} ∂Γ^{s̄}_{j̄ l̄}/∂z^k and R_{i j̄} = R^{k}_{\ i k̄ j̄} = −∂Γ^{k̄}_{ī k̄}/∂z^j (indices transcribed as printed; the paper's index placement is loose).

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) Supersymmetry condition and holonomy argument

The paper does NOT contain δψ_M = ∇_M ε = 0 or any spinor-based derivation. Its argument for Calabi-Yau is worldsheet-based, in §1.3, pp. 8-9, for type IIA/IIB (footnote 2, p. 8: "Such theories can always be converted into more phenomenologically viable heterotic string theories but that will not be required for our purposes"). The chain is:

1. Perturbative consistency requires a 2d (super)conformal field theory of central charge c = 15; space-time supersymmetry leads to N = 2 (i.e. (2,2)) worldsheet supersymmetry; the c = 15 theory is split as (c = 6 free theory of four bosons + superpartners, giving M_4) × (c = 9 "internal" theory) (p. 8).
2. Compactification: six dimensions are replaced by a compact M giving "a space-time of the Kaluza-Klein type M_4 × M where M_4 is Minkowski four-dimensional space" (p. 9). The internal action (unnumbered display, p. 9):
   $$S = \frac{1}{4\pi\alpha'}\int dz\,d\bar z\Big[g_{mn}(\partial X^m\bar\partial X^n + \partial X^n\bar\partial X^m) + B_{mn}(\partial X^m\bar\partial X^n - \partial X^n\bar\partial X^m) + \cdots\Big],$$
   with g_{mn} a metric on M, B_{mn} an antisymmetric tensor field, fermionic terms omitted.
3. The verbatim argument (p. 9): "To meet the criterion of conformal invariance, M must, to lowest order in sigma model perturbation theory [footnote 3: 'to lowest order in α'/R^2 where R is a typical radius'], admit a metric g_{μν} whose Ricci tensor R_{μν} vanishes. In order to contribute nine to the central charge, the dimension of M must be six, and to ensure the additional condition of N = 2 supersymmetry, M must be a complex Kähler manifold. These conditions together are referred to as the 'Calabi-Yau' conditions".

The holonomy statement itself is in §2.8, pp. 25-26 (no equation number beyond (2.44) v' = A_C v and (2.45) v = v^j ∂_{z^j} + v^{j̄} ∂_{z̄^j}): for a Kähler manifold the Levi-Civita connection (2.40)-(2.41), Γ^l_{jk} = g^{l s̄} ∂g_{k s̄}/∂z^j and its conjugate, has only pure-index components, so holomorphic and antiholomorphic components of a vector "do not mix" under parallel transport, hence the holonomy lies in U(d) ⊂ SO(n); "the second special case are those complex Kähler manifolds whose holonomy group is even further restricted to lie in SU(d) ... having holonomy SU(d) means that the U(1) part of the Levi-Civita connection Γ vanishes" (p. 26).

## (b) Definition of a Calabi-Yau manifold and stated equivalences

Definition 1 (§2.11, p. 31): "A Calabi-Yau manifold is a compact, complex, Kähler manifold which has SU(d) holonomy." Equivalent list given at the start of §2.1, p. 12: compact; complex; Kähler; SU(d) holonomy, with d = n/2.

Equivalence with Ricci-flatness (unnumbered display, p. 31):
$$R_{i\bar j} = -\frac{\partial}{\partial \bar z^{j}}\Gamma^{k}_{ik} = 0 ,$$
"It is not hard to show that the vanishing of the U(1) part of the connection, effectively its trace, which ensures that the holonomy lies in SU(d) is tantamount to having a Ricci-flat metric."

Chern class expansion (2.68), p. 31:
$$c(X) = 1 + \sum_j c_j(X) = \det(1+\mathcal R) = 1 + \mathrm{tr}\,\mathcal R + \mathrm{tr}(\mathcal R\wedge\mathcal R - 2(\mathrm{tr}\,\mathcal R)^2) + \ldots,\qquad \mathcal R = R^{k}{}_{l i \bar j}\, dz^i\wedge d\bar z^{\bar j}.$$

Theorem 2 (Yau), p. 31: "If X is a complex Kähler manifold with vanishing first Chern class and with Kähler form J, then there exists a unique Ricci-flat metric on X whose Kähler form J' is in the same cohomology class as J." Comment, p. 32: "no explicit Ricci-flat metrics are known on any Calabi-Yau manifolds."

Hodge-number constraints from SU(d) holonomy (p. 32, no equation number): h^{0,s} = h^{s,0} = 0 for 1 < s < d [sic; the text literally says "for 1 < s < d", although the diamond displayed below it also has h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = 0], h^{0,d} = h^{d,0} = 1 (the holomorphic form Ω), h^{0,0} = 1 by connectedness. General Kähler relations: h^{r,s} = h^{m−r,m−s} (2.55) [Hodge star], h^{r,s} = h^{s,r} (2.56), H^p_d(X) = ⊕_{r+s=p} H^{r,s}_{∂̄}(X) (2.57), all p. 27.

Also printed: d = 1 (1; 1 1; 1) and d = 2 (1; 0 0; 1 20 1; 0 0; 1), with "there is a unique two-dimensional Calabi-Yau manifold" (K3), while "in the case of complex dimension three, there are numerous possibilities for the Hodge numbers" (p. 32).

Index theorem (2.75), p. 35:
$$\chi(X) = \sum_{r,s} (-1)^{r+s} h^{r,s} .$$
The specialised formula χ = 2(h^{1,1} − h^{2,1}) is NOT written explicitly anywhere in the paper (full-text search); it follows from (2.75) and the diamond above, and the paper uses it numerically: "Using our earlier calculation that the Euler number of the quintic is −200, and our present calculation that h^{2,1} = 101, we can deduce h^{1,1} = 1" (p. 35). For K3, (2.76), p. 35: χ(K3) = 24 = 1 + 1 + 1 + 1 + h^{1,1}, giving h^{1,1} = 20. The conifold formula (8.32), p. 103, (h^{2,1}, h^{1,1}) → (h^{2,1} − (P − R), h^{1,1} + R) with "the Euler characteristic of the variety thus jumps by 2P", is consistent with χ = 2(h^{1,1} − h^{2,1}).

## (d) Moduli counting and (absent) generation counting

Metric deformations preserving Ricci-flatness (2.74), p. 34:
$$\delta g = \delta g_{ij}\,dz^i dz^j + \delta g_{i\bar j}\,dz^i d\bar z^{\bar j} + \text{c.c.}$$
"δg_{i j̄} dz^i ∧ dz̄^{j̄} must be harmonic and hence is uniquely associated to an element of H^{1,1}_{∂̄}(X). Using the holomorphic three-form Ω, it can also be shown that Ω_{ijk} g^{k k̄} δg_{k̄ l̄} dz^i ∧ dz^j ∧ dz̄^{l̄} is an element of H^{2,1}_{∂̄}(X)" (p. 34). Interpretation (p. 35): mixed-type deformations are "deformations of the Kähler class J of X to a new element of H^{1,1}_{∂̄}(X)"; pure-type deformations give a metric Hermitian with respect to a different complex structure, i.e. "deformations of the complex structure of X", in one-to-one correspondence with changes of the defining polynomial(s) preserving degree and homogeneity. Hence the moduli space has h^{1,1} real Kähler parameters and h^{2,1} complex complex-structure parameters (real dimension 2h^{2,1} + h^{1,1}; quintic: 2·101 + 1 = 203, p. 35).

Stringy completion (§7.3.1, p. 84): string theory combines J with the antisymmetric tensor B into a complexified Kähler form B + iJ; with e^l an integral basis of H^2(M,Z),
$$B + iJ = \sum_l (B_l + iJ_l)\, e^l \quad (7.3), \qquad w_l = e^{2\pi i (B_l + iJ_l)} \quad (7.2),$$
and B → B + Q with Q ∈ H^2(M,Z) leaves the model unchanged. The Kähler form must obey the Kähler-cone positivity ∫_{M_r} J^r > 0 for every r-dimensional complex submanifold M_r (7.1), p. 82. Local product structure of the CFT moduli space (7.5), p. 86: M_CFT ≡ M_complex structure × M_Kähler structure.

Moduli-space geometry (p. 35, no equation numbers): both moduli spaces are Kähler; the Kähler potential on the complex-structure moduli space is −ln(i ∫_M Ω ∧ Ω̄) and on the Kähler moduli space ∫_M J ∧ J ∧ J (three-fold), "the latter, we shall see, suffers quantum corrections in string theory"; both are special Kähler with Kähler potential determined by a prepotential F(z), (2.77), p. 36:
$$K = i\Big(\bar w^j \frac{\partial\mathcal F}{\partial w^j} - w^j\frac{\partial\bar{\mathcal F}}{\partial \bar w^j}\Big).$$

Generation counting: NOT in this paper. There is no statement of "number of generations = |χ|/2", no E_6, no 27/27-bar representation content, and no "standard embedding" (none of these strings occur in the text; the only "generation" hits are "degeneration"). The lectures are about type II (N = 2 space-time SUSY) compactifications; the only remarks on heterotic strings are footnote 2 (p. 8) and the K3 × T^2 duality remark in §12 (p. 144). The tutorial must source the generation formula elsewhere (e.g. Candelas-Horowitz-Strominger-Witten 1985 or GSW vol. 2).

## (e) The quintic

Defining equation (2.65), p. 29, the locus in CP^4 of
$$\sum_{i=1}^5 (z_i)^5 = 0 ,$$
rewritten on the patch U_j as 1 + Σ_{i=1}^4 (ξ^i_{(j)})^5 = 0 (2.66), p. 30, with explicit holomorphic coordinate changes (Jacobian (2.67), p. 30) building an atlas. Calabi-Yau proof via Chern classes (pp. 33): c(CP^4) = (1 + J)^5 (2.69), c_1(CP^4) = 5J; adjunction TCP^4|_{P=0} = TX ⊕ NX (2.70), c(TCP^4|_{P=0}) = c(T_P) ∧ c(NX) (2.71), c(TX) = c(TCP^4|_{P=0})/c(NX) (2.72), and for a degree-t hypersurface
$$c(TX) = \frac{(1+J)^5}{(1+tJ)} = 1 + (5-t)J + \cdots \quad (2.73),$$
so c_1 = 0 iff t = 5. Expanding (2.73) to third order, c_3 = −40 J^3, and "As J^3 integrates to 5 ... we see that the quintic has Euler number −200" (p. 33). Hodge numbers (p. 35): h^{2,1} = 101 (126 quintic monomials in five variables minus 25 GL(5,C) reparametrisations), h^{1,1} = 1 (inherited from the ambient CP^4), deduced from χ = −200 via (2.75). Table 1, p. 78, lists the quintic as (h^{2,1}, h^{1,1}, χ) = (101, 1, −200) and its Z_5^3 mirror quotient as (1, 101, 200). Quartic in CP^3: c_2 = 6J^2, χ = 24, this is K3 (p. 34).

## (f) Mirror symmetry (one paragraph)

Section 6, pp. 67-80. Motivation (pp. 67-68): in the c = 9 (2,2) SCFT the two kinds of truly marginal operators differ "only in a rather trivial way: the conventional sign of a U(1) charge", whereas geometrically they correspond to the very different groups H^1(M,T) (complex-structure deformations, ≅ H^{2,1} after contraction with Ω) and H^1(M,T^*) ≅ H^{1,1} (Kähler deformations). The mirror conjecture of Dixon and Lerche-Vafa-Warner resolves this asymmetry by pairing each Calabi-Yau M with a second M̃ giving the same conformal field theory with the roles of the two cohomology groups reversed, so that (6.1)-(6.2), p. 68, h^{1,1}(M) = h^{d−1,1}(M̃), h^{d−1,1}(M) = h^{1,1}(M̃): the Hodge diamond of M̃ is the mirror reflection of that of M through a diagonal axis. The lectures present the Greene-Plesser construction (orbifold of the Gepner/minimal-model point of the quintic family by a discrete group G, M̃ = M/G, with the orbifold identification x = g(y) (6.3), p. 69) as "the only established construction" at the time, with explicit tables of mirror pairs (Table 1, p. 78, and Table 2, p. 79). Implications (§6.6, pp. 77-80): mirror symmetry is a strong/weak sigma-model coupling duality because the expansion parameter α'/R^2 of one model is a complex-structure parameter of the other; and the three-point function of (2,1)-forms, computed exactly by the classical integral ∫_M Ω^{abc} b̃_a^{(i)} ∧ b̃_b^{(j)} ∧ b̃_c^{(k)} ∧ Ω (6.27), equals the Kähler-side Yukawa coupling ∫_{M̃} b^{(i)} ∧ b^{(j)} ∧ b^{(k)} plus an infinite series of worldsheet instanton corrections from rational curves (6.28)-(6.29), pp. 78-80, which allowed Candelas et al. to count rational curves of every degree on the quintic. Strominger-Yau-Zaslow's T-duality interpretation is mentioned on p. 68.

# Attributable statements

1. "A Calabi-Yau manifold is a compact, complex, Kähler manifold which has SU(d) holonomy" (Definition 1, §2.11, p. 31); "we shall take Calabi-Yau to mean holonomy which fills out SU(d)" rather than a proper subgroup (§2.8, p. 26).
2. Conformal invariance to lowest order in α'/R^2 requires a Ricci-flat metric on M; c = 9 requires dim M = 6; N = 2 worldsheet supersymmetry requires M to be complex Kähler; these are collectively "the Calabi-Yau conditions" (§1.3, p. 9).
3. SU(d) holonomy is equivalent to the vanishing of the U(1) (trace) part of the Levi-Civita connection, which is "tantamount to having a Ricci-flat metric" (§2.11, p. 31); for a Kähler manifold the holonomy already lies in U(d) because the connection has only pure-index components (§2.8, pp. 25-26).
4. Yau's theorem (Theorem 2, p. 31) guarantees existence and uniqueness (within a Kähler class) of a Ricci-flat metric when c_1 = 0; "no explicit Ricci-flat metrics are known on any Calabi-Yau manifolds" (p. 32).
5. For SU(d) holonomy, h^{0,s} = h^{s,0} = 0 for 0 < s < d and h^{d,0} = h^{0,d} = 1, the latter being the nowhere-vanishing holomorphic (d,0)-form Ω; hence the three-fold Hodge diamond is fixed by h^{1,1} and h^{2,1} alone (§2.11, p. 32).
6. Ricci-flat metric deformations split into mixed-type ones (harmonic (1,1)-forms, Kähler-class deformations, counted by h^{1,1}) and pure-type ones (mapped by Ω to H^{2,1}, complex-structure deformations, counted by h^{2,1}); "the parameter space of those Calabi-Yau manifolds continuously connected to some initial one X thereby consists of the possible choices of complex and Kähler structures" (§2.12, pp. 34-35).
7. The quintic hypersurface in CP^4 is Calabi-Yau because c(TX) = (1+J)^5/(1+5J) has vanishing first-order term; c_3 = −40J^3 with ∫ J^3 = 5 gives χ = −200; h^{2,1} = 101 (126 monomials minus 25 GL(5,C) parameters), h^{1,1} = 1 (§2.11-2.12, pp. 33-35).
8. Complex-structure and Kähler moduli spaces are each special Kähler manifolds, with Kähler potentials −ln(i ∫ Ω ∧ Ω̄) and ∫ J ∧ J ∧ J respectively, the latter receiving quantum (α') corrections (§2.12, pp. 35-36).
9. Mirror pairs (M, M̃) correspond to the same conformal field theory with h^{1,1}(M) = h^{d−1,1}(M̃) and h^{d−1,1}(M) = h^{1,1}(M̃); the Hodge diamond of M̃ is the mirror reflection of that of M (§6, eq. (6.2), p. 68).
10. The triple intersection form I^{1,1}(A,B,C) = ∫_X A ∧ B ∧ C is a topological invariant, whereas I^{2,1}(A,B,C) = ∫ Ω_{ijk} Ã^i ∧ B̃^j ∧ C̃^k ∧ Ω is only pseudo-topological, depending on the complex structure (§2.13, eqs. (2.78)-(2.81), p. 36).

# Caveats

- The paper is a type II / (2,2) SCFT treatment. It never discusses the heterotic string on M_4 × K, the gravitino variation, covariantly constant spinors, the standard embedding of the spin connection in the gauge group, E_8 → E_6 breaking, or the generation formula |χ|/2. Any tutorial attribution of these items to Greene 1997 would be wrong; cite it only for the geometric content (definition, holonomy, Hodge diamond, moduli counting, quintic, mirror symmetry) and for the worldsheet version of the Calabi-Yau argument.
- α' corrections: the Ricci-flatness condition is explicitly "to lowest order in sigma model perturbation theory", i.e. lowest order in α'/R^2 (p. 9 and footnote 3). The Kähler-moduli-space metric (∫ J ∧ J ∧ J) "suffers quantum corrections in string theory" (p. 35); the full Kähler-side Yukawa coupling (6.28) includes worldsheet instanton corrections. The complex-structure side is protected by a non-renormalisation theorem (p. 78).
- Holonomy: Greene takes "Calabi-Yau" to mean holonomy exactly SU(d), excluding proper subgroups (e.g. tori, K3 × T^2) which are "equally consistent as string compactifications" (p. 26).
- The statement "h^{0,s} = h^{s,0} = 0 for 1 < s < d" on p. 32 is printed with a strict inequality at the lower end; the displayed diamond nonetheless has h^{1,0} = h^{0,1} = 0, so read it as 0 < s < d.
- χ = 2(h^{1,1} − h^{2,1}) is not written as a displayed formula; only the general index theorem (2.75) and its numerical uses are. Cite (2.75) plus the diamond, not a non-existent equation number.
- Moduli-space metric: Greene only quotes the Kähler potentials and the special-Kähler prepotential formula (2.77) without derivation, referring to ref. [28] (Candelas-de la Ossa) for details. The Kähler cone condition (7.1) and the complexification by the B-field (7.2)-(7.3) are in §7.3, not in §2.12; the stringy moduli space extends beyond the Kähler cone (flops, pp. 82-83, 86-93).
- The moduli space factorisation M_CFT = M_cs × M_Kähler (7.5) holds only locally ("up to subtleties", p. 85).
- The "unique two-dimensional Calabi-Yau" (K3, h^{1,1} = 20) statement refers to a unique diffeomorphism type; the K3 lectures are referred to Aspinwall (p. 34).
- Index placement in the curvature formulas (2.42)-(2.43) and the mixing of real indices (μ, ν) and complex ones on p. 9 and p. 84 is informal; do not transcribe them into the tutorial as rigorous conventions.
