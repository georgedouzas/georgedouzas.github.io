For h^{1,1}_+ = 1, h^{1,1}_- = 0: -2 ln K = -3 ln[T + \bar T] (5.27). No-scale property (5.28): ∂_I K ∂_J K K^{IJ} = 3 for I = (G^a, T_α) (footnote 22: including τ gives 4).

# Equations the tutorial uses

(a) Flux quantisation, Eq. (2.5), p. 6:
$$\frac{1}{(2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'})^{p-1}}\int_{\Sigma_p}\hat F_p \in \mathbb{Z}$$
for any p-cycle Σ_p; footnote 2 (p. 5): "From the pure supergravity point of view, the charges are continuous parameters. In the quantum theory, they are quantized". Integer flux vectors N = (e_K, m^K), N_RR^{IIB} = (e_{RR K}, m^K_RR), Eq. (2.9).

(b) Tadpole cancellation, type IIB (Sec. 4.2, p. 26-27). Sourced Bianchi identity, Eq. (4.18):
$$dF_n = H_3\wedge F_{n-2} + (2\pi\sqrt{\alpha'})^{n-1}\rho^{\rm loc}_{8-n}$$
D3 tadpole, Eq. (4.21):
$$N_{D3} - \tfrac14 N_{O3} + \frac{1}{(2\pi)^4\alpha'^2}\int H_3\wedge F_3 = 0$$
Flux-induced D3 charge, Eq. (4.22):
$$N_{\rm flux} = \frac{1}{(2\pi)^4\alpha'^2}\int H_3\wedge F_3 = \left(e_K m^K_{RR} - m^K e_{K\,RR}\right) = N\,\eta\,N^t_{RR},\qquad \eta = \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\-1&0\end{pmatrix}$$
F-theory version, Eq. (4.23): N_{D3} + N_{flux} = χ(X_4)/24. D5 tadpole (4.20): N_{D5}(\tilde Σ_2) - N_{O5}(\tilde Σ_2) + (1/((2π)^2 α')) ∫_{Σ_4} H_3 ∧ F_1 = 0. IIA D6 tadpole (4.19): N_{D6}(\tilde Σ_3) - 2 N_{O6}(\tilde Σ_3) + (F_0/(2π√α')) ∫_{Σ_3} H_3 = 0. NS5 (4.24): dH_3 = ρ_{NS5}. Torus example (6.13), p. 51: N_{D3} + (1/2)(e_K m^K_RR - m^K e_{K RR}) = 16 for T^6/Z_2 with 2^6 O3-planes.

(c) GVW superpotential, Eq. (5.43), p. 42:
$$W_{O3/O7} = \int G_3\wedge\Omega = (e_{K\,RR} - i\tau e_K) Z^K - (m^K_{RR} - i\tau m^K)\mathcal{F}_K$$
with G_3 = F_3 - i e^{-φ} H_3 = \hat F_3 - τ H_3, Eq. (3.23) p. 15; periods Z^K = ∫_{A_K} Ω, F_K = ∫_{B^K} Ω, Ω = Z^K α_K - F_K β^K, Eqs. (5.7)-(5.8). Kähler potential: (5.26) above. "This superpotential depends on the complex structure moduli through Ω, and on the dilaton-axion, by the definition of G_3. On the contrary, the Kähler moduli ... do not appear" (p. 42). Covariant derivatives, Eq. (5.44):
$$D_\tau W = \tfrac{i}{2}e^{\phi}\int\bar G_3\wedge\Omega + iG_{ab}b^ab^b W,\quad D_{T_\alpha}W = -2\frac{v^\alpha}{\mathcal K}W,\quad D_{G^a}W = 2iG_{ab}b^bW,\quad D_{z^k}W = \int G_3\wedge\chi_k$$
using ∂Ω/∂z^k = k_k Ω + χ_k (5.45). Resulting no-scale potential, Eq. (5.46):
$$V_{O3/O7} = \frac{18 i e^{\phi}}{\mathcal K^2\int\Omega\wedge\bar\Omega}\left(\int\bar G_3\wedge\Omega\int G_3\wedge\bar\Omega + G^{kl}\int G_3\wedge\chi_k\int\bar G_3\wedge\bar\chi_l\right)\ \ge 0$$
Supersymmetric Minkowski conditions, Eq. (5.47)/(6.1): W = 0, D_I W = 0 ⇒ ∫ G_3∧Ω = 0, ∫ \bar G_3∧Ω = 0, ∫ G_3∧χ_k = 0, i.e. G_3 has no (0,3), (3,0), (1,2) pieces: G_3 is (2,1), automatically primitive on a CY (no 1-forms). ISD condition, Eq. (3.24), p. 16: ∗G_3 = i G_3 and G_{(0,3)} = 0 for type B susy; GKP's non-susy generalisation (p. 29, 61): ISD allows (2,1) primitive plus (0,3); the (0,3) piece breaks supersymmetry since then W ≠ 0 and D_T W ≠ 0.

(d) No-go theorem (Sec. 4.1, p. 24-25, Maldacena-Nuñez [27] and GKP [21]). From R_{μν} = T_{μν} - (1/8) e^{2A} \tilde g_{μν} T^L_L (4.2), contracting:
$$\tilde R + e^{2A}\left(-T^\mu_\mu + \tfrac12 T^L_L\right) = 4\left(\nabla^2A + 2(\nabla A)^2\right) = 2e^{-2A}\nabla^2e^{2A}\qquad (4.3)$$
With \hat T ≡ -T^μ_μ + (1/2)T^L_L = (1/2)(-T^μ_μ + T^m_m) (4.4): internal n-form flux gives \hat T_int = ((n-1)/(2n)) F^2 ≥ 0 (4.7), external flux gives \hat T_ext = -((9-n)/(2n)) F^2 ≥ 0 (4.9) (F^2 ≤ 0 for temporal components). "Multiplying (4.3) by e^{2A} and integrating over the internal manifold, we get the (in)famous no-go theorems: the right hand side vanishes, while the left hand side is non-negative for de Sitter or Minkowski spaces" (p. 25). Hence dS ruled out; Minkowski only with F_1; AdS has Λ ∝ flux^2. Loophole, Eq. (4.11)-(4.13): with localized sources, \tilde R + e^{2A}(1/2)(\hat T^{flux} + \hat T^{loc}) = 2 e^{-2A} ∇^2 e^{2A}; Minkowski requires Σ_i ∫ (\hat T^{flux}_i + \hat T^{loc}_i) = 0 (4.12); a p-brane wrapped on a (p-3)-cycle gives \hat T^{loc} = ((7-p)/2) T_p δ(Σ) (4.13), positive for p < 7, so "In order to compactify we need to include negative tension objects. String theory does have such negative tension objects: orientifold planes, and can therefore evade no-go theorems" (p. 25). The theorem "applies to any solution, regardless of its supersymmetry properties" (p. 25). GKP refinement, Eq. (4.34) (Einstein frame): ∇̂^2(e^{4A} - f) = e^{2A+φ}(1/6)|∗G_3 - iG_3|^2 + e^{-6A}|∂(e^{4A} - f)|^2 + (2π)^8 α'^4 e^{2A}[(1/2)\hat T^{loc} - T_3 ρ_3^{loc}]; with the BPS bound (1/2)\hat T^{loc} ≥ T_3 ρ_3 (4.33) the integrated equation forces e^{4A} = f, ISD G_3, and saturation of (4.33). Warp factor (4.32): -∇̂^2 e^{-4A} = (2π)^4 α'^2 ρ_3(x) + (e^φ/12) G_{mnp} \bar G^{mnp}.

(e) KKLT (Sec. 7.3-7.4, p. 60-63). Setup: IIB CY O3 orientifold, h^{2,1} arbitrary, h^{1,1}_+ = 1, h^{1,1}_- = 0, single Kähler modulus T with Re T = (1/2) K^{2/3} ~ R^4, K = -3 ln[T + \bar T] (5.27). Complex structure moduli and τ fixed by fluxes with masses m ~ α'/R^3, then integrated out. Non-perturbative superpotential (7.6): W_np = B_n e^{-2π n^α T_α}. Total (7.7):
$$W = W_0 + B e^{-2\pi T}$$
Setting the axion ρ = 0 and σ ≡ Re T = (1/2) K^{2/3}, the susy minimum D_T W = 0 is at (7.8):
$$W_0 = -B e^{-2\pi\sigma_{cr}}\left(1 + \frac{4\pi}{3}\sigma_{cr}\right)$$
with volume K_cr = (2σ_cr)^{3/2}, large for small |W_0|. AdS minimum (7.9):
$$V_{\min} = -3(e^K|W|^2)_{\min} = -\frac{2\pi^2B^2e^{-4\pi\sigma_{cr}}}{3\sigma_{cr}}$$
Uplift (7.10): anti-D3 branes at the bottom of the warped throat add V_{\bar D3} = D/σ^3 = D/(Re T)^3, D ∝ n e^{4A}. Full potential (7.11):
$$V = \frac{B\pi e^{-2\pi\sigma}}{\sigma^2}\left(Be^{-2\pi\sigma}\left(1 + \frac{2\pi}{3}\sigma\right) + W_0\right) + \frac{D}{\sigma^3}$$
("few terms because the no scale structure ... gives the cancellation K^{T\bar T} ∂_T K ∂_{\bar T} K |W|^2 - 3|W|^2 = 0"). Figure 1 (p. 63): V × 10^15 vs σ for W_0 = -10^{-4}, B = 1, D = 3 × 10^{-9}, with 2π → 0.1 in the exponent; local dS minimum near σ ≈ 110, barrier, runaway to infinite volume. The paper writes the exponent as e^{-2πT}, not e^{iaρ}; the "A e^{iaρ}" form of the task prompt is KKLT's own notation and does not appear here. Required: W_0 ≠ 0 (from a (0,3) piece of G_3) and e^K|W_0|^2 ≪ 1 (p. 61); consistency regime (7.5): W_0 ~ W_np ⇒ V_{W_np} ≫ V_{K_p}.

(f) Moduli counting and which fluxes fix what. CY: one (0,0), one (3,0), one (0,3), one (3,3) harmonic form, h^{1,1} (1,1)- and (2,2)-forms, h^{2,1} (2,1)- and (1,2)-forms, total 2h^{2,1} + 2 three-forms, no 1- or 5-forms (Sec. 5.1, p. 30, Table 5.1). # Attributable statements

1. (Sec. 2, p. 5-6) Dirac quantisation (2.5): fluxes through any p-cycle are integers in units of (2π√α')^{p-1}; electric/magnetic integer fluxes (e_K, m^K) organise into symplectic vectors with Sp(2h^{2,1}+2, Z) invariance, Eq. (2.9).
2. (Sec. 4.1, p. 24-25) Every n-form flux, internal or spacetime-filling, contributes positively to \hat T (Eqs. (4.7), (4.9)) except F_1/F_9 (zero) and F_0 (handled separately in [27]); integrating (4.3) over a compact internal space rules out warped dS and Minkowski compactifications without sources, independently of supersymmetry.
3. (Sec. 4.1, p. 25) The loophole is negative-tension sources: Dp-branes with p < 7 give \hat T^{loc} = ((7-p)/2) T_p δ(Σ) > 0, so orientifold planes (charge -2^{p-5} of a Dp-brane, footnote 19) are needed; D7-branes/F-theory evade the Einstein-equation no-go but are bounded by the induced D3 charge, (4.23).
4. (Sec. 4.2, p. 26) For supersymmetric backgrounds the no-go theorems come from the integrated Bianchi identities, which are stronger than the Einstein-trace condition: "Einstein's equation gives no-go theorems based on the effective tension of the fluxes, while Bianchi identities and the equations of motion for the fluxes restrict the magnetic and electric charges".
5. (Sec. 4.3, p. 29) GKP: assuming the BPS inequality (1/2)\hat T^{loc} ≥ T_3 ρ_3 for all sources, a warped Minkowski compactification exists only if e^{4A} = f (five-form tied to warp factor), G_3 is ISD, and the inequality is saturated; D3/O3/D7/O7 saturate, D5 and anti-D3 satisfy but do not saturate, O5 and anti-O3 violate it.
6. (Sec. 5.3, p. 38) The flux expansion in the harmonic forms of the fluxless CY is justified when the flux energy scale N_flux α'/R^3 is well below the KK scale 1/R, i.e. R ≫ √N_flux × string length, the same regime needed to neglect α' corrections; the truncation yields N=2 (or N=1) gauged supergravity.
7. (Sec. 5.4, p. 42) The GVW superpotential W = ∫ G_3 ∧ Ω depends on complex structure moduli and τ only; the resulting potential (5.46) is positive semi-definite by no-scale, vanishes exactly when G_3 is (2,1), and reproduces the gauged-supergravity potential from KK reduction.
8. (Sec. 6.1, p. 49-50) In IIB CY O3 orientifolds, fluxes fix all h^{2,1}_- complex structure moduli and the axio-dilaton via 2h^{2,1}_- + 2 real equations; the Kähler moduli (in particular the overall volume) are not stabilised, "since as GKP showed, all the type B conditions are invariant under rescalings of the metric" (p. 57).
9. (Sec. 7.3, p. 61) KKLT "should be taken only as a toy model of complete moduli stabilization": the two-step integrating-out of heavy moduli can be inconsistent (tachyons), Kähler-potential corrections are neglected (justified only for W_0 ~ W_np, Eq. (7.5)), and h^{1,1} = 1 lacks enough divisors for the instanton contributions [178].
10. (Sec. 7.4, p. 63) The uplifted KKLT dS vacuum is metastable with runaway to infinite volume; its lifetime is long compared to the cosmological time scale and shorter than the recurrence time t_r ~ e^S.

# Caveats

- Warping and backreaction (p. 43, p. 52, p. 70): once (2,1) flux is turned on "the back-reacted geometry is no longer a product, but it is a warped product, and the internal manifold is no longer Calabi-Yau"; e^{2A} ~ 1 + O(g_s N α'^2 r^{-4}), so warping is dropped at large radius/weak coupling. "An honest computation of supersymmetry conditions via a superpotential and Kähler potential should nevertheless include the warping"; Refs. [144, 146] claim the warp factor affects K but not W (footnote 30, p. 53). In all explicit models "the back-reaction of the geometry is basically neglected" (Sec. 9, p. 70).
- Flux vs KK scale (p. 38): the harmonic truncation requires R ≫ √N_flux ℓ_s; fluxes make the internal manifold non-Ricci-flat and a CY is "no longer a solution to the equations of motion".
- Trust regime (Sec. 9, p. 71): need string-frame volume ≫ KK and string scales, small g_s, "or more the more restrictive condition g_s N_flux ≪ 1"; cosmological constants of non-susy solutions are subject to corrections of the same order and "cannot therefore be fully trusted".
- IIA vs IIB (p. 53, p. 57): the CY O6 superpotential (5.50) is formally used though IIA susy vacua with SU(3) structure are never CY (W_1, W_2 or W_3 ≠ 0); legitimate only when the structure group is further reduced (tori) and h^1 = 0. In IIB the flux integers enter the tadpole so cannot be scaled up freely; in IIA the F_2/F_4 fluxes do not enter the tadpole, enabling the large-N scaling (6.36).
- Corrections hierarchy (Sec. 7.1, p. 58-59): K receives perturbative corrections at every order, W only non-perturbative ones; neglecting K_p while keeping W_np is consistent only for W_0 = 0 or W_0 ~ W_np, Eq. (7.5); α'^3 R^4 terms break no-scale, leading at O(α'^3/R^6) [162, 163, 174]; large-volume minima can coexist with the KKLT one.
- KKLT fine tuning (p. 61, p. 64): e^K|W_0|^2 ≪ 1 must be tuned (examples with ~10^{-3} exist); the uplift constant D needs extra tuning; no explicit dS model was constructed at the time.
- Instanton zero modes (Sec. 7.2, p. 60): the non-perturbative superpotential needs divisors with holomorphic Euler characteristic χ(D) = 1 (relaxed by fluxes); B_n depends on complex structure moduli.
- Tori subtlety (p. 50): on T^6 the structure group is trivial, extra harmonic 1-forms exist, so primitivity J ∧ G_3 = 0 must be imposed separately (it is automatic on a CY) and it is what fixes some Kähler moduli; "not all complex structure deformations correspond to deformations of the metric" (footnote 29).
- The KST example fixes g_s = 1/√3, "where perturbative corrections are important" (p. 53).
- Notational: F^{(10)} vs \hat F vs internal F (footnote 1, p. 5); in most of the text (2π)^2 α' = 1 so flux integers appear without prefactors.
