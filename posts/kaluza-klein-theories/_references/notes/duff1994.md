# Conventions

- Metric signature: $(-,+,+,+,+)$ in five dimensions (stated explicitly, Sec. 1, p.2). Internal space has Euclidean signature, $M_4$ has "the usual signature" (Sec. 5, p.14).
- Riemann/Ricci sign conventions: NOT stated anywhere in the paper. Only $\hat R_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=0$ and the action with $+\hat R$ appear; the sign of $R$ is fixed implicitly by requiring the reduced action (8) to have the standard $-\tfrac12(\partial\phi)^2$ kinetic sign with the stated signature.
- Index conventions: hatted quantities are five-(or $D$-)dimensional, $\hat\mu=0,1,2,3,4$; unhatted are four-dimensional, $\mu=0,1,2,3$; $\hat x^{\hat\mu}=(x^\mu,y)$. In Sec. 5 the internal indices are $n=4,5,\dots,D-1$ and the $4+k$ split is $\hat x^{\hat\mu}=(x^\mu,y^n)$ (p.15).
- 5D KK metric ansatz, Eq. (2), p.2 ("Kaluza then made the $4+1$ split"):
  $$\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=e^{\phi/\sqrt3}\begin{pmatrix} g_{\mu\nu}+e^{-\sqrt3\phi}A_\mu A_\nu & e^{-\sqrt3\phi}A_\mu\\ e^{-\sqrt3\phi}A_\nu & e^{-\sqrt3\phi}\end{pmatrix}\qquad(2)$$
  The scalar enters purely through exponentials: an overall conformal factor $e^{\phi/\sqrt3}$ and $e^{-\sqrt3\phi}$ multiplying the $A_\mu$ and $yy$ components. Hence $\hat g_{44}=e^{-2\phi/\sqrt3}$ and $\hat g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\phi/\sqrt3}g_{\mu\nu}+e^{-2\phi/\sqrt3}A_\mu A_\nu$.
- Frame: the overall $e^{\phi/\sqrt3}$ factor is precisely the Weyl rescaling that makes $g_{\mu\nu}$ the 4D Einstein-frame metric: the reduced action (8) has a pure $R$ with no scalar prefactor and a canonically normalised dilaton kinetic term. The paper does not use the words "Einstein frame", but in Sec. 3 (p.10) it distinguishes $g_{\mu\nu}$ from the Weyl-rescaled $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\sqrt3\phi}g_{\mu\nu}$, which it calls (p.26) the "fundamental metric" coupling to the worldline of an electrically charged particle; the "dual metric" is $e^{-\sqrt3\phi}g_{\mu\nu}$.
- Field strength: $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$ (p.5, after Eq. (8)).
- Coupling normalisation: $\hat S=\frac{1}{2\hat\kappa^2}\int d^5\hat x\sqrt{-\hat g}\,\hat R$ (Eq. (3)); 4D coupling $\kappa$ with $2\pi\kappa^2=m\hat\kappa^2$ (p.5), where $m$ is the inverse radius of $S^1$ ($0\le my\le 2\pi$, p.3). The Maxwell term carries $\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}\cdot\frac14 e^{-\sqrt3\phi}F^2$, i.e. $A_\mu$ is NOT canonically normalised; it has gravitational dimension and the gauge coupling appears in the charge formula (16). In Sec. 8 (p.27) Duff sets $\kappa^2=16\pi$ and normalises the fundamental charge by $Q^2=e^2/2\pi$.
- Scalar-Maxwell coupling parameter: KK corresponds to $e^{-a\phi}F^{\mu\nu}F_{\mu\nu}$ with $a=\sqrt3$ (p.30); $a=1$ is the supersymmetric dilaton black hole, $a=0$ Reissner-Nordstrom.

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) The 5D ansatz
Line element Eq. (1), p.2: $d\hat s^2=\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}d\hat x^{\hat\mu}d\hat x^{\hat\nu}$. Metric ansatz Eq. (2), p.2 — quoted in full in Conventions above. 5D action Eq. (3), p.4:
$$\hat S=\frac{1}{2\hat\kappa^2}\int d^5\hat x\,\sqrt{-\hat g}\,\hat R\qquad(3)$$
5D general coordinate invariance Eq. (4), p.4:
$$\delta\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=\partial_{\hat\mu}\hat\xi^{\hat\rho}\hat g_{\hat\rho\hat\nu}+\partial_{\hat\nu}\hat\xi^{\hat\rho}\hat g_{\hat\rho\hat\mu}+\hat\xi^{\hat\rho}\partial_{\hat\rho}\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}\qquad(4)$$
Fourier expansions Eq. (5), p.4: $g_{\mu\nu}(x,y)=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}g_{\mu\nu n}(x)e^{inmy}$, same for $A_\mu(x,y)$ and $\phi(x,y)$, with reality condition Eq. (6): $g^*{}_{\mu\nu n}(x)=g_{\mu\nu\,-n}(x)$ etc. Gauge parameters Eq. (7), p.5: $\hat\xi^\mu(x,y)=\sum_n\xi^\mu{}_n(x)e^{inmy}$, $\hat\xi^4(x,y)=\sum_n\xi^4{}_n(x)e^{inmy}$, with $\hat\xi^{*\hat\mu}{}_n=\hat\xi^{\hat\mu}{}_{-n}$.

## (b) The reduced 4D action (zero modes)
Eq. (8), p.5, obtained by substituting (2) and (7) into (3), integrating over $y$ and keeping $n=0$ (0 subscripts dropped):
$$S=\frac{1}{2\kappa^2}\int d^4x\sqrt{-g}\Big[R-\frac12\partial_\mu\phi\,\partial^\mu\phi-\frac14 e^{-\sqrt3\phi}F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}\Big]\qquad(8)$$
with $2\pi\kappa^2=m\hat\kappa^2$ and $F_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu A_\nu-\partial_\nu A_\mu$. Note that with Duff's conventions the dilaton kinetic term has coefficient $\tfrac12$ (canonical) and the exponent in front of $F^2$ is $-\sqrt3\phi$. The paper does not display the intermediate computation; it also does not write the reduced field equations, only states (p.3) that the $4+1$ split of $\hat R_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=0$ yields the Einstein, Maxwell and massless Klein-Gordon equations.

## (c) Symmetries of the reduced action, including the gauge transformation from the $x^5$ shift
From (4), the action (8) is invariant under (p.5):
- 4D general coordinate transformations with parameter $\xi^\mu{}_0$, Eq. (9):
  $$\delta g_{\mu\nu}=\partial_\mu\xi^\rho g_{\rho\nu}+\partial_\nu\xi^\rho g_{\mu\rho}+\xi^\rho\partial_\rho g_{\mu\nu},\quad \delta A_\mu=\partial_\mu\xi^\rho A_\rho+\xi^\rho\partial_\rho A_\mu,\quad \delta\phi=\xi^\rho\partial_\rho\phi\qquad(9)$$
- local gauge transformations with parameter $\xi^4{}_0$ (the $y$-shift), Eq. (10):
  $$\delta A_\mu=\partial_\mu\xi^4\qquad(10)$$
- global scale transformations with parameter $\lambda$, Eq. (11):
  $$\delta A_\mu=\lambda A_\mu,\qquad\delta\phi=-2\lambda/\sqrt3\qquad(11)$$
Vacuum Eq. (12), p.6: $\langle g_{\mu\nu}\rangle=\eta_{\mu\nu},\ \langle A_\mu\rangle=0,\ \langle\phi\rangle=\phi_0$; symmetry of the vacuum is Poincare $\times\,R$. Once $n\neq0$ modes are kept, the scale symmetry is seen to be the 5D rescaling Eq. (13) $\delta\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=-\tfrac12\lambda\hat g_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}$ combined with the coordinate transformation Eq. (14) $\xi^4=-\lambda y/m$, forbidden by periodicity; so $\phi_0$ is only a pseudo-Goldstone boson (p.6).
The infinite tower of local transformations (7) corresponds to the global algebra Eq. (15), p.7:
$$P^\mu{}_n=e^{inmy}\partial^\mu,\quad M^{\mu\nu}{}_n=e^{inmy}(x^\mu\partial^\nu-x^\nu\partial^\mu),\quad Q_n=ie^{inmy}\partial/\partial(my)\qquad(15)$$
a Kac-Moody-Virasoro generalisation of the Poincare/gauge algebra [7].

## (d) The KK spectrum
Eq. (16), p.7: an infinite tower of charged, massive spin-2 particles with
$$e_n=n\sqrt2\,\kappa m,\qquad m_n=|n|\,m\qquad(16)$$
Mechanism (p.7): for $n\neq0$ the $A_{\mu n}$ and $\phi_n$ are Goldstone bosons eaten by $g_{\mu\nu n}$ (2+2+1 = 5 degrees of freedom of a massive spin 2); "the massive spectrum is pure spin two" [8]. Radius-coupling-Planck relation (p.8): identifying the fundamental unit $e=\sqrt2\kappa m$ with the electron charge forces $m\sim$ the Planck mass $10^{19}$ GeV, i.e. a circle radius of Planck size $10^{-35}$ m. Charge conjugation is parity $y\to-y$ in the fifth dimension (p.8). The same charge/mass ratio $\sqrt2\kappa m\ge e$ is the extremality bound Eq. (19), p.9, of the KK black hole (17)-(18), which is the seed of Sec. 8's claim that the massive KK states are extreme black holes.


## (d') The KK black hole (Sec. 3, pp.9-10), referenced by the spectrum discussion
Electrically charged solution of (8), Eq. (17), p.9:
$$ds^2=-\Delta_+\Delta_-^{-1/2}dt^2+\Delta_+^{-1}\Delta_-^{-1/2}dr^2+r^2\Delta_-^{3/2}d\Omega^2,\quad e^{2\phi}=\Delta_-^{\sqrt3},\quad e^{-\sqrt3\phi}{*F}=(r_+r_-)^{1/2}\epsilon_2\qquad(17)$$
with $\Delta_\pm=1-r_\pm/r$ and $\epsilon_2$ the volume form on $S^2$. Charge and ADM mass, Eq. (18): $\sqrt2\kappa e/4\pi=(r_+r_-)^{1/2}$, $2\kappa^2 m/4\pi=2r_+-r_-$. Horizon condition $r_+\ge r_-$ gives the bound Eq. (19): $\sqrt2\kappa m\ge e$, saturated in the extreme limit $r_+=r_-$, Eq. (20): $ds^2=-\Delta_-^{1/2}dt^2+\Delta_-^{-1/2}dr^2+r^2\Delta_-^{3/2}d\Omega^2$. Magnetic solution Eq. (21): same metric, $e^{-2\phi}=\Delta_-^{\sqrt3}$, $F=(r_+r_-)^{1/2}\epsilon_2$, with $\sqrt2\kappa g/4\pi=(r_+r_-)^{1/2}$, Eq. (22); its extreme limit is the KK monopole [15,17,18]. Weyl-rescaled extreme monopole, Eq. (23): $d\tilde s^2=-\Delta_-^{-1}dt^2+\Delta_-^{-2}dr^2+r^2d\Omega^2$, regular at $r=r_-$.

## (e) Non-abelian generalisation (Sec. 5, pp.13-16)
$4+k$ split $\hat x^{\hat\mu}=(x^\mu,y^n)$, $\hat\mu=0,\dots,D-1$, $n=4,\dots,D-1$. Lowest Fourier term of the off-diagonal metric, Eq. (27), p.15:
$$\hat g_{\mu n}(x,y)=A_\mu{}^i(x)K_n{}^i(y)+\dots\qquad(27)$$
where $K^i=K^{ni}\partial_n$ are Killing vectors obeying the Lie algebra of $G$, Eq. (28):
$$[K^i,K^j]=f^{ij}{}_k K^k\qquad(28)$$
Special coordinate transformation Eq. (29): $\hat\xi^{\hat\mu}(x,y)=(0,\epsilon^i(x)K^{mi}(y))$ with $\epsilon^i(x)$ arbitrary. Then (4) and (27) give Eq. (30), p.15:
$$\delta A_\mu{}^i(x)=\partial_\mu\epsilon^i(x)-f^i{}_{jk}A_\mu{}^j(x)\epsilon^k(x)\qquad(30)$$
"This is precisely the transformation law for a Yang-Mills field with gauge group $G$. Hence $G$ is a subgroup of the $D$-dimensional general coordinate group" (p.16). Statement of the gauge group (p.13-14): "the gauge group $G$ obtained in $D=4$ was connected to the isometry group of the extra dimensions"; "any $M_k$ with $G$ as its isometry group will do, i.e., any metric admitting the Killing vectors of $G$"; for homogeneous spaces $M_k=G/H$ with $H$ the isotropy subgroup, $k=\dim G-\dim H$, so one is "no longer obliged to have only one gauge boson for each extra dimension"; a group manifold with bi-invariant metric has isometry $G\times G$, so $S^3$ gives $SU(2)\times SU(2)$, not merely $SU(2)$ (p.15). Note: the paper gives only the transformation law (30); it does NOT display the reduced non-abelian action or the Yang-Mills coupling in terms of the internal volume.
Torus case (Sec. 4, p.11): ground state $M_4\times T^k$ with $G=[U(1)]^k$; massless modes: 1 spin 2 (2 d.o.f.), $k$ spin 1 ($2k$), $k(k+1)/2$ spin 0; total $(4+k)(1+k)/2$ matches a graviton in $D=4+k$; scalars are the moduli of $T^k$ parametrising $GL(k,R)/SO(k)$ [21].

## (f) Consistent truncation and sphere/coset examples
- 5D: "Kaluza-Klein theory is consistent by virtue of having an infinite tower of such states. Any attempt to truncate to a finite non-zero number of massive modes would reintroduce the inconsistency [10]" (p.8). The $n=0$ truncation is the one kept, and "this truncated $n=0$ theory has lost all memory of the periodicity in $y$", so its gauge group is $R$ rather than $U(1)$ (p.6).
- $D>5$ pure gravity: $\hat R_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=0$ is consistent with $M_4\times T^k$ (p.11), but "it was necessary to augment pure gravity with matter fields in order to achieve a satisfactory compactification" (Cremmer et al [46], p.14) on non-flat $M_k$; spontaneous compactification = stable ground-state solutions of the field equations of product form $M_4\times M_k$ (p.14).
- Sphere/coset: Freund-Rubin [53] 3-form mechanism compactifies 7 of 11 dimensions; $(AdS_4)\times S^7$ with $S^7$ isometry $SO(8)$ gives $D=4$ $SO(8)$ gauge invariance; $S^7$ admits 8 Killing spinors hence $N=8$ supersymmetry; conjectured [55,56,57] massless supermultiplet spins $(2,3/2,1,1/2,0^+,0^-)$ in $SO(8)$ reps $(1,8,28,56,35,35)$ corresponds to gauged $N=8$ supergravity of de Wit-Nicolai [58] (p.18). Salam-Strathdee [8] harmonic expansions on coset spaces, "essential for the understanding of the massive modes" (p.18).
- Sec. 8, p.28: the heterotic action (24) "can be consistently truncated by keeping the metric $g_{\mu\nu}$, just one field strength ($F^1$ say), and one scalar field $\phi$ via the ansatz $\Phi=\phi/\sqrt3$ and $M_{11}=e^{2\phi/\sqrt3}=M_{77}^{-1}$", upon which (24) reduces to (8).
- No explicit statement about $S^7$ or other sphere reductions being consistent truncations (in the modern technical sense) is made; only the term "consistently truncated" above for the toroidal heterotic case.

## (g) Kaluza's and Klein's original papers
- Kaluza [1] (1921): postulated a fifth dimension, made the $4+1$ split (2) of 5D pure gravity with no $y$-dependence; fields $g_{\mu\nu},A_\mu,\phi$ transform as tensor, vector, scalar under 4D diffeomorphisms; the "Kaluza miracle": the split of $\hat R_{\hat\mu\hat\nu}=0$ gives Einstein + Maxwell + massless Klein-Gordon equations (pp.2-3). The scalar "was considered an embarrassment in 1919, and was (inconsistently) set equal to zero", later revived by Jordan [4], Thiry [5], Brans-Dicke [6] (footnote 3, p.2).
- Two drawbacks of Kaluza (p.3): $y$-dependence suppressed for no good reason; why has the fifth dimension not been seen.
- Klein [2,3] (1926): took the extra dimension seriously, assumed circular topology $R^4\times S^1$, $0\le my\le2\pi$ (hosepipe analogy, pp.3-4); "Klein explained (for the first time) the quantization of electric charge [3]" (p.8) via (16); the small radius answers Kaluza's second question (p.8). Klein [37] (1939) "came close to discovering non-abelian gauge fields" while investigating $D=5$ geometries (p.13, footnote 4 citing Gross [38]); "One can only speculate ... if ... Klein had applied his own ideas to a sphere instead of a circle" (p.16).

# Attributable statements

1. Kaluza's 4+1 split of the 5D metric is Eq. (2), with the scalar entering through $e^{\phi/\sqrt3}$ and $e^{-\sqrt3\phi}$ so that the reduced action (8) has canonical $R$ and $\phi$ kinetic terms and a dilaton-dependent Maxwell coupling $e^{-\sqrt3\phi}F^2$ (Sec. 1-2, pp.2, 5).
2. Maxwell's equations follow from 5D Einstein equations: "Thus Maxwell's theory of electromagnetism is an inevitable consequence of Einstein's general theory of relativity, given that one is willing to buy the idea of a fifth dimension" (Sec. 1, p.3).
3. A KK theory describes not only an infinite number of 4D fields (textbook) but also an infinite number of 4D symmetries, a Kac-Moody-Virasoro extension of Poincare $\times$ gauge, Eq. (15) (Sec. 2, pp.4-7, citing Dolan-Duff [7]).
4. Masslessness of graviton, photon and dilaton: general covariance, gauge invariance, and Goldstone boson of the global scale invariance (11), respectively; with $n\neq0$ modes the dilaton is only a pseudo-Goldstone boson (Sec. 2, p.6).
5. The massive KK tower is pure spin 2, with $e_n=n\sqrt2\kappa m$, $m_n=|n|m$; the massive gauge fields eat $A_{\mu n}$ and $\phi_n$ (Sec. 2, p.7, Eq. (16)).
6. Klein explained charge quantisation for the first time; identifying $e=\sqrt2\kappa m$ with the electron charge forces $m$ to be the Planck mass and the radius to be $10^{-35}$ m (Sec. 2, p.8).
7. The KK theory with its full infinite tower is consistent, but any truncation to a finite non-zero number of massive modes reintroduces the inconsistencies of coupling massive spin-2 fields to gravity/electromagnetism (Sec. 2, p.8, citing [9,10]).
8. Non-abelian: the 4D gauge group is the isometry group of the internal space; any $M_k$ admitting the Killing vectors of $G$ will do, in particular cosets $G/H$ with $k=\dim G-\dim H$; the Yang-Mills transformation law (30) follows from $D$-dimensional diffeomorphisms (29) via the ansatz (27) (Sec. 5, pp.14-16).
9. "What we perceive to be internal symmetries in four dimensions are really spacetime symmetries in the extra dimensions" (Sec. 5, p.16).
10. Historical irony: the heterotic string demands extra dimensions a la KK but takes its Yang-Mills fields from the $D=10$ formulation and compactifies on Calabi-Yau manifolds with no isometries at all; nonetheless all 496 gauge bosons can be understood as KK gauge bosons from compactification on a group manifold (Sec. 7, pp.20-21, citing [62,63]).
11. Extreme electrically charged KK black holes (17)-(20) have exactly the charge/mass ratio (16) of the massive KK states, and in the $N=4$ heterotic setting the $N_R=1/2$, $N_L=1$ states are extreme KK black holes (Secs. 3, 8; pp.9-10, 28-29).

# Caveats

- The scalar: setting $\phi=0$ in Kaluza's ansatz is inconsistent (footnote 3, p.2); the dilaton is forced by the field equations and is massless as a (pseudo-)Goldstone boson. The $e^{-\sqrt3\phi}$ coupling means the 4D monopole metric $g_{\mu\nu}$ has a curvature singularity at $r=r_-$ even though the 5D metric is regular; the Weyl-rescaled $\tilde g_{\mu\nu}=e^{\sqrt3\phi}g_{\mu\nu}$ (Eq. (23), p.10) removes it, so frame choice matters physically and "the five-dimensional perspective" is not an unnecessary luxury (Sec. 3, p.10).
- Consistency/truncation: keeping only the $n=0$ modes is fine; the $n=0$ theory has gauge group $R$ not $U(1)$ (p.6); truncation to finitely many massive modes is inconsistent (p.8); the paper does not discuss consistency of sphere reductions beyond citing Freund-Rubin and Salam-Strathdee.
- Gyromagnetic ratio: massive KK modes have $g=1$ [11], which seems to give unacceptable high-energy Compton scattering [12]; string theory cures the high-energy behaviour (p.8, footnote 7 p.31).
- Compactification mechanism: in early non-abelian work "little attention was paid to the question of why the extra dimensions were compactified and whether this was consistent with the higher-dimensional field equations"; pure gravity does not suffice for a satisfactory compactification on curved $M_k$, matter is needed (Cremmer et al [46]) (Sec. 5, p.14).
- "Stone soup" (Weinberg [52], p.17): the promise of unification from pure gravity became gravity plus arbitrary matter in $D>4$ with the extra headache of choosing $D$; $D=11$ supergravity is singled out as "marble soup" because supersymmetry fixes the matter content.
- Chirality: Witten [54] pointed out that with $D=11$ supergravity "quarks and leptons do not fit into the right representations, in particular the four dimensional theory cannot be chiral" (p.18); superstrings looked better from the point of view of chirality (p.19); the $E_8\times E_8$ heterotic string on a Calabi-Yau gives chiral families (p.20).
- Cosmological constant: the Freund-Rubin ground state is $AdS_4\times S^7$ (p.18), i.e. a negative cosmological constant; "fermion condensates and the cosmological constant" are listed (p.21) among KK-supergravity-era discoveries that still influence string theory, without further detail.
- Vacuum degeneracy: finding the right compactification is equivalent to finding the right $c=9$ superconformal field theory, with billions of candidates, unresolved in weak-coupling perturbation theory (p.22-23).
- Minor typos in the original: "embarassment", "signaature", "compactfied", "Poincare" without accent, "the the"; the "1919" vs "1921" of Kaluza's paper (text says 1919 for the idea, reference [1] is 1921).
