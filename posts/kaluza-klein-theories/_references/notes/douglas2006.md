# Conventions

- Signature: mostly-plus. The 4d metric is $\eta_{\mu\nu}$ with 10d warped ansatz $ds^2_{10} = e^{2A(y)}\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu + e^{-2A(y)}\tilde g_{mn}(y)dy^m dy^n$ (eq. 41, p. 29); the Freund-Rubin ansatz is $ds^2=\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu + R^2 g_{mn}(y)dy^m dy^n$ (eq. 15, p. 13). Units $\hbar=1$; the 6d toy uses $M_6$ units.
- KK reduction of the Planck scale (unnumbered, p. 6): $M_{P,D}^{D-2}\int d^Dx\sqrt g R^{(D)} \to (\mathrm{Vol}M)\, M_{P,D}^{D-2}\int d^4x\sqrt g R^{(4)}$, with $M_{KK}=1/\mathrm{Vol}(M)^{1/(D-4)}$ and $M_{P,4}^2 = M_{P,D}^{D-2}/M_{KK}^{D-4}$ (eq. 1, p. 6).
- Type IIB string-frame action (eq. 36, p. 29): $L=\frac{1}{2\kappa_{10}^2}\int d^{10}x(-G)^{1/2}e^{-2S}\Big(R+4\partial_\mu S\partial^\mu S-\frac12|F_1|^2-\frac{1}{12}G_3\cdot\bar G_3-\frac{1}{4\cdot 5!}\tilde F_5^2\Big)+\frac{1}{8i\kappa_{10}^2}\int e^S C_4\wedge G_3\wedge\bar G_3+S_{loc}$. Here $S$ is the dilaton ($g_s=e^S$ on p. 29; later the text writes $g_s=e^\phi$ for IIA, p. 38, so the symbol $\phi$ is overloaded).
- $G_3=F_3-\phi H_3$ (eq. 37), axio-dilaton $\phi=C_0+ie^{-S}$ (eq. 38; also written $\phi=C_0+i/g_s$ on p. 31 and $\tau$ in Sec. V), $\tilde F_5=F_5-\frac12 C_2\wedge H_3+\frac12 B_2\wedge F_3$ (eq. 39), self-duality $\tilde F_5=*\tilde F_5$ (eq. 40).
- Compact fluxes are integrally quantised: $F_3,H_3\in H^3(M,\mathbb Z)$ (eq. 43) with the normalisation $F_3=-(2\pi)^2\alpha'(f_a\alpha^a+f_{a+h_{2,1}+1}\beta_a)$, $H_3=-(2\pi)^2\alpha'(h_a\alpha^a+h_{a+h_{2,1}+1}\beta_a)$ (eqs. 57-58, p. 31), symplectic basis $\int_{A^a}\alpha_b=\delta^a_b$, $\int_{B_b}\beta^a=-\delta^a_b$, $\int_M\alpha_a\wedge\beta^b=\delta_a^b$ (eq. 53).
- N=1 supergravity potential (eq. 21, p. 17): $V=e^{K/M_{Pl,4}^2}\Big(\sum_i|F_i|^2-3\frac{|W|^2}{M_{Pl,4}^2}\Big)+\frac12\sum_\alpha D_\alpha^2$, $F_i=DW/D\phi^i\equiv\partial W/\partial\phi^i+\frac{1}{M_{Pl,4}^2}(\partial K/\partial\phi^i)W$. Gravitino mass $m_{3/2}=e^{K/2M_{Pl,4}^2}|W|/M_{Pl,4}^2$.
- Kahler potentials: complex structure plus axio-dilaton $\mathcal K=-\log\big(i\int_M\Omega\wedge\bar\Omega\big)-\log(-i(\phi-\bar\phi))$ (eq. 55, p. 31); Kahler moduli $\mathcal K_k=-2\log(V)$ (eq. 60) with $V=\frac16 S_{\alpha\beta\gamma}t^\alpha t^\beta t^\gamma$ (eq. 61); single-modulus KKLT $\mathcal K_k=-3\log(-i(\rho-\bar\rho))$ (eq. 77, p. 34).
- Flux superpotential (Gukov-Vafa-Witten): $W=\int_M G_3\wedge\Omega(z)=(2\pi)^2\alpha'(f-\phi h)\cdot\Pi(z)$ (eq. 59, p. 31).

# Equations the tutorial uses

## (a) Moduli problem

- Introduction, p. 2: $g_{55}$ "parameterizes the radius $R$ of the extra-dimensional circle. Since the classical Einstein equations are scale invariant ... there is no preferred value for this radius $R$"; it "describe[s] a (non-minimally coupled) massless scalar field", predicting "new long range forces, or time dependence of parameters, in direct conflict with observation." Same for the family of Ricci-flat metrics $\lambda g_{ij}$ (II.B, p. 9).
- General KK mode expansion (eq. 6, p. 10): $\phi(x,y)=\sum_k\phi_k(x)f_k(y)$ with $0=(\eta^{\mu\nu}\partial_\mu\partial_\nu+\Delta_M)\phi$; moduli directions $\delta G_\alpha\equiv\partial G(\vec t)/\partial t^\alpha$ (unnumbered, p. 10) are zero modes of the linearised Einstein equation, hence 4d massless fields. The authors stress the difference from Goldstone modes: moduli "can exist without a symmetry" (p. 10).
- Bounds (Intro p. 4): fifth-force experiments constrain moduli masses below about $10^{-3}$ eV; cosmology (Polonyi/BBN) constrains masses up to 10 TeV. Also II.F.2, p. 18: $m_{moduli}\sim 1$ TeV estimate from eq. (24) $m_i\sim cF_X/M_P$ with gravity mediation $F\sim(10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV})^2$; moduli with $m<100$ TeV cause the Polonyi problem via $\ddot\phi_i+3H\dot\phi_i=-\partial V/\partial\phi_i$ (eq. 26, p. 18). Moduli controlling gauge couplings must satisfy $m\gtrsim 100$ GeV (Banks et al. 2002) or the fine-structure constant would vary.
- Flux-induced complex-structure mass (eq. 27, p. 18): $M_F\sim\alpha'/R^3$, with $M_F\ll M_{KK}$ at large radius.

## (b) Simplest toy model: Freund-Rubin on $S^2$ (II.D.1, pp. 13-14)

- 6d action (eq. 14): $S=\int d^6x\sqrt{-G_6}\,(\mathcal R_6-|F_2|^2)$, in units of $M_6$.
- Ansatz (eq. 15): $ds^2=\eta_{\mu\nu}dx^\mu dx^\nu+R^2 g_{mn}(y)dy^mdy^n$, $g$ the unit $S^2$ metric; flux (eq. 16): $\int_{S^2}F_2=N$.
- General flux energy (eq. 13, p. 13): $V=\int_M d^Dy\sqrt G\,G^{ij}G^{kl}(F_2)_{ik}(F_2)_{jl}=\int_M F_2\wedge(*F_2)$, applicable to any $F_{p+2}$ with $2\to p+2$.
- Scaling argument (p. 14, prose): after Weyl rescaling to 4d Einstein frame the $S^2$ Euler-characteristic curvature term scales as $-R^{-4}$; flux quantisation gives $F_2\sim N/R^2$, integral over $S^2$ contributes $R^2$, so the flux term scales as $N^2/R^6$. Total (eq. 17):
  $$V(R)\sim\frac{N^2}{R^6}-\frac{1}{R^4}.$$
  "It is not hard to see that this function has minima where $R\sim N$." (Explicit: $V'=0\Rightarrow -6N^2/R^7+4/R^5=0\Rightarrow R^2=\tfrac32N^2$; the paper gives only $R\sim N$.) Large $N$ gives large radius and small curvature, "justifying the use of supergravity." Caveat (p. 14): the minimum has negative vacuum energy with 4d curvature comparable to that of $S^2$, so Freund-Rubin vacua are not compactifications with a valid 4d EFT.
- IIA analogue (eq. 109, p. 39, with $g_s=e^\phi$): $V=\frac{e^{4\phi}}{R^6}-\frac{e^{3\phi}}{R^9}+\frac{e^{2\phi}}{R^{12}}+N^2\frac{e^{4\phi}}{R^{14}}$, minima at $R\sim N^{1/4}$, $g_s\sim N^{-3/4}$; building blocks eqs. 105-107: $V_{RR}=N^2e^{4\phi}/R^{6+2p}$, $V_{NS}=N^2e^{2\phi}/R^{12}$, $V_{O(p+3)}=-Ne^{3\phi}/R^{12-p}$.

## (c) Flux quantisation

- Definition of flux (eq. 11, p. 12): $\int_\Sigma F_{p+2}=n\neq0$ over a nontrivial $(p+2)$-cycle; for a basis $\sigma_i$ of $H_{p+2}(M,\mathbb R)$, $\int_{\sigma_i}F_{p+2}=n_i$ (eq. 12), $1\le i\le b_{p+2}$. Illustration: Maxwell theory on $S^2$, $B_{\theta\phi}=g\sin\theta\,d\theta\,d\phi$, quantised by Dirac's argument $e\cdot g=2\pi$ ($\hbar=1$) (p. 12). Integrality for $p>0$ and torsion is made precise via K-theory (Moore 2003).
- Bousso-Polchinski toy (eqs. 18-20, p. 15): $\int_{\Sigma^i}F=n^i$, $i=1,\dots,N$; cutoff $\sum_i n_i^2\le L$; $V\sim-V_0+c_in_i^2$. Counting: number of lattice points in a sphere of radius $\sqrt L\sim10$ in 100 dimensions, $\approx\frac{\pi^{50}}{50!}\times10^{100}\sim10^{60}$; vacua with $|\Lambda|<\epsilon$ lie in a shell between radii $\sqrt{V_0}$ and $\sqrt{V_0+\epsilon}$, giving typical spacing $1/N_{vac}$.
- IIB tadpole (eqs. 46-47, p. 30; 67-69, p. 32): $d\tilde F_5=H_3\wedge F_3+2\kappa_{10}^2T_3\rho_3^{loc}$; $\frac{1}{2\kappa_{10}^2T_3}\int_M H_3\wedge F_3+Q_3^{loc}=0$; $N_{flux}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^4(\alpha')^2}\int_M F_3\wedge H_3=f\cdot\Sigma\cdot h$, $N_{flux}+N_{D3}=L$, and in F-theory $L=\chi(Y)/24$.

## (d) IIB superpotential, F-flatness, ISD

- $W=\int_M G_3\wedge\Omega(z)=(2\pi)^2\alpha'(f-\phi h)\cdot\Pi(z)$ (eq. 59). Periods $z^a=\int_{A^a}\Omega$, $\mathcal G_b=\int_{B_b}\Omega$, $\Pi=(\mathcal G_b,z^a)$ (eq. 54); $\int\Omega\wedge\bar\Omega=-\Pi^\dagger\Sigma\Pi$ (eq. 56).
- No-scale potential (eqs. 62-63, p. 31): $V=e^{\mathcal K+\mathcal K_k}\Big(\sum_{i,j}g^{i\bar j}D_iW\overline{D_jW}-3|W|^2\Big)\to V=e^{\mathcal K_{tot}}\Big(\sum_{a,b}g^{a\bar b}D_aW\overline{D_bW}\Big)$, positive semi-definite, vacua at $V=0$; SUSY vacua $D_\phi W=D_aW=0$, non-SUSY have some $D_aW\neq0$ with $V=0$ at tree level.
- F-flatness (eqs. 64-65, p. 32): $D_\phi W=D_aW=0\iff(f-\bar\phi h)\cdot\Pi(z)=(f-\phi h)\cdot(\partial_a\Pi+\Pi\partial_a\mathcal K)=0$, i.e. the $(3,0)$ and $(1,2)$ parts of $G_3$ vanish, so $G_3$ is imaginary self-dual, $*_6G_3=iG_3$ (eq. 51, p. 30), with $e^{4A}=\alpha$ (eq. 52). $h_{2,1}+1$ equations in $h_{2,1}+1$ unknowns: generic flux fixes all complex-structure moduli and the axio-dilaton (p. 32).
- 10d derivation (eqs. 44-50, pp. 29-30): $\tilde\nabla^2(e^{4A}-\alpha)=\frac{e^{2A}}{24\,\mathrm{Im}(\tau)}|iG_3-*_6G_3|^2+e^{-6A}|\partial(e^{4A}-\alpha)|^2+2\kappa_{10}^2e^{2A}[\frac14(T^m_m-T^\mu_\mu)^{loc}-T_3\rho_3^{loc}]$ (eq. 49); with BPS-like assumption $\frac14(T^m_m-T^\mu_\mu)^{loc}\ge T_3\rho_3^{loc}$ (eq. 50) the compact integral forces ISD and $e^{4A}=\alpha$. No-go without sources: eq. (44) needs $(T^m_m-T^\mu_\mu)_{loc}<0$ (eq. 45).
- Conifold example (eqs. 71-75, pp. 32-33): periods $\int_A\Omega=z$, $\int_B\Omega=\frac{z}{2\pi i}\log z+\text{regular}$; fluxes $\int_AF_3\sim M$, $\int_BH_3\sim-K$ give $W(z)=-K\phi z+M\mathcal G(z)$ (eq. 73), $D_zW=\frac{M}{2\pi i}\log z-i\frac{K}{g_s}+\dots$ (eq. 74), $z\sim\exp(-2\pi K/g_sM)$ (eq. 75); warp factor $e^A\sim e^{-2\pi K/3g_sM}$ (eq. 83, p. 35).

## (e) KKLT (IV.A.3, p. 34)

- $\mathcal K_k=-3\log(-i(\rho-\bar\rho))$ (eq. 77), $\mathrm{Im}(\rho)\sim R^4/(\alpha')^2$, $\mathrm{Re}(\rho)$ a $C_4$ axion.
- $W=W_0+Ae^{ia\rho}$ (eq. 78): $W_0$ is the flux superpotential (eq. 59) evaluated at its minimum in complex-structure moduli space; $A$ a determinant, $a$ set by rank of the D7 gauge group (or D3-instanton, eq. 76: number of zero modes $=2\chi(D)=2\sum_{p=0}^3h^{0,p}(D)$).
- Solving $D_\rho W=0$ gives "a vacuum with all moduli stabilized and unbroken supersymmetry"; for small $W_0$, $\mathrm{Im}(\rho)$ large "with logarithmic speed". The paper does not write the explicit AdS vacuum equation or the anti-D3 uplift formula; the uplift is described in words in IV.A.4.a-b (pp. 35-37) using anti-D3 branes in the Klebanov-Strassler throat, with $V_{eff}\simeq e^{-8\pi K/3Mg_s}\times p\frac{\mu_3}{g_s}\big(1-\frac{8\pi^2}{3}\frac{(p^2-1)}{M}\frac{1}{b_0^{12}}\big)$ (eq. 100, p. 37).
- Required tuning (eq. 79, p. 34): $|W|^2\le M_P^2(10^{11}\,\mathrm{GeV})^4\to(W/M_P^3)\le10^{-14}$. Hierarchy of scales: complex-structure moduli get $M_F\sim\alpha'/R^3$, Kahler moduli masses "significantly smaller" (p. 34).

## (f) Counting of vacua

- Rigid CY (V.B.1, pp. 45-46): $W=(f_1+\Pi f_2)\tau+g_1+\Pi g_2\equiv F\tau+G$, $K=-\log\mathrm{Im}\,\tau$, $DW=0\leftrightarrow\bar\tau=-G/F$ (eq. 154), tadpole $\mathrm{Im}F^*G\le L$ (eq. 155), $SL(2,\mathbb Z)$ identification (eq. 156), total $N_{vac}(L)=2\sigma(L)=2\sum_{k|L}k$ (eq. 157), $\sum_{L\le N}\sigma(L)=\frac{\pi^2}{12}N^2+\mathcal O(N\log N)$; continuum limit $\lim_{L\to\infty}dN_{vac}=\pi L\frac{d^2\tau}{(\mathrm{Im}\tau)^2}$ (eq. 161), valid for $L>K/r^2$ (eq. 162).
- Ashok-Douglas formula (eq. 163, p. 47): $\sum_{L\le L_{max}}dI_{vac}(L)=\frac{(2\pi L_{max})^{b_3}}{\pi^{b_3/2}b_3!}\det(-R-\omega)$, index density on the $b_3/2$-dimensional moduli space; mirror quintic near conifold $dN_{vac}\sim d^2\psi/(|\psi-1|^2(\log|\psi-1|)^3)$ (eq. 164), $N_{vac}=\pi^4L_*^4/(18\ln\frac1{R^2})$ (eq. 165). Index density definition $dN_{vac}(T)=\sum_i\delta(T-T_i)(-1)^F_i$ (eq. 166), Kac-Rice form $dI_{vac}(z)=\langle\delta(V'(z))\det V''(z)\rangle$ (eq. 168), integral formula eq. 169, Denef-Douglas change of variables eq. 172.
- Absolute numbers (V.D.4, pp. 54-55): "for the three examples which were shown to do so in Denef et al. (2004), one finds $10^{307}$, $10^{393}$ and $10^{506}$. ... Thus, one can take $10^{500}$ as a reasonable estimate at present"; F-theory fourfolds with $K\sim1000$ "might drastically increase the numbers, to say $10^{1000}$." Intro (p. 5): "perhaps greater than $10^{500}$". Vacuum counting distribution $dN_{vac}(T)=\sum_i\delta(T-T_i)$ (eqs. 149-151, p. 44), explicitly not a probability measure (eq. 152).
- SUSY-breaking distribution $dN_{vac}[M_{susy}]\sim(M_{susy}/M_F)^{12}$ (eq. 175, p. 51) vs field-theory naturalness (eq. 174); discrete symmetries cost $L^{K/2}/L^K\sim10^{-200}$ (p. 52).

# Attributable statements

1. "the five dimensional metric $g_{\mu\nu}$, regarded as a field in four dimensions, contains additional, non-metric degrees of freedom" and $g_{55}$ "describe[s] a (non-minimally coupled) massless scalar field" whose long-range forces are "in direct conflict with observation" (I, p. 2).
2. Ricci-flat metrics "are never unique, but always come in families with at least one parameter"; moduli differ from Goldstone modes because they "can exist without a symmetry" (II.B, pp. 9-10).
3. Fifth-force bounds matter for moduli masses below about $10^{-3}$ eV; cosmology constrains masses up to 10 TeV via BBN; "Both bounds admit loopholes" (I, p. 4).
4. Fluxes are the "generalization of the Maxwell action" with energy $\propto F^2$; because they thread cycles, the cost "will depend on the precise choice of metric on $M$. In other words, it will generate a potential on the moduli space $\mathcal M$" (II.D, p. 13).
5. Freund-Rubin: $V(R)\sim N^2/R^6-1/R^4$ "has minima where $R\sim N$. So with moderately large flux, one can achieve radii which are large in fundamental units" (II.D.1, eq. 17, p. 14); but these vacua "are not compactifications which yield lower-dimensional EFT's" since the 4d curvature is comparable to that of $S^2$.
6. In the no-go theorem without localized sources, "the only solutions have $G_3=0$ and $e^A=$ constant, and IIb supergravity does not allow nontrivial warped compactifications" (IV.A.1, p. 30).
7. "generic fluxes will fix all of the complex structure moduli as well as the axio-dilaton" via $h_{2,1}+1$ equations in $h_{2,1}+1$ variables; the tree-level flux superpotential "does not depend explicitly on the Kahler moduli" (IV.A.2, pp. 31-32).
8. In the no-scale potential "the potential is positive semi-definite with vacua precisely when $V=0$!"; this "miracle of vanishing cosmological constant ... depended on the tree-level structure of the Kahler potential (60) which is not radiatively stable" (IV.A.2, pp. 31-32).
9. KKLT: "It is then straightforward to show that one can solve the equation $D_\rho W=0$, yielding a vacuum with all moduli stabilized and unbroken supersymmetry" requiring an "exponentially small value of $W_0$"; "This provides a loose proof-of-principle that one can find models with all moduli stabilized" (IV.A.3, p. 34).
10. "one can take $10^{500}$ as a reasonable estimate at present, unless and until we can argue that further conditions of the sort discussed in Sec. III are required" (V.D.4, p. 55); and "TeV scale supersymmetry is not an inevitable prediction of string/M theory in this framework" (VI, p. 60).

# Caveats

- The KK Planck-mass relation on p. 6 is unnumbered; only $M_{P,4}^2=M_{P,D}^{D-2}/M_{KK}^{D-4}$ carries number (1). The flux-energy formula (13) is the only explicit potential; (17) is a scaling result, not a derived coefficient formula, and the minimisation $R\sim N$ is stated, not computed.
- The Freund-Rubin toy uses 6d Einstein-Maxwell, "not directly realized in string theory" (p. 12); its minimum is AdS with curvature radius of order the $S^2$ radius, so it does not give a 4d EFT (p. 14, and III.F p. 28). Attributing it as a "stabilised compactification" needs that qualifier.
- Symbol overloading: $\phi$ denotes the IIB axio-dilaton (eqs. 38, 55, 59) but also a generic scalar and, in IIA (p. 38), the dilaton with $g_s=e^\phi$; Sec. V switches to $\tau$. $R$ is both radius and curvature two-form (eq. 163).
- Flux normalisation (eqs. 57-58) carries a factor $-(2\pi)^2\alpha'$ and $N_{flux}$ divides by $(2\pi)^4(\alpha')^2$; the simpler $\int_AF_3\sim M$, $\int_BH_3\sim-K$ of the conifold example (eq. 72, 82) suppresses these factors.
- The ISD derivation (eqs. 44-52) is tree-level; footnote 10 (p. 37) notes the no-go for anti-D3 sources is "true only at tree level", and the same effects stabilising Kahler moduli allow anti-branes with small warped tension.
- The paper does not write the KKLT AdS vacuum condition ($D_\rho W=0$ solved explicitly) nor the uplift term $D/\sigma^3$ (or $D/\sigma^2$); only the microscopic anti-D3 potential (eqs. 97-101) is given.
- Vacuum counts are "index" densities (signed, eq. 166); the ratio of index to actual number is estimated as $1/c^{b_3}$ (p. 48). The $10^{500}$ figure is explicitly heuristic ("make these estimates rather heuristic at present", p. 54), ignores Kahler-moduli stabilisation requirements, and could be undercounted by F-theory ($10^{1000}$) or reduced by large CY symmetry groups.
- The Bousso-Polchinski count $10^{60}$ (p. 15) uses $N=100$, $L=100$ and is a toy; the authors stress the continuous-flux approximation is good only for $L\gg K$ (eq. 162 and p. 49).
- Fifth-force and cosmological bounds (p. 4) are quoted without numbers beyond $10^{-3}$ eV and 10 TeV; the $m_{moduli}\sim1$ TeV estimate (p. 18) is unnumbered.
