# Conventions

- Signature: mostly minus. $\eta_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(+1,-1,\dots,-1)$ (p. 3; eq. (A.4) p. 36). In Sect. 3 the general case $\mathrm{SO}(t,s)$ with $\eta_{ab}=\mathrm{diag}(\underbrace{+1,\dots,+1}_{t},\underbrace{-1,\dots,-1}_{s})$, $d=t+s$, eq. (3.1); Minkowski is $t=1$, $s=d-1$.
- Clifford algebra: $\{\gamma^a,\gamma^b\}=2\eta^{ab}$, eq. (3.2) p. 7 (plus sign, so $(\gamma^0)^2=+1$). $\gamma^a$ hermitian for $a=1,\dots,t$, antihermitian for the spacelike ones. Minimal size $2^{[d/2]}\times 2^{[d/2]}$; explicit tensor-product representation eq. (3.3); odd-$d$ last matrix $\gamma^{2n+1}=(-1)^{\frac14(s-t)}\,i\,\gamma^1\cdots\gamma^{2n}$, eq. (3.4).
- Antisymmetrised products have unit strength: $\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}=\frac{1}{3!}(\gamma^\mu\gamma^\nu\gamma^\rho\pm\text{perms})$, eq. (2.3).
- Chirality matrix: $\bar\gamma=(-1)^{\frac14(s-t)}\gamma^1\gamma^2\cdots\gamma^d$, $\bar\gamma^2=1$, $\{\bar\gamma,\gamma^a\}=0$, eqs. (3.6)-(3.7); Weyl: $\bar\gamma\psi=\pm\psi$, eq. (3.8). In App. B (d=4): $\gamma_5=i\gamma^0\gamma^1\gamma^2\gamma^3$, $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$ (p. 39).
- Dirac conjugate $\bar\psi=\psi^\dagger A$, $A=\gamma^1\cdots\gamma^t$, eq. (3.15). Charge conjugation $\psi^c=B_\pm^{-1}\psi^*$ with $(\gamma^a)^*=B_+\gamma^aB_+^{-1}$, $-(\gamma^a)^*=B_-\gamma^aB_-^{-1}$, eqs. (3.10)-(3.11); equivalently $\psi^c=C_\pm\bar\psi^T$, $C_\pm=B_\pm^{-1}A^{-1T}$, eq. (3.16), with $\gamma^{aT}=\pm(-1)^{t+1}C_\pm^{-1}\gamma^aC_\pm$, $C_\pm^\dagger C_\pm=\mathbf 1$, $C_\pm^T=(\pm1)^t(-1)^{\frac12 t(t-1)}\epsilon_\pm C_\pm$, eq. (3.17). $B_\pm^*B_\pm=\epsilon_\pm\mathbf 1$, $\epsilon_\pm(t,s)=\sqrt2\cos[\frac{\pi}{4}(s-t\pm1)]$, eq. (3.12).
- Majorana condition $\psi^c=\psi$, eq. (3.9); possible iff $\epsilon_+=1$ (pseudo-Majorana, uses $B_+$) or $\epsilon_-=1$ (Majorana, uses $B_-$), p. 10. In d=4 the matrix $C$ of Sect. 2 is $C_-$ with $C^{-1}\gamma^aC=-\gamma^{aT}$, $C^T=-C$, eq. (2.1), and $\psi^c\equiv C\bar\psi^T$ (p. 3). Majorana-Weyl only for $s-t=0$ mod 4, i.e. $d=2$ mod 4 in Minkowski (p. 11). Symplectic (pseudo-)Majorana $\psi^i=\Omega^{ij}(\psi^j)^c$, eq. (3.19).
- Vielbein: $g_{\mu\nu}=e_\mu{}^a e_\nu{}^b\eta_{ab}$ (A.6); $e=\det e_\mu{}^a=\sqrt{-g}$; $\gamma^\mu=\gamma^a e_a{}^\mu$ (p. 38). Local Lorentz $\delta_L e_\mu{}^a=-\lambda^a{}_b e_\mu{}^b$, $\delta_L\psi=-\frac14\lambda^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\psi$, eqs. (2.9), (3.5), (A.10).
- Spin connection: $D_\mu\psi=(\partial_\mu+\frac14\omega_\mu{}^{ab}\gamma_{ab})\psi$ (A.12); torsionless condition $D_\mu e_\nu{}^a-D_\nu e_\mu{}^a=0$ with $D_\mu e_\nu{}^a\equiv\partial_\mu e_\nu{}^a+\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b e_\nu{}^b$ (A.14); solution $\omega_{\mu ab}(e)=\frac12(e_a{}^\nu\Omega_{\mu\nu b}-e_b{}^\nu\Omega_{\mu\nu a}-e_a{}^\rho e_b{}^\sigma e_\mu{}^c\Omega_{\rho\sigma c})$, $\Omega_{\mu\nu a}=\partial_\mu e_{\nu a}-\partial_\nu e_{\mu a}$ (A.15). Curvature $R_{\mu\nu}{}^a{}_b=\partial_\mu\omega_\nu{}^a{}_b-\partial_\nu\omega_\mu{}^a{}_b+\omega_\mu{}^a{}_c\omega_\nu{}^c{}_b-\omega_\nu{}^a{}_c\omega_\mu{}^c{}_b$ (A.17); $R=e_a{}^\mu e_b{}^\nu R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}$ (A.18). Christoffel/Riemann in (A.2)-(A.3).
- Einstein-Hilbert normalisation: $\mathcal L=-\frac{1}{16\pi G}\sqrt{-g}R$ (A.1), then "$4\pi G=1$" so that $\mathcal L_E=-\frac14 eR$ (p. 36). Gravitino kinetic term: $-\frac12 i e\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_\nu\psi_\rho$ (eqs. (2.2), (5.1)). Spin-1/2 kinetic term $\mathcal L=ie\bar\psi\gamma^\mu D_\mu\psi$ (A.13). The sign pattern (negative EH, factor $i$ in fermion terms) is tied to the mostly-minus signature.

## (b) Eleven-dimensional supergravity (Sect. 5, p. 16; ref. [12])

- Fields: vielbein $e_\mu{}^a$, Majorana Rarita-Schwinger $\psi_\mu$, real 3-form $B_{\mu\nu\rho}$ (Tanii writes $B$, not $C$ or $A$). Lagrangian, eq. (5.1):
$$\mathcal L=-\frac14 eR-\frac12 ie\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_\nu\psi_\rho-\frac1{48}eF_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}F^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}+\frac1{96}e\left(\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\psi_\nu+12\,\bar\psi^\alpha\gamma^{\beta\gamma}\psi^\delta\right)F_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}+\frac{2}{144^2}\epsilon^{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_4\beta_1\cdots\beta_4\mu\nu\rho}F_{\alpha_1\cdots\alpha_4}F_{\beta_1\cdots\beta_4}B_{\mu\nu\rho}+(\text{4-fermi terms}),$$
with $F_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=4\partial_{[\mu}B_{\nu\rho\sigma]}$ (unit-strength antisymmetrisation). Local supersymmetry, eq. (5.2):
$$\delta_Qe_\mu{}^a=-i\bar\epsilon\gamma^a\psi_\mu,\qquad \delta_QB_{\mu\nu\rho}=\frac32\bar\epsilon\gamma_{[\mu\nu}\psi_{\rho]},\qquad \delta_Q\psi_\mu=D_\mu\epsilon+\frac{i}{144}\left(\gamma^{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}{}_\mu-8\gamma^{\beta\gamma\delta}\delta^\alpha_\mu\right)\epsilon F_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}+(\text{3-fermi terms}).$$
- Gauge symmetry of the 3-form, eq. (5.3): $\delta_ge_\mu{}^a=0$, $\delta_g\psi_\mu=0$, $\delta_gB_{\mu\nu\rho}=3\partial_{[\mu}\Lambda_{\nu\rho]}$, $\Lambda_{\mu\nu}=-\Lambda_{\nu\mu}$.
- The Chern-Simons coefficient $2/144^2$ is as printed; the paper gives no $\epsilon$-tensor normalisation or $\kappa$ (the $4\pi G=1$ choice of App. A is implicit).

## (c) d=4, N=1 supergravity (Sect. 2 and App. B)

- Lagrangian, eq. (2.2): $\mathcal L=-\frac14 e\hat R-\frac12 ie\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\hat D_\nu\psi_\rho$.
- Definitions (2.4): $\hat R=e_a{}^\mu e_b{}^\nu\hat R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}$, $\hat R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}=\partial_\mu\hat\omega_\nu{}^{ab}-\partial_\nu\hat\omega_\mu{}^{ab}+\hat\omega_\mu{}^a{}_c\hat\omega_\nu{}^{cb}-\hat\omega_\nu{}^a{}_c\hat\omega_\mu{}^{cb}$, $\hat D_\nu\psi_\rho=(\partial_\nu+\frac14\hat\omega_\nu{}^{ab}\gamma_{ab})\psi_\rho$.
- Torsionful (1.5-order) spin connection, eq. (2.5): $\hat\omega_{\mu ab}=\omega_{\mu ab}-\frac12 i\bar\psi_a\gamma_\mu\psi_b-\frac12 i\bar\psi_\mu\gamma_a\psi_b+\frac12 i\bar\psi_\mu\gamma_b\psi_a$; torsion $\hat D_\mu e_\nu{}^a-\hat D_\nu e_\mu{}^a=-i\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^a\psi_\nu$ (2.6).
- Equivalent second-order form (2.7): $\mathcal L=-\frac14 eR-\frac12 ie\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_\nu\psi_\rho+(\text{4-fermi terms})$.
- Alternative form (B.1): $\mathcal L_E=\frac1{16}\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\epsilon_{abcd}e_\rho{}^ce_\sigma{}^d\hat R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}$, $\mathcal L_{RS}=\frac12\epsilon^{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\bar\psi_\mu\gamma_\nu\gamma_5\hat D_\rho\psi_\sigma$.
- Local symmetries, eqs. (2.8)-(2.10): $\delta_G(\xi)e_\mu{}^a=-\xi^\nu\partial_\nu e_\mu{}^a-\partial_\mu\xi^\nu e_\nu{}^a$, $\delta_G\psi_\mu=-\xi^\nu\partial_\nu\psi_\mu-\partial_\mu\xi^\nu\psi_\nu$; $\delta_Le_\mu{}^a=-\lambda^a{}_be_\mu{}^b$, $\delta_L\psi_\mu=-\frac14\lambda^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\psi_\mu$;
$$\delta_Q(\epsilon)e_\mu{}^a=-i\bar\epsilon\gamma^a\psi_\mu,\qquad \delta_Q(\epsilon)\psi_\mu=\hat D_\mu\epsilon\equiv\left(\partial_\mu+\frac14\hat\omega_\mu{}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\right)\epsilon \quad (2.10),\ \epsilon^c=\epsilon.$$
- Algebra, eq. (2.11): $[\delta_Q(\epsilon_1),\delta_Q(\epsilon_2)]=\delta_G(\xi)+\delta_L(\xi\cdot\hat\omega)+\delta_Q(\xi\cdot\psi)$, $\xi^\mu=i\bar\epsilon_2\gamma^\mu\epsilon_1$; closes only on-shell (B.13, p. 41), off-shell closure needs auxiliary fields (p. 6; not known for extended/higher-d theories).
- Proof of invariance (App. B): $\delta_Q\mathcal L_E=-\frac12 ie\bar\epsilon\gamma^\mu\psi_a(e_b{}^\nu\hat R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}-\frac12 e_\mu{}^a\hat R)$ (B.5) cancels $\delta_Q\mathcal L_{RS}$ (B.8) using $[\hat D_\mu,\hat D_\nu]\epsilon=\frac14\hat R_{\mu\nu}{}^{ab}\gamma_{ab}\epsilon$ (B.9), the Fierz identity (B.3), Majorana flip properties (B.4), and the 1.5-order property $\delta\int\mathcal L/\delta\hat\omega=0$ (B.2).
- Why local supersymmetry forces gravity (p. 2, eq. (1.1)): gauging rigid SUSY introduces $\psi_\mu^\alpha$ with $\delta_Q\psi^\alpha_\mu=\partial_\mu\epsilon^\alpha+\cdots$; since $\{Q^\alpha,\bar Q_\beta\}=(\gamma^a)^\alpha{}_\beta P_a$, "gauging of supersymmetry leads to gauging of translation. Since the local translation is the general coordinate transformation, we also need the gravitational field $g_{\mu\nu}(x)$ as a gauge field."
- Also p. 3: the N=1 massless multiplet of helicities $(2,\frac32)$ shows a theory with only $(g_{\mu\nu},\psi_\mu)$ is possible (ref. [6]).

## (d) Dimensional reduction

- No explicit reduction formulas, no reduced action, and no reduction of the gravitino are given.
- The only statements (p. 17): "The (1,1) supergravity [13] can be obtained from the $d=11$ theory by a dimensional reduction and is vector-like (left-right symmetric). This theory is a massless sector of the type IIA superstring theory."
- And: "Supergravities in $d<10$ dimensions, whose field contents are given in Table 3, can be obtained from $d=11$ or $d=10$ supergravity by dimensional reductions and truncations of fields."
- Field content of IIA is read from Table 3 (row d=10, (1,1), n=128, same as d=11).
- The statement that toroidal reduction preserves all supersymmetry is NOT written explicitly; it is only implicit in the equal $n=128$ counts for the maximal theories in each $d$ of Table 3 (d=11 N=1, d=10 (1,1), d=9 N=2, ..., d=4 N=8 all have 128+128) and in the phrase "dimensional reductions and truncations of fields" (truncation being what reduces N).
- The general structure after reduction (p. 17): vielbein, gravitini, antisymmetric tensors, spin-1/2 fields, scalars; scalars form a G/H sigma model with G non-compact and H its maximal compact subgroup (Sect. 6); in even $d=2n$, G acts on $(n-1)$-forms by duality, a symmetry of the equations of motion but not of the action (Sect. 7).

## (e) Supersymmetry preserved by other compactifications / Killing spinors

- Not present. Neither "Killing spinor" nor Freund-Rubin nor sphere compactifications appear.
- Nearest item (i): gauged supergravities (p. 6) with O(N) gauge fields, "a cosmological term $e$ proportional to $g^2$ and a gravitino mass term $\bar\psi_\mu\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_\nu$ proportional to $g$", reducing to ordinary supergravity as $g\to0$.
- Nearest item (ii): the brane scan (Sect. 8): $\kappa$-symmetry requires $d-p-1=\frac14 n$ for $p\ge2$ (8.8), $d-2=\frac14 n$ or $\frac12 n$ for $p=1$ (8.9), satisfied for 12 pairs $(d,p)$ (Fig. 1 p. 35); for $(d,p)=(11,2),(10,1),(10,5)$ the $\kappa$-invariance constraints on background superfields are equivalent to the field equations of $d=11$, $N=1$ and $d=10$ (1,1) supergravity (p. 35).

# Attributable statements

1. (Sect. 1, p. 2) Supergravities are field theories with local supersymmetry; since $\{Q,\bar Q\}\propto\gamma^aP_a$ (1.1), gauging supersymmetry gauges translations, hence requires $g_{\mu\nu}$ and the Rarita-Schwinger field $\psi_\mu^\alpha$.
2. (Sect. 2, p. 3) The d=4, N=1 multiplet is the helicity pair $(2,\frac32)$; the pure theory has only $(e_\mu{}^a,\psi_\mu)$ with $\psi_\mu$ Majorana, Lagrangian (2.2).
3. (Sect. 2, p. 5 and App. B, p. 41) The local supersymmetry algebra (2.11) closes only on-shell; the $[\delta_Q,\delta_Q]$ commutator yields a general coordinate transformation with $\xi^\mu=i\bar\epsilon_2\gamma^\mu\epsilon_1$ plus field-dependent Lorentz and supersymmetry transformations.
4. (Sect. 3, p. 11, Table 2) Minkowski spinor types are periodic in $d$ mod 8; Majorana exists in d=2,3,4,10,11; Majorana-Weyl only in $d=2$ mod 4 (d=2,10); d=5,6,7 need Dirac or symplectic Majorana spinors.
5. (Sect. 4, p. 14) Requiring helicities $\le2$ allows only finitely many $(d,N)$, and $d\le11$; supergravity multiplets are the massless representations (4.7).
6. (Sect. 4, Table 3, p. 15) The maximal supergravity multiplet has 128+128 physical degrees of freedom in every $d\le11$; d=11 N=1 contains exactly $e_\mu{}^a,\psi_\mu,B_{\mu\nu\rho}$ (44+84 bosonic, 128 fermionic, via (4.8)).
7. (Sect. 5, p. 16) The highest-dimensional supergravity is d=11, N=1 (Cremmer-Julia-Scherk [12]) with Lagrangian (5.1), including the $FFB$ Chern-Simons term with coefficient $2/144^2$, and transformations (5.2).
8. (Sect. 5, p. 17) Three ten-dimensional supergravities exist, $(N_+,N_-)=(1,1),(2,0),(1,0)$; (1,1) follows from d=11 by dimensional reduction and is the massless sector of IIA; (2,0) is chiral, has a self-dual 4-form and no Lorentz-covariant action, and SU(1,1)/U(1) scalars; (1,0) couples to Yang-Mills matter (type I / heterotic).
9. (Sect. 5, p. 17; Sect. 6) All $d<10$ supergravities of Table 3 arise from d=11 or d=10 by dimensional reduction and truncation; their scalars form G/H non-linear sigma models (Table 4), e.g. $E_{7(+7)}$/SU(8) for d=4, N=8.
10. (Sect. 2, p. 6) Gauged (O(N)) supergravities have a cosmological term $\propto g^2$ and a gravitino mass term $\propto g$ and reduce to ungauged ones as $g\to0$.

# Caveats

- Signature is mostly minus, $(+,-,\dots,-)$; the EH term is $-\frac14 eR$ with $4\pi G=1$ (not $\frac1{2\kappa^2}$), and all fermion kinetic terms carry an explicit $i$. Translating to mostly-plus conventions (used by most compactification literature) flips signs of $R$, of $F^2$ terms and changes the $i$'s and gamma-matrix hermiticity; do not mix (5.1) with mostly-plus formulas without re-deriving.
- The 3-form is called $B_{\mu\nu\rho}$ (Tanii's generic symbol for all antisymmetric tensors) and $F=4\partial_{[\mu}B_{\nu\rho\sigma]}$ with unit-strength brackets; many papers write $A$ or $C$ with $F=dA$ (no factor 4 in form language).
- The d=11 Lagrangian (5.1) omits 4-fermi terms, and $\delta\psi_\mu$ (5.2) omits 3-fermi terms; the $D_\mu$ in (5.1)-(5.2) is not specified as torsionful or torsionless (contrast d=4 where the 1.5-order $\hat\omega$ is defined in (2.5)). The gravitino kinetic term is written with $D_\nu$ not $\hat D_\nu$.
- The sign/normalisation of $\epsilon^{\alpha_1\cdots\mu\nu\rho}$ in the Chern-Simons term is not stated; only $\epsilon^{0123}=+1$ is given, for d=4 in App. B.
- Nothing in the paper on Kaluza-Klein reduction mechanics, circle/torus ansaetze, Killing spinors, Freund-Rubin or sphere reductions, or counting preserved supersymmetry; "toroidal reduction preserves all supersymmetry" must be attributed elsewhere (only the 128-dof pattern in Table 3 is suggestive).
- The paper does not use the "number of real supercharges" language (32, 16, ...); it labels theories by $N$ (or $(N_+,N_-)$) and spinor type. Converting requires the component counts of Sect. 3 ($2^{[d/2]}$ complex components, halved by Weyl and by Majorana).
- The d=4 charge-conjugation matrix is $C_-$; in general $d$ the choice $B_\pm$/$C_\pm$ and the "pseudo-Majorana" distinction matter (p. 10); the Majorana flip signs (B.4) are specific to d=4 with this $C$.
- Table 3 counts spinor fields by (symplectic/pseudo) Majorana(-Weyl) spinors, not by Dirac spinors; Table 4 omits rows with no scalars (d=11, d=6 (4,0),(2,0), d=5 N=2, d=4 N<=3).
- Off-shell formulations with auxiliary fields exist for d=4 N=1 but "for general supergravities (extended and/or higher dimensions) such an off-shell formulation is not known" (p. 6).
- Quantum properties (UV divergences, anomalies) and matter couplings are explicitly excluded (pp. 2, 6, 14).
